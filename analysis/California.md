**Nature of the documents.** Both the 2023 guidance and the 2026 Model Policy are **explicitly voluntary**. Each carries the statutory disclaimer that "compliance with any information or guidance in this document is not mandatory. (See Educ. Code § 33308.5.)" (CDE-Learning-with-AI-2023.pdf, Introduction; CDE-AI-Model-Policy-2026.docx, opening). The Model Policy is a template LEAs and charters may "adopt and customize." Nothing in either is a statewide mandate. sources.md separately records AB 2148 (the "natural person" law) — its bill text is not in the provided files, so it is noted but not analyzed here.

## Executive Summary

California offers two layered, voluntary instruments. The **2023 guidance** frames AI education around "Learning with AI, Learning about AI," builds on the **5 Big Ideas of AI** (ai4k12.org), and treats students not only as AI consumers but as potential **AI creators** ("Developing AI in Schools"). Its recurring theme is that "AI or any other technology cannot replace the value of a student's relationship with a caring educator" (CDE-Learning-with-AI-2023.pdf, Introduction).

The **June 2026 Model Policy**, developed under SB 1288, is one of the most detailed model AI policies in the set — roughly two dozen principles spanning disclosure and citation, a caution against AI-detection software as sole evidence, per-assignment instructional discretion, privacy-by-design (barring student data from training public models), parent review rights, informed consent for autonomous AI tools, vendor contractual safeguards, and **AI literacy as a core academic competency** tied to Education Code §33548 (CDE-AI-Model-Policy-2026.docx). For a higher-education reader, California's documents read almost like a higher-education AI policy already: they emphasize human judgment over detection tools, per-assignment permissioning, and critical-evaluation skills. The caution is timing and voluntariness — the Model Policy posted June 25, 2026, so near-term incoming cohorts predate it, and both documents are non-binding, so exposure varies by LEA.

## K–12 AI Policy and Guidance

### Primary themes and policy objectives
- **Human relationships are central; AI supplements, never replaces.** "AI must be used to support human-centered learning environments, not replace them" (CDE-AI-Model-Policy-2026.docx, framing); "AI or any other technology cannot replace the value of a student's relationship with a caring educator" (CDE-Learning-with-AI-2023.pdf, Introduction). *[Recommendation]*
- **Learning with AI and learning about AI** as twin goals (CDE-Learning-with-AI-2023.pdf, throughout). *[Recommendation]*
- **Equity and the digital divide** are throughlines, tied to the state's "Closing the Digital Divide" and "Antibias Education" initiatives (CDE-Learning-with-AI-2023.pdf, "Pros and Cons," "Social Impacts"). *[Recommendation]*
- **AI literacy as a core academic competency** for all students and educators, per Education Code §33548 (CDE-AI-Model-Policy-2026.docx, "AI Literacy as a Core Academic Competency"). *[Recommendation, tied to statute]*

### Intended audiences and scope
- The 2023 guidance addresses educators, administrators, and educational leaders (CDE-Learning-with-AI-2023.pdf). *[Scope]*
- The Model Policy is written for a Governing Board to adopt at the LEA/charter level, covering students, educators, and staff (CDE-AI-Model-Policy-2026.docx). *[Scope]*

### Permitted, restricted, and prohibited AI uses (Model Policy)
- **Acceptable use:** AI "should be used to support learning, instruction, and operations in ways that enhance human decision-making and should not substitute for critical thinking or required academic work"; all AI "must be [District or Charter School] authorized" (CDE-AI-Model-Policy-2026.docx, "Acceptable Use"). *[Recommendation / model rule]*
- **Instructional discretion (per-assignment):** "Educators should have the discretion to determine when and how AI may be used in individual assignments... Permitted AI use may vary from one assignment to another" (Model Policy, "Instructional Discretion"). *[Recommendation / model rule]*
- **Plagiarism:** "submitting AI-generated content without clear disclosure constitutes plagiarism and academic dishonesty" (Model Policy, "Plagiarism, Disclosure, & Citation"). *[Recommendation / model rule]*
- **Safety prohibitions:** AI "should not be used to harm individuals, encourage self-harm, or cause harm to the school community" (Model Policy, "Safe & Responsible AI Use"). *[Recommendation / model rule]*
- **Data-entry restrictions:** students and educators "should not enter education records or other confidential information into AI tools unless expressly authorized"; personal AI accounts must not be used for student data or work (Model Policy, "Data Entry Restrictions," "Institutional AI Access"). *[Recommendation / model rule]*

### Privacy, security, equity, accessibility, ethics, and academic integrity
- **AI detection software** "should not be used as the sole basis for disciplinary action or grade penalty," with reliance instead on "educator evaluation of student engagement, alternative assessment, and documented evidence of authorship," and "clear criteria for rebutting claims" made available to students and parents (CDE-AI-Model-Policy-2026.docx, "AI Detection Software"). *[Recommendation]*
- **Privacy by design:** AI tools should "protect student data, prohibit its use for training public AI models or advertising, and maintain strong security safeguards"; compliance with FERPA and California Education Code §49073.1 (Model Policy, "Privacy by Design," "Legal Compliance"). *[Recommendation]*
- **Parent/guardian review rights:** guardians "should have the right to request timely human review of any AI-generated outputs that inform decisions about their child"; annual informed consent required before autonomous AI-powered student-interaction tools (Model Policy, "Parent/Guardian Review Rights," "Transparency & Informed Consent"). *[Recommendation]*
- **Vendor safeguards:** pre-deployment evaluation for educational value, bias, and equity; enforceable contract provisions (breach notification, audit rights, data deletion on termination) (Model Policy, "Pre-Deployment Evaluation," "Vendor Contractual Safeguards"). *[Recommendation]*
- **Accessibility:** the 2023 guidance ties AI to Universal Design for Learning and assistive uses (text-to-speech, translation, screen readers) (CDE-Learning-with-AI-2023.pdf, "Accessibility & personalization"). *[Recommendation]*
- **Ethics/bias:** algorithmic bias and social impacts are a dedicated theme (CDE-Learning-with-AI-2023.pdf, "Social Impacts"); ethical risk assessment including "environmental costs, effects on mental health, embedded bias" (Model Policy, "Ethical Risk Assessment"). *[Recommendation]*

### Teacher training, curriculum integration, assessment, and implementation
- **Grading:** students "should be informed when their work will be submitted to AI to support grading or feedback," and "the educator of record retains sole authority to determine final grades" (CDE-AI-Model-Policy-2026.docx, "Grading and Assessment"). *[Recommendation]*
- **Curriculum and assessment redesign:** emphasize "human thinking, process, equity, and insight... over easily AI-generated products," with "cross-disciplinary grade-band progressions across all grade levels (TK-12) incorporating the foundational concepts of AI: Perception, Representation and Reasoning, Learning, Natural Interaction, and Societal Impact" (Model Policy, "Curriculum & Assessment Redesign"). *[Recommendation]*
- **AI literacy skills** named concretely: "claim extraction, confidence calibration, source triangulation, and mismatch detection," and analysis of "misinformation, disinformation, and malinformation" (Model Policy, "AI Literacy as a Core Academic Competency"). *[Recommendation]*
- **Curriculum integration** in the 2023 guidance builds on the California Computer Science Standards and the 5 Big Ideas of AI (CDE-Learning-with-AI-2023.pdf, "Integrate 5 big ideas of AI"). *[Recommendation]*
- **PD:** the 2023 guidance ties to the State Superintendent's professional-learning initiative; the Model Policy requires "ongoing professional development and user training addressing AI literacy, data fluency, and learning science" (Model Policy, "Professional Development & User Training"). *[Recommendation]*

## Learning and Cognitive-Offloading Strategies

- **Critical thinking as the explicit design goal:** "Educators and students are urged to leverage AI to foster and not inhibit critical thinking" (CDE-Learning-with-AI-2023.pdf, "Demystify"). *[Recommendation]*
- **Substitution guardrail:** AI "should not substitute for critical thinking or required academic work" (CDE-AI-Model-Policy-2026.docx, "Acceptable Use"). *[Recommendation]*
- **Redesign against easy AI outputs:** curriculum and assessment redesign should emphasize "human thinking, process, equity, and insight... over easily AI-generated products" (Model Policy, "Curriculum & Assessment Redesign"). *[Recommendation]*
- **Modeling the efficiency-vs-learning distinction:** educators "should model the difference between using AI for efficiency and using AI to support learning, giving students opportunities to practice and demonstrate both" (Model Policy, "Modeling, Practice, & Ongoing Discussion"). *[Recommendation]*
- **Students as creators, not just consumers:** the 2023 "Developing AI in Schools" section frames students "becoming creators and innovators," which positions AI as an object of study rather than a shortcut (CDE-Learning-with-AI-2023.pdf, "Developing AI in Schools"). *[Recommendation]*
- A single named cognitive-offloading section (as in Arizona) is not present, but the substitution guardrail plus the redesign and modeling principles function as California's learning-preservation strategy. *[Recommendation]*

## Expected Graduate AI Profile

- **Uneven and time-dependent.** Both documents are voluntary; the Model Policy is brand-new (June 25, 2026), so most near-term incoming cohorts predate it and were governed, if at all, by the 2023 guidance as adopted locally. *Basis: Educ. Code §33308.5 disclaimers; Model Policy posting date.*
- **Where adopted**, California students may bring: familiarity with the 5 Big Ideas of AI and the learning-with/about distinction (2023 guidance); per-assignment AI-permission expectations and disclosure habits (Model Policy); a taught skepticism of AI detectors (Model Policy); exposure to AI-creation and computer-science-standards-based AI concepts (2023 guidance); and a human-connection framing.
- **Higher-education implication:** The most California-shaped students may arrive already fluent in the exact critical-evaluation vocabulary (claim extraction, source triangulation, confidence calibration) the Model Policy names — an unusually strong foundation to build on. But assume wide variance given voluntariness and the Model Policy's recency.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** California's Model Policy anticipates key higher-education debates. Students from adopting LEAs may arrive expecting that AI detectors are not used as sole evidence and that authorship concerns rely on process and engagement (Model Policy, "AI Detection Software"). Institutions relying on detectors as primary evidence will be out of step with these students' K-12 experience.
- **Higher-education implication:** Students may expect per-assignment AI-permission clarity and disclosure norms (Model Policy, "Instructional Discretion," "Plagiarism, Disclosure, & Citation"). A silent syllabus will contrast with that.
- **Higher-education implication:** The 2023 guidance explicitly connected K-12 to postsecondary via its webinar "featuring educators from K12 and postsecondary spaces" and a college/career-readiness section (CDE-Learning-with-AI-2023.pdf). California higher education is already positioned as a partner in this pipeline.
- **Higher-education implication:** California students may expect privacy-by-design guarantees — that their data will not train public models (Model Policy, "Privacy by Design"). Institutions should confirm their sanctioned tools meet that expectation.
- **Higher-education implication:** Note AB 2148 (recorded in sources.md, effective Jan 1 2027) is a "natural person" law, not a ban on instructional AI; do not overstate it as restricting classroom AI. Its exact scope should be confirmed from bill text not in the provided files.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Academic integrity:** Align with California's stance — do not treat AI detectors as sole evidence; use process, engagement, and authorship evidence, with rebuttal criteria available to students (mirrors Model Policy, "AI Detection Software").
- **Assessment design:** Adopt per-assignment AI permissioning and redesign assessments to emphasize human process over easily AI-generated products (Model Policy, "Instructional Discretion," "Curriculum & Assessment Redesign").
- **AI literacy onboarding:** Use California's named literacy skills (claim extraction, confidence calibration, source triangulation, mismatch detection) as an entry rubric, since some students will already have them (Model Policy, "AI Literacy as a Core Academic Competency").
- **AI tool availability and privacy:** Provide sanctioned tools that bar student data from training public models, matching the privacy-by-design expectation (Model Policy, "Privacy by Design").
- **Governance:** The Model Policy's ~two dozen principles are a ready scaffold for an institutional AI policy, including vendor contractual safeguards and pre-deployment evaluation.
- **Advancing learning rather than replacing thinking:** Adopt the efficiency-vs-learning modeling distinction (Model Policy, "Modeling, Practice, & Ongoing Discussion") and the students-as-creators framing (2023 guidance) to keep AI a subject of study, not a shortcut.

## Unique Aspects

- **A two-instrument approach:** an early (2023) conceptual guidance plus a detailed (2026) model policy under SB 1288 — few states have both.
- **Explicit anti-sole-evidence stance on AI detectors** with parent/student rebuttal criteria (Model Policy, "AI Detection Software").
- **Parent/guardian right to human review** of AI-generated outputs and annual informed consent for autonomous AI tools (Model Policy).
- **AI literacy as a core academic competency tied to statute** (Education Code §33548) with concrete misinformation-analysis skills.
- **Students-as-AI-creators** framing and the 5 Big Ideas of AI foundation (2023 guidance).
- **Curriculum/assessment redesign** guidance emphasizing human process over AI-generated products, with TK-12 grade-band AI-concept progressions.
