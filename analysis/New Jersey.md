## Executive Summary

New Jersey has no published state K-12 AI framework of the kind other states produced. NJDOE's Office of Innovation offers a curated AI resources page (an AI glossary, a weak-versus-strong AI explainer, a "Considerations for Artificial Intelligence in Education" webinar, and links to Code.org, ISTE, and the U.S. Department of Education), plus a 2025 AI Innovation in Education grant. The page states the resources are informational only (sources.md). There is no state permission scale, no prohibition list, and no student-use vocabulary.

The center of gravity in New Jersey is legislative, not administrative. A4352 (with companion S2862) would, if enacted, do three things. It would require every school district to "incorporate instruction on artificial intelligence" in grades K-12 as part of the New Jersey Student Learning Standards in Computer Science and Design Thinking (bill, sec.1.b). It would require the Commissioner of Education to provide districts with age-appropriate sample activities (sec.1.d). And it would require that "Each public institution of higher education shall offer certificate and degree programs in artificial intelligence" (sec.2.a), with the Secretary of Higher Education developing model curricula for four-year institutions and county colleges (sec.2.b).

For a higher-education reader, this is the strongest direct higher-education hook in the dataset, and it corrects a prior project note. The bill does not merely direct model curricula. As introduced, it would place an affirmative obligation on New Jersey public colleges and universities to *offer* AI certificate and degree programs. That obligation is contingent on enactment, and the bill was only introduced, so it is a pending requirement, not current law.

## K–12 AI Policy and Guidance

### Primary themes and policy objectives
- **New Jersey's operative policy vehicle is legislation, not agency guidance.** NJDOE's contribution is a resources page "for informational purposes only" (sources.md), so the substantive policy content lives in A4352, which is not yet law. *[Framing]*
- **Where the bill states objectives, they are workforce- and standards-oriented.** K-12 AI instruction is folded into the existing Computer Science and Design Thinking standards (bill, sec.1.b), and the higher-education provisions are tied to career pathways and "artificial intelligence career pathway marketing materials" (sec.2.d). *[Pending requirement, if enacted]*

### Intended audiences and scope
- **NJDOE resources** address educators informally (sources.md). *[Scope statement]*
- **A4352, if enacted,** would reach all K-12 districts (grades K-12, sec.1.b) and all public institutions of higher education, both four-year institutions and county colleges (sec.2). *[Pending scope, if enacted]*

### Permitted, restricted, and prohibited AI uses
- **There is no statewide permission scale and no statewide prohibition list.** NJDOE published none, and A4352 sets curricular requirements, not permitted/prohibited use rules for students. *[Not found in the provided documents]*
- The bill's K-12 content requirement includes "best practices to ensure the responsible, appropriate, and ethical use of artificial intelligence" (sec.1.c.3), but does not define permitted or prohibited student uses. *[Pending requirement, if enacted]*

### Privacy, security, equity, accessibility, ethics, and academic integrity
- Not found in the provided documents. Neither the NJDOE resources page (as recorded) nor A4352 sets privacy, security, equity, accessibility, or academic-integrity rules. The bill's only ethics content is the "responsible, appropriate, and ethical use" instructional requirement (sec.1.c.3). Federal law (FERPA, COPPA) applies to New Jersey districts as elsewhere, but no state AI-specific text restates it in the record. *[Unresolved]*

### Teacher training, curriculum integration, assessment, and implementation
- **Curriculum integration, if the bill passes, would be by embedding** AI instruction into the existing Computer Science and Design Thinking standards rather than a standalone course (sec.1.b). *[Pending requirement, if enacted]*
- **Implementation support, if enacted,** would come from the Commissioner of Education's "age-appropriate sample learning activities and resources" (sec.1.d) and, for higher education, the Secretary of Higher Education's model curricula developed in consultation with Labor and Workforce Development, the Economic Development Authority, the NJ Council of County Colleges, the NJ Presidents' Council, and the state Chief Innovation Officer (sec.2.b). *[Pending requirement, if enacted]*
- **Assessment** is not addressed by either source. *[Not found in the provided documents]*
- NJDOE's current, non-binding support is the "Considerations for Artificial Intelligence in Education" webinar and the 2025 AI Innovation in Education grant (sources.md). *[Example]*

## Expected Graduate AI Profile

Because there is no current state guidance and the bill is not enacted, no state-shaped graduate profile can be asserted for students already in the pipeline.

- **Current New Jersey graduates were governed at the district level**, with only informal NJDOE resources at the state level (sources.md). No state scale or vocabulary shaped their experience. *Basis: sources.md.*
- **If A4352 is enacted, future graduates would arrive having received K-12 AI instruction** covering general AI concepts, foundational user skills, and responsible-use best practices, embedded in Computer Science and Design Thinking (sec.1.b-c). *Basis: bill secs.1.b-c, pending.*
- **Higher-education implication:** For students entering now, treat prior AI instruction as unknown and district-specific. For cohorts several years out, watch A4352's status; if enacted, incoming New Jersey students would carry a defined, standards-based AI-instruction floor.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** A4352 is the dataset's most direct obligation on higher education itself. As introduced, "Each public institution of higher education shall offer certificate and degree programs in artificial intelligence" (sec.2.a). If enacted, every New Jersey public four-year institution and county college would have to stand up AI certificate and degree programs, supported by state model curricula (sec.2.b). This is a pending requirement, not current law, and its force depends entirely on passage.
- **Higher-education implication:** The bill corrects a common tracker error in both directions. It is not merely a "model curricula" bill, and it is not merely a K-12 bill. It pairs a K-12 instruction mandate with an affirmative program-offering mandate on public colleges (secs.1-2). Institutions should read the operative text, not the compressed synopsis.
- **Higher-education implication:** The model curricula, if developed, would be built with workforce and county-college input (Labor and Workforce Development, Economic Development Authority, NJ Council of County Colleges, NJ Presidents' Council, sec.2.b) and would have to "account for academic quality and other applicable standards required for accreditation" (sec.2.c). Institutions may face a state-shaped template for AI program design.
- **Higher-education implication:** For students entering before any enactment, there is no state AI baseline to assume, so entry planning should mirror the no-state-guidance states, an explicit orientation and diagnostic at entry.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Track the bill.** New Jersey public institutions should monitor A4352/S2862. If it advances, they will need to plan AI certificate and degree programs and engage with the Secretary of Higher Education's model-curriculum process (secs.2.a-b).
- **Engage the consultation channels.** The bill routes model-curriculum development through the NJ Presidents' Council and the NJ Council of County Colleges (sec.2.b). Institutions can shape the template through those bodies rather than receive it passively.
- **Onboarding for current cohorts:** Assume no state AI baseline for students entering now. Give an explicit AI-use orientation and a short literacy diagnostic.
- **Assessment design:** State AI permission explicitly per assignment; New Jersey students do not yet share a standardized state scale or vocabulary.
- **Prepare for a standards-based intake later.** If A4352 is enacted, align entry expectations to its K-12 content (general concepts, user skills, responsible-use best practices, sec.1.c) once those cohorts arrive.

## Unique Aspects

- **The dataset's strongest direct higher-education mandate, pending enactment.** A4352 would require public colleges and universities to *offer* AI certificate and degree programs (sec.2.a), not merely to receive model curricula.
- **A paired K-12-and-higher-ed structure.** The same bill mandates K-12 AI instruction and higher-education AI programs, with the Secretary of Higher Education's model curricula explicitly framed as support for the program-offering mandate (sec.2.b).
- **Workforce and county-college integration.** Development involves Labor and Workforce Development, the Economic Development Authority, and the NJ Council of County Colleges, plus AI "career pathway marketing materials" (sec.2.b, d).
- **An agency posture of informational-only resources.** NJDOE deliberately labels its AI page informational (sources.md), leaving substantive policy to the legislature, a different division of labor from the guidance-authoring states.
