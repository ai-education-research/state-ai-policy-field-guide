## Executive Summary

New York has no statewide K-12 AI guidance from NYSED in the record. The only NYSED AI page found concerns AI use in licensed proprietary (adult career) schools through the Bureau of Proprietary School Supervision, not K-12 (sources.md). Statewide AI policy for schools is, at this point, entirely legislative and entirely pending.

Two introduced Assembly bills define the near-term direction. A9190 (introduced Nov 3, 2025) would prohibit AI in classrooms before ninth grade, with carve-outs for diagnostics, explicit-instruction interventions for students with disabilities, and staff administrative or planning use, and would direct the Commissioner to issue guidance on permissible uses (sources.md). A6972 (introduced Mar 18, 2025) would create a 15-member AI working group in the education department with a schedule of deliverables, guidance, a model policy, and a final report, sunsetting in 2031 (sources.md). Neither is enacted.

For a higher-education reader, two facts matter. First, New York has set no state AI-permission scale, prohibition list, or student-use vocabulary at the K-12 level, so there is no state baseline to assume. Second, the most consequential real-world influence on New York graduates is almost certainly NYC Public Schools' own guidance, given the district's size, but that document is JS-rendered and could not be verified here. Search snippets suggest it is developing grade-band-differentiated guidance (K-5, 6-8, 9-12), but that is unconfirmed from a primary source.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Onboarding:** Assume no statewide baseline. Give incoming New York students an explicit AI-use orientation and a short literacy diagnostic.
- **Check the NYC district framework directly.** For institutions drawing heavily from NYC, retrieve and review the NYC Public Schools guidance (which this analysis could not machine-read) to understand what a large fraction of incoming students actually experienced.
- **Assessment design:** State AI permission explicitly per assignment; New York students share no statewide scale or vocabulary.
- **Academic-integrity processes:** Set integrity expectations from first principles; New York established no statewide stance on AI detectors or disclosure.
- **Monitor pending legislation.** Track A9190 and A6972; either could establish a state baseline (an age-gate or a model policy) that changes entry assumptions for future cohorts.
