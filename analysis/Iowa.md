## Executive Summary

Iowa is a no-current-guidance state with active legislation. The Iowa DOE has issued no K-12 AI classroom guidance (sources.md). The substantive activity is a pair of introduced bills — HF 2540 (successor to HSB 610) and SF 2094 — that would, **beginning with the 2030-31 graduating class, require one semester of computer science and artificial intelligence for graduation** (HF2540.pdf, Sec. 1). The bills also set AI content standards ("instruction that explains what artificial intelligence is, how artificial intelligence works, and the societal and ethical impacts of artificial intelligence," Sec. 2), direct the department to publish approved course lists and a teacher-capacity plan by June 30, 2027 (Sec. 4), and specifically require targeted support for small schools (Sec. 4). Separately, Iowa deployed a $3 million AI-powered reading tutor (EPS Reading Assistant) to elementary schools in 2024 (sources.md).

For a higher-education reader, three points matter. First, Iowa currently offers **no shared AI baseline**, so incoming Iowa students' AI exposure is district-dependent. Second, if the bills pass, a guaranteed CS+AI credit arrives with the class of 2030-31 — well beyond near-term admissions cycles. Third, the bills carry higher-education hooks: they invite input from "higher education institutions that offer computer science teacher preparation pathways" (Sec. 4) and, per sources.md, SF 2094 includes college-admissions provisions.

## K–12 AI Policy and Guidance

### Primary themes and policy objectives
- **No current state guidance.** The honest finding is that Iowa has issued no statewide K-12 AI classroom guidance (sources.md). *[Absence — confirmed]*
- **Proposed objective (pending bills):** establish CS+AI education "setting a foundation for personal and professional success in a high-technology, knowledge-based Iowa economy" (HF2540.pdf, Sec. 2). *[Unresolved — introduced bill]*

### Intended audiences and scope
- The bills would apply to "all students in school districts and accredited nonpublic schools" at the high-school graduation level, with CS+AI instruction offered at additional grade levels (HF2540.pdf, Secs. 1, 5-6). *[Scope — proposed]*

### Permitted, restricted, and prohibited AI uses
- **Not found in the provided documents.** Iowa has no guidance defining permitted/prohibited classroom AI *use*; the bills concern AI *as a subject of instruction* (a CS+AI course), not student AI-use permissions. *[Absence / distinction]*

### Privacy, security, equity, accessibility, ethics, and academic integrity
- **Ethics as curriculum content:** proposed standards require instruction on "the societal and ethical impacts of artificial intelligence" (HF2540.pdf, Sec. 2). *[Unresolved — proposed]*
- **Equity via small-school support:** the department's proposed teacher-capacity plan must "specifically address targeted support for schools with a total enrollment of less than five hundred students in grades nine through twelve" (HF2540.pdf, Sec. 4). *[Unresolved — proposed]*
- A privacy/academic-integrity framework for student AI *use* is **Not found in the provided documents** (no guidance exists). *[Absence]*

### Teacher training, curriculum integration, assessment, and implementation
- **Proposed department duties (HF 2540):** publish a list of qualifying CS+AI courses by June 30, 2027 (Sec. 4); create and publish a teacher-capacity expansion plan by June 30, 2027, with input from students, PD providers, industry, and higher-education CS-teacher-prep programs (Sec. 4); maintain a statewide K-12 CS+AI instruction plan (Sec. 3). *[Unresolved — proposed]*
- **Course structure:** one semester of CS+AI would satisfy both the CS+AI requirement and one semester of the math or science requirement (HF2540.pdf, Sec. 1). *[Unresolved — proposed]*
- **Deployed AI tool:** Iowa DOE launched a $3M AI reading tutor (voice-recognition EPS Reading Assistant) free to elementary schools (sources.md). *[Other — deployed program, not guidance]*

## Expected Graduate AI Profile

- **Near-term (current and next several cohorts): no guaranteed baseline.** With no state guidance and the graduation requirement not effective until the class of 2030-31 (if enacted), incoming Iowa students' AI exposure is entirely district-dependent. *Basis: sources.md; HF2540.pdf, Sec. 1.*
- **Youngest students:** may have used the state-funded AI reading tutor in elementary school (sources.md), an early exposure to AI as a learning tool.
- **Future (class of 2030-31 onward, if enacted):** would have completed a semester of CS+AI covering what AI is, how it works, and its societal/ethical impacts (HF2540.pdf, Secs. 1-2).
- **Higher-education implication:** For all current admissions cycles, assume no common Iowa AI baseline and high inter-district variance. Do not assume incoming Iowa students have had formal AI instruction; the guaranteed CS+AI credit is a 2030-31-and-later phenomenon and contingent on enactment.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** Because Iowa has no state guidance and no enacted requirement yet, Iowa cohorts will be especially heterogeneous in AI literacy. Plan for a common entry baseline rather than assuming prior instruction.
- **Higher-education implication:** If enacted, the bills reach higher education directly: the department must consult "higher education institutions that offer computer science teacher preparation pathways" (HF2540.pdf, Sec. 4), and SF 2094 reportedly includes college-admissions provisions (sources.md). Iowa teacher-prep programs could be formally embedded in implementation.
- **Higher-education implication:** The proposed course would double-count as a math or science credit (HF2540.pdf, Sec. 1). Admissions offices evaluating Iowa transcripts should understand that a CS+AI course may occupy a math/science slot.
- **Higher-education implication:** Iowa's youngest students already use a state-funded AI reading tutor (sources.md), so early AI-as-tool familiarity may precede any formal AI literacy instruction.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Onboarding:** Treat Iowa cohorts as pre-baseline; run an explicit AI-literacy entry check. Revisit once/if the CS+AI requirement is enacted and implemented.
- **Academic integrity and assessment design:** Provide explicit per-assignment AI-permission structures, since Iowa students are unlikely to arrive with a shared AI-use framework.
- **Teacher-prep alignment:** Iowa CS-teacher-preparation programs should monitor HF 2540 / SF 2094, which would name them as consulted parties (HF2540.pdf, Sec. 4).
- **Admissions:** Track SF 2094's college-admissions provisions (sources.md) and the math/science double-counting (HF2540.pdf, Sec. 1) when evaluating Iowa transcripts.
- **Monitoring:** Watch for enactment; the bills are introduced, not law.

## Unique Aspects

- **A CS+AI graduation requirement structured to double-count** as a math or science credit (HF2540.pdf, Sec. 1) — an unusual scheduling design.
- **AI content standards written into statute (proposed)** — "what artificial intelligence is, how artificial intelligence works, and the societal and ethical impacts of artificial intelligence" (Sec. 2).
- **Explicit small-school equity provision** in the proposed teacher-capacity plan (Sec. 4).
- **A deployed statewide AI reading tutor** for elementary students (sources.md) — AI adoption preceding AI guidance.
- **Higher-education teacher-prep and college-admissions hooks** in the pending bills (Sec. 4; sources.md).

## Evidence Gaps and Uncertainties
