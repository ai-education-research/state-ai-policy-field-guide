## Executive Summary

Florida is not an "absence" state, but its state-level AI-education effort is **university-led rather than agency-issued**. The Florida K-12 AI Education Task Force is coordinated by the University of Florida's CS Everyone Center and aligns its guidance to the externally developed EDSAFE "SAFE Framework"; its working materials live as Google Docs and an "FL AI Policy Tracker," none captured in the provided files (sources.md). Separately, FLDOE is reported to be moving through rulemaking to require districts to fold AI into their internet-safety policies, but that rule's specifics are unverified here.

For a higher-education reader, the single most useful fact is the **authorship hook**: Florida's most substantive statewide AI-education guidance is produced by a university (UF), not the state agency. That is itself a higher-education involvement story. Beyond that, the substantive content — principles, prohibitions, any student-use instrument — is not in the provided documents, so what Florida graduates were actually taught cannot be characterized from this evidence and would require capturing the task force's live materials.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** Florida's most substantive statewide AI-education guidance is **authored by a university** (UF's CS Everyone Center), not the state agency (sources.md). For higher education this is a direct hook: a Florida university is effectively shaping the K-12 AI-readiness of the students that Florida colleges will enroll. In-state institutions could engage with or build on that task force.
- **Higher-education implication:** If FLDOE's rulemaking frames AI primarily as an internet-safety/filtering matter (sources.md), Florida students may arrive having encountered AI chiefly as a safety-and-restriction topic rather than a literacy-and-creation one. This should be verified, but if so, it implies a narrower prior exposure than in instrument-rich states.
- **Higher-education implication:** The evidence gap is itself the administrator's lesson: Florida's guidance is real but invisible to document-based review because it lives in university-hosted web documents. Verify against those sources rather than concluding Florida has "no framework."
