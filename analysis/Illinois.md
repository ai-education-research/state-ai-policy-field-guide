## Executive Summary

Illinois' guidance is unusually deep and deliberately not technology-centered. It was developed under Public Act 104-0399, which charged ISBE with statewide guidance on AI use "while preserving the human relationships essential to effective teaching and learning" (ISBE-AI-Guidance.pdf, p.2). It is organized around **four guiding tenets**: (1) teaching and learning are shaped by human relationships and each child's experiences, interests, and assets; (2) schools serve academic, developmental, and civic purposes concurrently; (3) AI is a means to inform teaching and learning rather than an end in itself; and (4) informed use requires deliberate, context-sensitive, and locally determined purpose and use (p.4).

For a higher-education reader, three findings matter. First, Illinois **cautions against relying on AI-detection tools** — they should be "one data point, not... definitive proof of cheating," and it warns against "gotcha" approaches that "produce false [positives]" (academic-integrity discussion; lines ~2004, ~3635, ~3642). Second, it engages **over-reliance and reduced productive struggle** directly as a design concern and stresses "metacognitive challenge while preserving originality and student voice" (lines ~4113, ~8245). Third, it draws the **support-versus-high-stakes line** the dataset flagged: AI may "assist in supporting aspects of evaluation (i.e., development of rubrics)" but "teacher evaluations" appear on a high-stakes list where AI should not be the basis (lines ~2085, ~8371). The document is voluntary and locally determined, so student exposure varies by district, and it discloses that AI assisted its own drafting (sources.md).

## K–12 AI Policy and Guidance

### Primary themes and policy objectives
- **The teacher-student interaction is the centerpiece.** "It is organized around the enduring educational premise that the teacher-student interaction is the centerpiece of teaching and learning" (ISBE-AI-Guidance.pdf, p.2). *[Recommendation]*
- **AI as means, not end** (Tenet 3, p.4): AI "must remain a means to inform teaching and learning rather than a substitute for educator judgment, student relationships, or the developmental and civic purposes of public schooling" (p.2). *[Recommendation]*
- **Schools serve academic, developmental, and civic purposes concurrently** (Tenet 2, p.4). *[Recommendation]*
- **Locally determined use** (Tenet 4, p.4): informed use is "deliberate, context-sensitive, and locally determined." *[Recommendation]*

### Intended audiences and scope
- Explicitly multi-audience: FAQ answers are tagged by audience — "Superintendent; principal; teacher; parent/caregiver; advocate/stakeholder group; community" (ISBE-AI-Guidance.pdf, p.4). *[Scope]*
- Covers prekindergarten through Grade 12, at "district, school, and classroom levels" (p.4). *[Scope]*

### Permitted, restricted, and prohibited AI uses
- **No single statewide permission scale**; the document models informed, locally determined use rather than issuing rules. *[Not a mandate]*
- **High-stakes cautions:** AI should not be the basis for "student discipline outcomes, teacher evaluations, hiring decisions, or other high-stakes" decisions (line ~8371). *[Recommendation]*
- **Detection tools:** "Schools use AI-detection tools as one data point, not as definitive proof of cheating" and should "Be cautious with AI-detection tools; some district guidance advises against relying on them" (lines ~2004, ~3635). *[Recommendation]*

### Privacy, security, equity, accessibility, ethics, and academic integrity
- **Academic integrity** is handled through informed-use and disclosure rather than a detection regime; it warns that "early reliance on detection or 'gotcha' approaches can produce false [positives]" (line ~3642). *[Recommendation]*
- **Equity/accessibility:** the guidance foregrounds "equity, accessibility, privacy, safety, accountability, and civic responsibility" (p.3), and includes IEP/504-specific safeguards, e.g., "cognitive load safeguards" and misinformation-review triggers for high-incidence IEP/504 (line ~13987). *[Recommendation]*
- **Safety/privacy:** panel expertise explicitly includes AI safety and youth protection (Common Sense Media), and the guidance addresses hallucinations that are "difficult to detect without verification" (line ~1845). *[Recommendation]*
- **Bias/transparency:** the guidance addresses disparate impact and monitoring for harm (error patterns, uneven quality, misuse) (lines ~4644, ~9291). *[Recommendation]*

### Teacher training, curriculum integration, assessment, and implementation
- **Prompt development** for teachers is treated as a thread of professional learning and a component of student digital citizenship (ISBE-AI-Guidance.pdf, p.5, Q2). *[Recommendation]*
- **Bloom's Taxonomy as a lens** for instructional planning is a named framework ("Taxonomy as a Lens for Instructional Planning," p.5, Q3; Bloom reference in the bibliography, line ~14603). *[Instructional guidance]*
- **AI Literacy and Digital Citizenship** is a major section, including "Development as an Individual and Citizen – An Ecological Framework" (p.5, Q4). *[Recommendation]*
- **Assessment:** the guidance treats participation, persistence, and cognitive engagement "beyond grades or scores alone" (line ~8016) and emphasizes "metacognitive challenge while preserving originality and student voice" (line ~8245). *[Recommendation]*
- **Case studies:** the document includes numbered classroom case scenarios (e.g., "Case 14 – Grade 9-12 Teacher" on AI detection, line ~6936) as practical implementation aids. *[Examples]*

## Learning and Cognitive-Offloading Strategies

- Illinois engages over-reliance directly. It prompts districts to consider what "might decrease (over-reliance, reduced struggle)" when adopting AI (line ~4113) and to weigh "overreliance" as a risk (line ~4638). *[Recommendation]*
- It frames good assessment design around "metacognitive challenge while preserving originality and student voice" (line ~8245) and treats engagement as including "persistence, and cognitive engagement," not grades alone (line ~8016). *[Recommendation]*
- It cautions that "instructional design should not separate cognitive demand from context" and links AI use to "cognitive development, social and emotional development" (lines ~7656-7657). *[Instructional guidance]*
- It cites the over-reliance research base (e.g., "over-reliance (Roscoe et al. 2017)," line ~11915; the GPT-4 System Card on hallucinations and overreliance, lines ~14626-14630). *[Instructional guidance, cited]*
- Its structural guard against offloading is Tenet 3 (AI as means, not end) plus its caution against detection-driven "gotcha" enforcement in favor of learning-centered expectations (lines ~3642, ~3990). *[Recommendation]*

## Expected Graduate AI Profile

- **Uneven and recent.** The guidance is voluntary and locally determined (Tenet 4), and it posted June 2026, so near-term incoming cohorts largely predate district implementation. *Basis: p.4; June 2026 date.*
- **Where adopted**, Illinois students may bring: a human-relationship-centered framing of AI; exposure to AI literacy and digital citizenship as an ecological/developmental competency (p.5); a disclosure-based rather than detection-based integrity experience (lines ~2004, ~3635); and prompt-development skills (p.5).
- **Higher-education implication:** The most Illinois-shaped students may arrive having been taught that AI detectors are unreliable and that AI is a means, not an end. Assume variance given voluntariness and recency.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** Illinois students from adopting districts may arrive having been taught that AI detectors are "one data point, not... definitive proof of cheating" (line ~2004). Institutions relying on detectors as decisive evidence will diverge from that experience and may face pushback grounded in the students' K-12 guidance.
- **Higher-education implication:** The high-stakes cautions (no AI as the basis for discipline, teacher evaluations, hiring, line ~8371) transfer directly to institutional practice — a ready list of decisions faculty and administrators should not delegate to AI.
- **Higher-education implication:** Illinois' emphasis on over-reliance and "reduced struggle" (line ~4113) means some students were taught to notice when AI use erodes their own learning. Faculty can build on that self-monitoring stance.
- **Higher-education implication:** The document's own disclosure that AI assisted its drafting (sources.md) models the transparency it asks for — an institution can adopt the same "disclose your AI use" norm for faculty and students.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Academic integrity:** Adopt Illinois' stance — detectors as at most one data point, not proof; favor disclosure and learning-centered expectations over "gotcha" enforcement (lines ~2004, ~3642).
- **Assessment design:** Design for "metacognitive challenge while preserving originality and student voice" (line ~8245); assess persistence and engagement, not just products (line ~8016).
- **Decision boundaries:** Reuse the high-stakes list (line ~8371) to define what AI must not decide (discipline, evaluations, hiring).
- **Onboarding and faculty development:** Use Illinois' AI-literacy/digital-citizenship framing and prompt-development thread (p.5) as ready scaffolds; run an entry AI-literacy check given uneven adoption.
- **Governance:** Borrow the four tenets (p.4) as an institutional AI-policy backbone, keeping AI a means to teaching and learning.
- **Advancing learning rather than replacing thinking:** Center the human relationship and treat AI as a means (Tenets 1, 3), mirroring the guidance students may have experienced.

## Unique Aspects

- **Scale and depth:** a ~409-page statewide guidance built by a Blue Ribbon Panel (Brookings, Khan Academy, EdSAFE AI Alliance, Common Sense Media, and others), organized around four tenets (ISBE-AI-Guidance.pdf, pp.2-4).
- **Explicit caution against AI-detection tools** and "gotcha" enforcement (lines ~2004, ~3635, ~3642).
- **Bloom's Taxonomy as an instructional-planning lens** and an "ecological framework" for development as an individual and citizen (p.5).
- **Disclosed AI-assisted authorship** — the document was drafted with AI assistance and says so (sources.md), modeling transparency.
- **Audience-tagged FAQ** and numbered classroom case studies as practical navigation aids (pp.4-5; line ~6936).

## Evidence Gaps and Uncertainties

- **Voluntariness and recency.** Adoption and student exposure are **Not found in the provided documents**; the guidance is recommendations, posted June 2026 (p.4).
- **Method limitation:** given the ~409-page length, this analysis relied on framing pages plus systematic keyword search; some claims are cited by descriptive location rather than exact page, and sections not surfaced by those searches were not exhaustively reviewed. *[Partial]*
- **The "support but not score a teacher evaluation" distinction** the dataset flagged is substantially **confirmed in substance** — AI may "support aspects of evaluation (development of rubrics)" (line ~2085) while "teacher evaluations" are listed among high-stakes uses AI should not decide (line ~8371) — but the exact phrasing was not isolated to a single passage; treat as confirmed-in-substance. *[Partial]*
- **Enabling statute:** Public Act 104-0399 (the enacted SB 1920) is referenced within the guidance (p.2); its full text is **Not found in the provided documents.** Pending bills (e.g., HB 4411, full-year CS+AI) are noted in sources.md, not in the provided files. *[Partial]*
