## Executive Summary

Michigan's instrument is an **AI Integration Framework rubric** structured as three maturity stages — **Investigating, Implementing, Innovating** — across **eight domains**: Leadership & Vision; Policy, Ethical, & Legal Considerations; Instructional Framework; Learning Assessments; Professional Learning; Student Use; Business & Technology Operations; and Outreach (MichiganVirtual-AI-Integration-Framework.pdf). Each cell describes what that domain looks like at each stage, so a district can self-locate and plan its next step.

For a higher-education reader, this is the most **directly adoptable instrument** in the set for an institution: it is domain-and-stage agnostic to grade level. Swap "district" for "institution" and "students" for "students," and the rubric works as a college or university AI-readiness self-assessment. Its "Student Use — Innovating" cell also sketches an advanced-AI-literate graduate: one who uses AI to support learning goals "while critically assessing AI's societal impact, including biases, privacy concerns, and fairness issues, and making informed judgments about the authenticity and origin of content." Because it is a self-assessment rubric authored by a nonprofit and endorsed by MDE, it is voluntary and prescribes no specific tools or student-use rules.

## K–12 AI Policy and Guidance

### Primary themes and policy objectives
- **Maturity self-assessment, not prescription.** The rubric helps leaders assess "preparedness" and plan (MichiganVirtual-AI-Integration-Framework.pdf, note). *[Recommendation]*
- **Human talent preserved:** the Leadership Innovating stage envisions harnessing AI "while leveraging the distinct human talents of educators and staff" (Leadership & Vision, Innovating). *[Recommendation]*

### Intended audiences and scope
- Addressed to "educational leaders"/district leadership for planning across "teaching, learning, and operational functions" (MichiganVirtual-AI-Integration-Framework.pdf, note). *[Scope]*
- The eight domains span instruction, assessment, PD, student use, operations, and community outreach. *[Scope]*

### Permitted, restricted, and prohibited AI uses
- **Not applicable — this is a maturity rubric, not a use policy.** It contains no student-use permission scale or prohibition list. Policy, ethical, and legal considerations are one of the eight *domains* to mature, not a set of rules (Policy, Ethical, & Legal Considerations domain). *[Not present by design]*

### Privacy, security, equity, accessibility, ethics, and academic integrity
- **Policy, Ethical, & Legal Considerations domain:** progresses from "early stages of examining" risks and "appropriate access for all student populations" (Investigating) to "Board-approved policies, robust ethical guidelines, and a strong legal framework, demonstrating a commitment to accountability, data privacy, compliance," including a plan "to narrow educational equity gaps" (Innovating). *[Rubric descriptors]*
- **Equity** appears across domains (e.g., "address equity gaps," "narrow educational equity gaps"). *[Rubric descriptors]*
- A specific AI-detection-tool position, privacy statute list, or academic-integrity rule is **Not found in the provided documents** (the rubric does not operate at that level). *[Limited by document type]*

### Teacher training, curriculum integration, assessment, and implementation
- **Professional Learning domain:** progresses from educators seeking training independently (Investigating) to a variety of PD opportunities and expertise "to teach AI ethics to students" (Implementing) to AI systems generating "personalized professional development solutions" (Innovating). *[Rubric descriptors]*
- **Instructional Framework domain:** from independent educator exploration to AI enabling "personalized learning, foster[ing] learner ownership, leverag[ing] intelligent tutoring services" and data-driven decisions (Innovating). *[Rubric descriptors]*
- **Learning Assessments domain:** from AI enhancing formative measures to educators and students using AI to "holistically assess learning experiences and outcomes, including creativity, critical thinking, inventive problem-solving" (Innovating). *[Rubric descriptors]*
- **Business & Technology Operations** and **Outreach** domains cover operational automation and stakeholder communication maturity. *[Rubric descriptors]*

## Learning and Cognitive-Offloading Strategies

- The rubric's learning-preservation signal is in its **Innovating** descriptors: Learning Assessments emphasize "creativity, critical thinking, inventive problem-solving, and the application of knowledge in real-world situations"; Student Use emphasizes "critically assessing AI's societal impact... and making informed judgments about the authenticity and origin of content" (MichiganVirtual-AI-Integration-Framework.pdf). *[Rubric descriptors]*
- Leadership Innovating preserves "the distinct human talents of educators and staff." *[Rubric descriptor]*

## Expected Graduate AI Profile

- **Highly uneven — depends on district maturity stage.** Because the rubric explicitly models districts at different stages (Investigating vs Innovating), Michigan graduates' AI experience will vary by how mature their district's AI integration was. *Basis: the rubric's three-stage structure.*
- **From an "Innovating" district**, a graduate would, per the Student Use descriptor, use AI to support learning goals while "critically assessing AI's societal impact, including biases, privacy concerns, and fairness issues" and judging "the authenticity and origin of content." From an "Investigating" district, a graduate may have had only basic AI exposure "in a handful of classes."
- **Higher-education implication:** Michigan is a strong illustration that "the state has guidance" tells you little about any given graduate — district maturity is the real variable. Plan for a wide maturity range within any Michigan cohort.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** The maturity rubric is directly adoptable by a college or university. Its eight domains (leadership, policy/ethics/legal, instruction, assessment, PD, student/learner use, operations, outreach) and three stages map onto institutional AI-readiness self-assessment with minimal adaptation.
- **Higher-education implication:** The Student Use "Innovating" descriptor is a useful definition of an AI-ready graduate (critical assessment of bias/privacy/fairness; judging authenticity/origin of content). Institutions can use it as a target competency and as a diagnostic for incoming students.
- **Higher-education implication:** Because Michigan's guidance is a *maturity* model, it reframes the higher-ed question from "does the state have a policy?" to "where on the maturity curve was this student's district?" — a more accurate lens for anticipating preparation.
- **Higher-education implication:** Provenance matters — this is a nonprofit-authored, MDE-endorsed rubric, not an agency mandate. Faculty should weight it as a planning tool rather than a governing rule.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Governance:** Adopt the three-stage × eight-domain rubric as an institutional AI-readiness self-assessment; identify the institution's current stage per domain and plan next steps.
- **Onboarding:** Use the Student Use "Innovating" descriptor as a target AI-literacy profile and diagnose incoming students against it, expecting wide variance.
- **Faculty development:** Mirror the Professional Learning domain's progression toward educators able to teach AI ethics and design AI-integrated learning.
- **Assessment design:** Move toward the Learning Assessments "Innovating" descriptor — AI-supported assessment of creativity, critical thinking, and real-world application.
- **Advancing learning rather than replacing thinking:** Center the "distinct human talents" framing and the authenticity/origin-judgment competency.

## Unique Aspects

- **A maturity rubric rather than a policy** — three stages (Investigating, Implementing, Innovating) across eight domains (MichiganVirtual-AI-Integration-Framework.pdf). Districts self-locate rather than comply.
- **The most directly higher-education-adoptable instrument in the set** — grade-agnostic and readily convertible to an institutional self-assessment.
- **Nonprofit authorship with state endorsement** (Michigan Virtual, endorsed by MDE) — a distinctive governance model.
- **A clear "Innovating" definition of AI-literate student use** (critical assessment of bias/privacy/fairness; authenticity/origin judgment).
