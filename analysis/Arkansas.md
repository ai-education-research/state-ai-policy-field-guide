**The governing fact for Arkansas.** There is **no Arkansas Department of Education (ADE/DESE) K-12 AI classroom guidance** in the provided files (sources.md confirms none located). The two documents present are (1) a **third-party** district-planning framework from Virtual Arkansas — explicitly an adaptation of the Michigan Virtual AI Integration Framework — and (2) a **government-wide** AI strategy report that is not education-classroom guidance. Neither may be presented as ADE guidance. Claims below are cited accordingly.

## Executive Summary

Arkansas is a **no-state-guidance** jurisdiction. The most education-relevant artifact is not a state agency product at all: the "Planning Guide for AI: A Framework for School Districts" is published by **Virtual Arkansas in collaboration with Michigan Virtual** and states plainly that "An earlier version of this publication was authored by our good friends at Michigan Virtual and has been modified for this release" (VirtualArkansas-AI-Planning-Guide.pdf, p.1). It reproduces the same three-stage maturity rubric (Investigating / Implementing / Innovating) across the same eight domains as Michigan's framework (p.6). Separately, the Arkansas AI & Analytics Center of Excellence Initial Report is a **government-wide** strategy focused on protecting data, improving government services, and building an "AI-ready" workforce (AI-CoE-Initial-Report.pdf, Executive Summary) — not K-12 classroom guidance.

For a higher-education reader, the operative facts are: (1) Arkansas offers **no shared state K-12 AI baseline**, so incoming Arkansas students' AI experience is entirely district-dependent; (2) any structured district planning that occurred likely used the Michigan-lineage maturity rubric; and (3) Arkansas's state-level AI energy is in workforce/economic strategy (the CoE), which touches higher education only at the talent-pipeline level.

## Expected Graduate AI Profile

- **No common baseline; extreme variance.** With no ADE guidance, incoming Arkansas students' AI exposure depends entirely on their district. *Basis: sources.md.*
- Districts that used the Virtual Arkansas planning guide would have followed the Michigan-lineage maturity model, so their students' experience resembles the Michigan "stage-dependent" profile (see `analysis/Michigan.md`).
- **Higher-education implication:** Do not assume any shared Arkansas AI baseline. Plan for large district-to-district variation and confirm rather than infer.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** The absence of a state baseline means Arkansas cohorts will be heterogeneous in AI literacy and governance experience; institutions should establish a common entry baseline rather than assume prior instruction.
- **Higher-education implication:** Because the state's substantive K-12 planning resource is a Michigan-Virtual adaptation, Arkansas and Michigan graduates from planning-guide-using districts may share the same maturity-model framing — useful if an institution enrolls from both.
- **Higher-education implication:** Arkansas's state AI strategy is workforce- and government-services-oriented (the AI CoE), with "employer-aligned AI talent pipelines" (AI-CoE-Initial-Report.pdf, Rec. 6). Higher education's tie-in here is workforce development, not K-12 classroom preparation.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Onboarding:** Given no state baseline, run an explicit AI-literacy orientation and assume high variance among Arkansas students.
- **Academic integrity and assessment design:** Provide clear per-assignment AI-permission structures, since Arkansas students are unlikely to arrive with a shared AI-use framework.
- **Governance:** If a maturity self-assessment is useful, the Michigan-lineage rubric that Arkansas districts may have used is a ready model (see `analysis/Michigan.md`).
- **Workforce alignment:** Engage the Arkansas AI CoE's talent-pipeline agenda (AI-CoE-Initial-Report.pdf, Rec. 6) as the state's actual higher-ed-adjacent AI hook.
