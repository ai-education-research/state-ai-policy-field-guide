**Nature of the two documents.** Alabama presents a split picture. The **template is voluntary**: the superintendent's message says LEAs "are encouraged to adopt and customize the template" (ALSDE-AI-Policy-Template.docx, State Superintendent's Message), though ALSDE "encourages LEAs to adhere to this format to maintain uniformity" (same, Purpose). **HB 329 is an enacted Requirement**, but its central student obligation does not bite until the graduating class of 2032 (HB329-enrolled.pdf, p.4, §16-46B-7(c)(1)). Every claim below is tagged accordingly.

## Executive Summary

Alabama is a governance-first state. Its ALSDE template is one of the most operationally detailed model AI policies in the set, organized around **eight pillars** (Strategy, Governance, Data Privacy and Security, Procurement, Implementation, Competency Development, Risk Management, and Utility and Effectiveness) and built on the NIST AI Risk Management Framework (ALSDE-AI-Policy-Template.docx, Risk Management). It reads like a compliance and procurement instrument for district administrators rather than a classroom-pedagogy guide. Its most distinctive provision is a required **process for students to contest AI-generated grades** (same, Use of AI Systems).

Two cautions dominate the higher-education read. First, the template is voluntary, so actual student experience depends on which LEAs adopted it and how they customized it. Second, HB 329's graduation requirement (an approved computer-science credit, with "computer science" now defined to include artificial intelligence) applies **beginning with the class of 2032** (HB329-enrolled.pdf, p.4). That means students entering college in the near term are *not* yet covered by a guaranteed statewide AI/CS baseline. HB 329 does carry real higher-education hooks: a completed CS course can satisfy a public in-state university's math or science freshman-admission requirement (p.4, §16-46B-7(b)), and higher-education institutions are named among approved professional-learning providers (p.2, §16-46B-1(7)).

## K–12 AI Policy and Guidance

### Primary themes and policy objectives
- **Responsible integration that maximizes benefit while mitigating risk** is the template's stated primary goal (ALSDE-AI-Policy-Template.docx, Executive Summary and Purpose). *[Recommendation]*
- **AI supplements, not replaces, human instruction.** "AI systems will be used to supplement, not replace, human instruction" (same, Purpose). The eight pillars "place students and educators at the core... ensuring that AI systems supplement, rather than replace, human instruction" (Executive Summary). *[Recommendation / stated principle]*
- **A create-not-just-consume stance in statute.** HB 329 amends the computer-science definition so that "Content should focus on teaching students how to create new technologies, not simply how to use technology" (HB329-enrolled.pdf, p.1, §16-46B-1(2)). *[Requirement — definitional]*

### Intended audiences and scope
- The template addresses LEA leadership and defines "Users" broadly as "parents/guardians, students, educators, administrators, and management" (ALSDE-AI-Policy-Template.docx, Terminology). *[Scope]*
- HB 329 applies to all Alabama public K-12 schools (HB329-enrolled.pdf, p.2, §16-46B-1(10)). *[Scope, Requirement]*

### Permitted, restricted, and prohibited AI uses
- The template sets **permissive default use with conditions**: "All users are allowed to use AI systems" (ALSDE-AI-Policy-Template.docx, Use of AI Systems). *[Recommendation / model rule]*
- Conditions on use (model rules a district would adopt): users "need to indicate that the work is the result of AI"; must "cite in their works the AI systems used"; must "verify and validate the content before it is submitted"; teachers must tell students "when AI system(s) will be in their classrooms and how the AI system(s) work" (same). *[Recommendation / model rules]*
- **Restriction on AI surveillance/profiling:** where law does not authorize it, "LEAs shall ensure the AI systems do not track student's behaviors and are not used for profiling students" (same). *[Recommendation / model rule]*
- **Vendor data-training prohibition (Appendix A):** a Data Usage Restriction Clause states the contractor "is expressly prohibited from using any data provided to it... for the purpose of training a new AI System to be used in a new product" (same, Appendix A). *[Recommendation / model contract language]*
- **This is a template, so none of these are statewide mandates on content.** They become binding only where a district adopts them.

### Privacy, security, equity, accessibility, ethics, and academic integrity
- **Data privacy and security is one of the eight pillars**, tied to federal, state, and local compliance, plus compliance audits and Corrective Action Plans from contractors (ALSDE-AI-Policy-Template.docx, Data Privacy and Security). *[Recommendation]*
- **Academic integrity** is handled through the disclosure-and-citation model rules above rather than a detection regime. *[Recommendation]*
- **Accessibility/equity:** the template commits that "everyone gets access to the AI System" under the Utility and Effectiveness pillar (same). A deeper equity or accessibility framework (for example specific assistive-tech requirements) is **Not found in the provided documents**. *[Recommendation, limited]*
- **Ethics/bias:** procurement requires a contractor statement that they "have taken steps to minimize bias" and used "human-in-the-loop during model development" (same, AI Procurement). *[Recommendation / model rule]*

### Teacher training, curriculum integration, assessment, and implementation
- **Competency Development pillar:** LEAs "will develop an education and training program to ensure all users understand how AI works" across "computer science, mathematics, social studies, sciences" and safe/effective/responsible use (ALSDE-AI-Policy-Template.docx, AI Competency Development). *[Recommendation]*
- **Assessment/grading:** teachers using AI for grading "need to verify and validate the accuracy and completeness of the grading," and "LEAs shall develop a process for students to contest the grades generated by AI systems" (same, Use of AI Systems). *[Recommendation / model rule]*
- **Curriculum (statute):** HB 329 requires the department to identify approved CS courses and to "develop and publish a set of minimum standards that must be taught in every course with embedded computer science skills and experiences" (HB329-enrolled.pdf, p.4, §16-46B-7(c)(1)). *[Requirement on the agency]* The AI-specific content of those minimum standards is **Not found in the provided documents** (not yet published). *[Unresolved]*
- **Implementation/governance:** the template requires (as a model) an AI Governance Committee or expansion of a Data Governance Committee, plus a risk register with Low/Medium/High risk levels under the NIST AI RMF (ALSDE-AI-Policy-Template.docx, AI Governance; Risk Management). *[Recommendation / model structure]*

## Learning and Cognitive-Offloading Strategies

- The template's core anti-offloading commitment is the repeated "supplement, not replace, human instruction" principle and the human-in-the-loop requirement: LEAs are "committed to using human judgment during the implementation and use of AI systems and will develop a process as to why and when a human will be involved" (ALSDE-AI-Policy-Template.docx, Utility and Effectiveness). *[Recommendation]*
- The verify-and-validate rule (users must check AI content before submission or use) is a light guard against uncritical acceptance of AI output (same, Use of AI Systems). *[Recommendation / model rule]*
- HB 329's definitional emphasis on creating rather than merely using technology (p.1) is the closest statutory signal toward preserving higher-order skill. *[Requirement — definitional]*
- **A developed cognitive-science treatment (metacognition, independent reasoning, creativity, over-reliance) comparable to some other states is Not found in the provided documents.** Alabama's documents are governance and compliance instruments, not pedagogy guides. *[Evidence gap]*

## Expected Graduate AI Profile

- **Near-term graduates (before class of 2032):** No guaranteed statewide AI or CS exposure. Experience depends entirely on which LEA policies were adopted from the voluntary template and on existing CS offerings. *Basis: template is voluntary; HB 329's graduation requirement starts class of 2032 (HB329-enrolled.pdf, p.4).*
- **Graduates from the class of 2032 onward:** Will have earned at least one department-approved CS credit (or a course with embedded CS skills), where "computer science" is statutorily defined to include artificial intelligence and to emphasize creating technology (HB329-enrolled.pdf, p.1, p.4). *Basis: §16-46B-1(2), §16-46B-7(c)(1).*
- **Where a district adopted the template:** students may have experienced explicit AI-disclosure and citation expectations, teacher notice when AI is used in class, and a formal route to contest an AI-generated grade (ALSDE-AI-Policy-Template.docx, Use of AI Systems).
- **Higher-education implication:** For the next several admission cycles, assume no common Alabama AI baseline and high inter-district variance. Do not assume incoming Alabama students have had formal AI instruction. The guaranteed baseline is a 2032-and-later phenomenon.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** Alabama students from adopting districts may arrive expecting a *right to contest an algorithmic grade* (ALSDE-AI-Policy-Template.docx, Use of AI Systems). Most university grade-appeal policies say nothing about AI-assisted assessment, so these students may expect a due-process step their institution has not defined.
- **Higher-education implication:** Alabama's vendor Data Usage Restriction Clause (Appendix A) is a ready-made procurement model. An institution negotiating enterprise AI contracts could adopt the same "your data will not train a new product" attestation language.
- **Higher-education implication:** HB 329 already reaches into higher education in two concrete ways that in-state universities should note. A completed CS course can satisfy a math or science freshman-admission requirement at a public in-state institution (HB329-enrolled.pdf, p.4, §16-46B-7(b)), and higher-education institutions are eligible "High Quality Professional Learning Providers" for K-12 CS teacher training (p.2, §16-46B-1(7)). In-state institutions may already be admissions-linked to, or PD partners of, the K-12 system.
- **Higher-education implication:** Because the statutory baseline is dated to 2032, institutions should plan for a transition cohort problem. Students arriving in the late 2020s and early 2030s will straddle the "no guaranteed baseline" and "guaranteed CS-with-AI credit" regimes within a single incoming class.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **AI tool availability:** Adopt Alabama's vendor data-protection attestation (Appendix A) into institutional AI procurement so student and faculty data are contractually barred from training vendor products.
- **Onboarding:** Because near-term Alabama cohorts have no common AI baseline, run an entry AI-literacy check rather than assuming prior instruction. Revisit this assumption for 2032-and-later cohorts.
- **Assessment design and academic integrity:** Mirror the template's disclosure-and-citation expectation (students disclose and cite AI use) so the college expectation is continuous with what adopting Alabama districts taught. Consider defining an explicit appeals path for AI-assisted or AI-generated grades, which Alabama students from adopting districts may expect.
- **Governance:** Alabama's eight-pillar, NIST-based structure is a usable scaffold for an institutional AI governance committee, risk register, and audit cycle.
- **Faculty development:** Position institutional CS/AI PD offerings for the K-12 pipeline, since HB 329 makes higher-education institutions eligible PD providers (p.2).
- **Advancing learning rather than replacing thinking:** Build on the statute's "create, not just use" framing (p.1) and the template's human-in-the-loop and verify-and-validate rules to keep AI positioned as a tool under human judgment.

## Unique Aspects

- **A required student process to contest AI-generated grades** (ALSDE-AI-Policy-Template.docx, Use of AI Systems). This algorithmic-grade due-process idea is rare in the set.
- **Appendix A vendor Data Usage Restriction Clause** — detailed model contract language prohibiting use of LEA data to train a new AI product, with a Request-for-Approval process for district-specific customization (same, Appendix A).
- **Eight-pillar, NIST-AI-RMF governance architecture** with a formal risk register and Corrective Action Plans from vendors (same, Risk Management; AI Governance).
- **Statutory higher-education linkage:** CS course counts toward public in-state university math/science admission requirements, and universities are named PD providers (HB329-enrolled.pdf, p.4 and p.2).
- **Statutory AI-in-CS definition** effective October 1, 2026, with the graduation requirement dated to the class of 2032 (HB329-enrolled.pdf, p.1, p.4, p.5).

## Evidence Gaps and Uncertainties

- **Template adoption is unknown.** The document is a voluntary model, so how many of Alabama's LEAs adopted it, and with what customizations, is **Not found in the provided documents**. Student experience cannot be assumed statewide.
- **The AI content of the class-of-2032 CS standards is not yet defined.** HB 329 directs the department to publish minimum standards for embedded-CS courses, but those standards are **Not found in the provided documents**. *[Unresolved]*
- **Provenance of the template host.** The only retrievable copy is on a third-party site (aiforeducation.io), not an ALSDE domain (sources.md). Content was verified by extraction, but an agency-hosted original was not located. Treat the text as authentic per extraction while noting the host caveat.
- **No classroom-level student-use scale, prohibition list of specific tasks, or grade-band literacy progression** appears in either document. Those instruments are **Not found in the provided documents**.
- **Signing date and gubernatorial action on HB 329** were not the subject of the enrolled text beyond the October 1, 2026 effective date (HB329-enrolled.pdf, p.5); a signing date is **Not found in the provided documents**.
