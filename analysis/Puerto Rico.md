# Puerto Rico

*Puerto Rico is a US territory, not a state. Its Department of Education (DEPR) operates the entire territory as a single unified school system, so its guidance functions more directly than a US state's voluntary, district-adopted guidance. Both source documents are in Spanish; quotations below are translated, with the original terms preserved for key instruments.*

**Nature of the documents (read before everything below):** Because DEPR runs the whole territory as one system, its directives carry more weight than a US state agency's recommendations. The 2026 guide uses directive language for platform use, "the institutional use of AI in DEPR must be carried out exclusively through authorized platforms, such as MS Copilot" (2026, p.~245). Its pedagogical model (SENSE) is, by contrast, "a recommended, but not exclusive, guide" (2026, p.~1414). So platform and privacy rules read as Requirement-level for the system, while the pedagogical model and the five-level scale are Recommendation/Instructional-level. Tags below reflect that split.

## Executive Summary

Puerto Rico has two DEPR documents. The 2024 framework, "La inteligencia artificial en el sistema educativo," carries a five-level student scaffolding scale as an annex. The 2026 student-facing guide is built around a single mandated platform, MS Copilot, and an original pedagogical model called SENSE, with a suite of assessment rubrics.

The most important finding for this project is about lineage. Puerto Rico's five-level scale is explicitly a Spanish translation of Washington OSPI's "5 Step Scaffolding Scale for Students," taken from OSPI's "Implementing AI: A Practical Guide for the Classroom" (2024) under a Creative Commons license (2024, Anejo, p.~2017). The document names the source directly. Puerto Rico's levels, verbatim in Spanish, are Nivel 1 Sin Asistencia de IA, Nivel 2 Asistencia con IA (Generación de ideas), Nivel 3 IA Apoyando Redacción, Nivel 4 IA Colaborativa Creación, and Nivel 5 IA como Cocreador. This means Puerto Rico's scale lineage runs through Washington OSPI, not directly through Perkins et al., and it independently corroborates that Washington's instrument is a five-step student-permission scale.

For a higher-education reader, three facts matter. First, Puerto Rico mandates a single authorized platform (MS Copilot) for students 13 and older and prohibits unsupervised use, a more prescriptive posture than any US state's voluntary guidance. Second, its scale starts at Nivel 1 (no AI) with no "Level 0," a labeling difference from the 0-based scales (Oklahoma, North Carolina). Third, the 2026 guide's SENSE model and rubrics give students a structured metacognitive and integrity vocabulary a faculty member could recognize.

## K–12 AI Policy and Guidance

### Primary themes and policy objectives
- **Human-centered AI.** The 2024 framework describes its approach as "centered on the human being," citing Washington OSPI (2024, p.~401). *[Recommendation]*
- **Structured, safe, supervised integration through a single platform.** The 2026 guide's spine is that generative AI use "must be carried out exclusively through authorized platforms, such as MS Copilot," which meet cybersecurity and privacy requirements (2026, p.~245). *[Requirement-level for the system]*
- **Equity and closing achievement/digital gaps.** A stated objective is to "reduce educational and digital gaps, ensuring that all students, regardless of socioeconomic context or geographic location, have guided and safe access to Gen AI" (2026, p.~586). *[Recommendation]*
- **Academic-standards alignment**, with Spanish and English named as priority competency areas (2026, p.~620). *[Recommendation]*

### Intended audiences and scope
- The 2024 framework addresses the education system broadly; the 2026 guide is student-facing and teacher-supervised (titles; 2026, Purpose). *[Scope statement]*
- Scope centers on students aged 13 and older for MS Copilot use, with explicit concern about impact on "students under 13" (2026, pp.~446, 1429). *[Scope statement]*

### Permitted, restricted, and prohibited AI uses
- **The five-level scaffolding scale (2024, Anejo) is the permission instrument**, a translation of Washington OSPI's scale. Its levels, translated:
  - **Nivel 1 Sin Asistencia de IA (No AI Assistance)** — "No AI tools are used at any point. Students depend solely on their knowledge and skills." *[Example / instrument]*
  - **Nivel 2 Asistencia con IA, Generación de ideas (AI-Assisted, Idea Generation)** — "AI tools can help generate ideas. The final content must be created by the student without direct AI input. AI assistance must be cited." *[Example]*
  - **Nivel 3 IA Apoyando Redacción (AI Supporting Drafting)** — "AI can help draft initial versions. The final version must be significantly revised by the student. There must be a clear distinction between the AI's contribution and the student's." *[Example]*
  - **Nivel 4 IA Colaborativa Creación (AI Collaborative Creation)** — "AI-generated content may be included. The student must critically evaluate and edit the AI's contributions. AI use must be transparent and cited." *[Example]*
  - **Nivel 5 IA como Cocreador (AI as Co-Creator)** — "Extensive use of AI in content creation. The student provides a justification for the AI use and ensures original thinking. The work adheres to academic integrity with appropriate citations." *[Example]*
- **A hard platform restriction.** Only authorized platforms (MS Copilot) may be used institutionally (2026, p.~245). *[Requirement-level for the system]*
- **Unsupervised use is prohibited.** "Unsupervised use is prohibited to avoid technological dependence and" related harms (2026, p.~916). *[Requirement-level for the system]*
- **Content prohibitions.** Gen AI use "to generate content" of prohibited kinds is barred (2026, p.~1285). *[Requirement-level for the system]*

### Privacy, security, equity, accessibility, ethics, and academic integrity
- **Privacy and cybersecurity drive the platform mandate.** MS Copilot is chosen because it "complies with cybersecurity requirements, privacy policy, [and] protection" of data (2026, p.~246), with attention to laws applicable to minors (2026, p.~606). *[Requirement-level for the system]*
- **Digital ethics is a SENSE component.** Students are guided toward "legitimate, honest, and conscious use of Gen AI to avoid plagiarism, technological dependence, harassment, or disinformation" (2026, p.~630). *[Recommendation]*
- **Academic integrity has its own rubric** (Anejo 10, "Rúbrica global para evaluar la integridad académica"), alongside rubrics for prompt quality, metacognition, and validation of AI-generated information (2026, Anejos 8-12). *[Instructional guidance / instrument]*
- **Position on AI detection tools:** the documents do not center AI detectors. Integrity is handled through the scale's citation rules, the SENSE ethics component, and dedicated rubrics rather than detection software. *[Not emphasized]*

### Teacher training, curriculum integration, assessment, and implementation
- **The SENSE model structures implementation.** SENSE stands for Seguridad, Ética, Narrativa, Sinergia humano-IA, y Evaluación de resultados (Safety, Ethics, Narrative, Human-AI Synergy, and Evaluation of results) (2026, pp.189, 972). It guides teachers in "planning, implementation" and evaluation (2026, p.959). *[Recommendation / instructional model]*
- **Teacher training precedes use.** "Prior training on the SENSE model, digital ethics, and safety in AI use" is expected (2026, p.~1235). *[Recommendation]*
- **Assessment is rubric-rich.** The guide includes analytic and global rubrics for SENSE progress, prompt quality, academic integrity, metacognition, and information validation (2026, Anejos 8-12). *[Instrument]*
- **Curriculum alignment** ties AI use to DEPR curriculum and priority competencies in Spanish and English (2026, pp.620, 925). *[Recommendation]*

## Learning and Cognitive-Offloading Strategies

Puerto Rico addresses over-reliance through supervision rules, the scale's design, and the SENSE model.

- **The prohibition on unsupervised use is an explicit anti-dependence control.** Unsupervised use is barred specifically "to avoid technological dependence" (2026, p.~916). *[Requirement-level for the system]*
- **Nivel 1 preserves unaided work by definition.** At Nivel 1, "students depend solely on their knowledge and skills" (2024, Anejo). *[Example / instrument]*
- **The scale requires escalating student responsibility.** By Nivel 5, "the student provides a justification for the AI use and ensures original thinking" (2024, Anejo). *[Example]*
- **SENSE embeds metacognition and human-AI synergy.** The model's "Sinergia humano-IA" and "Evaluación" components, plus a dedicated metacognition rubric (Anejo 11), keep student reflection central (2026, pp.972, Anejos). *[Recommendation / instrument]*
- **Digital ethics names dependence as a harm to avoid** alongside plagiarism, harassment, and disinformation (2026, p.630). *[Recommendation]*

## Expected Graduate AI Profile

These describe habits a Puerto Rico graduate *may* bring. Because DEPR runs a unified system, exposure is likely more uniform than in US states, though implementation still varies by school and teacher, and the platform-based program targets students 13 and older.

- **Familiarity with a five-level scaffolding scale**, from "No AI Assistance" to "AI as Co-Creator," with per-level citation expectations (2024, Anejo). *Basis: 2024 Anejo, translated from Washington OSPI.*
- **Experience with a single institutional AI platform (MS Copilot)** rather than a range of tools, for students 13 and older (2026, p.245). *Basis: 2026.*
- **A supervised-use norm.** Students were told unsupervised AI use is prohibited (2026, p.916). *Basis: 2026.*
- **A structured integrity, prompt-quality, and metacognition vocabulary** from the SENSE rubrics, where teachers used them (2026, Anejos 8-12). *Basis: 2026.*
- **Higher-education implication:** Because DEPR is a single system with directive platform and supervision rules, Puerto Rico graduates may share a more consistent AI baseline than US-state peers, but that baseline is platform-specific (Copilot) and supervision-heavy, which may not match a US university's open-tool environment.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** Puerto Rico students may arrive fluent in a *five-level scaffolding scale* whose levels match Washington's (and are close in spirit to the AIAS-derived scales). A university adopting a leveled AI-permission scale can expect partial recognition, though the vocabulary is Spanish and the top level is "IA como Cocreador."
- **Higher-education implication:** Puerto Rico students were trained on a *single mandated platform (MS Copilot)* under *supervision* (2026, pp.245, 916). A US institution's open, multi-tool, largely unsupervised environment will be a significant shift, and these students may expect a single sanctioned tool and clearer boundaries than they encounter.
- **Higher-education implication:** The scale's per-level *citation and transparency* rules (2024, Anejo) mean Puerto Rico students may already expect to disclose and cite AI use. This maps well onto university integrity norms.
- **Higher-education implication:** Because the program targets students 13 and older and prohibits unsupervised use, incoming students' independent AI-use experience may be more limited and more scaffolded than a faculty member assumes, especially for younger content or students who left the system early.
- **Higher-education implication:** Language matters. These students learned AI vocabulary in Spanish (SENSE, the scale levels). A bilingual bridge to English AI-literacy terms may ease their transition.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Assessment design:** Adopt an explicit per-assignment AI-permission scale; Puerto Rico students will recognize a leveled structure with citation rules (2024, Anejo). Preserve Nivel 1-style no-AI assessments where core-skill demonstration is the goal.
- **Tool and supervision expectations:** Recognize that Puerto Rico students may expect a single sanctioned platform and supervised use (2026, pp.245, 916). Set explicit expectations about which tools are allowed and how independent use is governed on campus.
- **Language bridge:** Offer AI-literacy materials that connect the Spanish vocabulary (SENSE, scale levels) to English equivalents for incoming Puerto Rico students.
- **Onboarding:** Give a short AI-literacy diagnostic; despite the unified system, school- and teacher-level implementation varied, and the program's 13+ scope means uneven independent experience.
- **Academic-integrity processes:** Build on the disclosure-and-citation habit the scale and SENSE rubrics instilled (2024 Anejo; 2026 Anejos), favoring process- and rubric-based integrity.

## Unique Aspects

- **A unified-system posture.** As a single territory-wide department, DEPR issues directive rules (mandatory authorized platform, prohibited unsupervised use) that function more like district policy than voluntary state guidance.
- **A single mandated AI platform (MS Copilot)** for students 13 and older, chosen for privacy and cybersecurity compliance (2026, pp.245-246), a more prescriptive tool posture than any US state in the dataset.
- **The SENSE model** (Seguridad, Ética, Narrativa, Sinergia humano-IA, Evaluación), an original DEPR pedagogical framework with a matching suite of rubrics (2026).
- **A five-level scaffolding scale translated verbatim from Washington OSPI**, with explicit Creative Commons attribution (2024, Anejo). Levels begin at Nivel 1 (no AI), with no "Level 0."
- **Spanish-language delivery**, giving graduates AI vocabulary in Spanish.
