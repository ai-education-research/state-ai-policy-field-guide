**Nature of the documents.** Maryland pairs an **enacted statute** (SB 720, binding) with a **published statewide framework** (June 2026). The framework "establishes statewide expectations but does not approve or prohibit specific tools" and leaves tool selection to LEAs (MSDE-AI-Guidance-A.pdf, p.3), so it sets guardrails, not a tool list. The statute imposes concrete duties (district AI policy, a district AI coordinator, a higher-education certification role, a state collaborative). Claims are cited to the guidance by page and to the statute by provision.

## Executive Summary

Maryland is a mandate-plus-framework state with the clearest **named higher-education hook** in the set. SB 720, the Artificial Intelligence Ready Schools Act, requires every local school system to have an AI policy and to designate a **central-office (non-instructional) AI coordinator**, establishes a statewide **Maryland AI Education Collaborative**, and — most notably for higher education — provides that **"Morgan State University or another 4-year institution of higher education in the State shall support"** the certification and evaluation of AI tools for K-12 use (SB0720-enrolled.pdf, higher-education and coordinator provisions). The June 2026 MSDE framework operationalizes this with detailed guidance across privacy, equity, academic integrity, professional learning, student AI literacy, and procurement.

For a higher-education reader, three points stand out. First, a Maryland university is **statutorily tasked with certifying/evaluating the AI tools K-12 uses** — a direct, named role few states create. Second, Maryland **does not endorse AI-detection tools** as a primary integrity mechanism, calling them "unreliable and inequitable" (MSDE-AI-Guidance-A.pdf, p.7). Third, Maryland requires **AI-resilient assessment** that emphasizes "reasoning, analysis, reflection, and process over recall" (p.7). The framework is guardrail-setting rather than tool-prescribing, and per sources.md AI literacy must be in state standards by June 1, 2027.

## K–12 AI Policy and Guidance

### Primary themes and policy objectives
- **Human judgment central; AI enhances, not replaces:** "Artificial intelligence should enhance, not replace, the educators, counselors, and school leaders who remain Maryland's primary decision makers" (MSDE-AI-Guidance-A.pdf, p.2). *[Recommendation / framework expectation]*
- **AI literacy as a core skill** enabling learners to "assess reliability, identify bias, understand underlying assumptions" (p.2). *[Framework expectation]*
- **Statutory objective:** an "Artificial Intelligence Ready Schools Act" establishing guidelines, professional development, and a collaborative (SB0720-enrolled.pdf, title). *[Requirement]*
- **Alignment** with the Blueprint for Maryland's Future and statewide CS/digital-literacy efforts (MSDE-AI-Guidance-A.pdf, p.2). *[Framework]*

### Intended audiences and scope
- The framework is "intended for state leaders, LEA and school administrators, educators, students and families, and vendors" and "applies to all AI tools that interact with Maryland students, staff, or data" (MSDE-AI-Guidance-A.pdf, pp.2-3). *[Scope]*
- SB 720 binds MSDE, county boards, local school systems, and a designated higher-education institution (SB0720-enrolled.pdf). *[Scope, Requirement]*

### Permitted, restricted, and prohibited AI uses
- The framework "does not approve or prohibit specific tools" (MSDE-AI-Guidance-A.pdf, p.3); districts define permitted/prohibited uses locally. *[Framework — delegated]*
- **Data prohibitions:** "Collection of biometric, behavioral, or location data is prohibited unless legally required and educationally justified"; avoid entering PII into generative tools unless explicitly approved (p.5). *[Framework expectation]*
- **Vendor gate:** "Tools that fail to meet these standards must not be used" (p.4). *[Framework expectation]*
- **Statutory tool certification:** AI tools must be certified "consistent with certain State guidelines," supported by a designated 4-year higher-education institution (SB0720-enrolled.pdf, higher-education provision). *[Requirement]*

### Privacy, security, equity, accessibility, ethics, and academic integrity
- **Privacy/civil rights:** compliance with FERPA, COPPA, PPRA, and Maryland regulations; "Algorithm-assisted decisions remain subject to nondiscrimination requirements"; data minimization (MSDE-AI-Guidance-A.pdf, p.5). *[Framework expectation]*
- **Equity and Access** is a dedicated section (p.6). *[Framework]*
- **Academic integrity — detectors not endorsed:** "Maryland does not endorse AI-detection tools as a primary means of enforcing academic integrity, as research shows they are unreliable and inequitable (Liang et al., 2023)"; integrity supported "through instructional design, transparency, and human judgment," and concerns addressed "through instruction rather than punishment" (p.7). *[Framework expectation]*
- **Vendors** must meet transparency, privacy, accessibility, equity, and security standards, including fairness testing and audit participation (p.4). *[Framework expectation]*

### Teacher training, curriculum integration, assessment, and implementation
- **Assessment redesign:** "Assessment practices must evolve to emphasize reasoning, analysis, reflection, and process over recall. Tasks should require visible evidence of student thinking and reduce opportunities for AI substitution" (MSDE-AI-Guidance-A.pdf, p.7). *[Framework expectation]*
- **Professional learning** is role-specific (teachers, administrators, district leaders, specialists), delivered via the MSDE AI Hub, with core educator AI competencies aligned to UNESCO (2023) (p.8). *[Framework expectation]*
- **Student AI literacy and curriculum integration** is a dedicated section (p.10); SB 720 requires AI literacy standards and ties AI literacy to workforce preparation (SB0720-enrolled.pdf, literacy provisions). *[Requirement + framework]*
- **Statutory structures:** a district AI policy by the statutory date; a central-office AI coordinator per district; the Maryland AI Education Collaborative; PD for educators and school leaders including "basic artificial intelligence vocabulary" and applying AI to instruction (SB0720-enrolled.pdf). *[Requirement]*
- Per sources.md: AI literacy in state standards by June 1, 2027; coordinator designation required now. *[Requirement — dates from sources.md]*

## Learning and Cognitive-Offloading Strategies

- **Over-reliance named as a first-order risk** in the executive summary: AI introduces risks including "over-reliance on automated systems" (MSDE-AI-Guidance-A.pdf, p.2). *[Framework]*
- **AI-resilient assessment:** emphasize "reasoning, analysis, reflection, and process over recall," require "visible evidence of student thinking," and "reduce opportunities for AI substitution"; any use that "replaces essential skill development violates academic integrity" (p.7). *[Framework expectation]*
- **Educator over-trust caution:** professional learning must counter "the documented tendency to over-trust automated recommendations (Goddard et al., 2012)" and emphasize "critical evaluation, verification, and reflective practice" (p.8). *[Framework expectation]*
- **Student critical evaluation:** students "must learn to critically evaluate AI outputs, verify information, and understand AI limitations" (p.7). *[Framework expectation]*
- This is a substantive, research-cited learning-preservation posture, though not a single named cognitive-offloading section. *[Framework]*

## Expected Graduate AI Profile

- **Becoming a mandated baseline, but phasing in.** SB 720 is enacted, but AI literacy in state standards is due June 1, 2027 (sources.md), so near-term Maryland graduates predate a fully standardized baseline; district policies and coordinators are being stood up now.
- **Where implemented**, Maryland students may bring: AI-literacy skills (assess reliability, identify bias, understand assumptions, p.2); experience with disclosure-and-documentation of AI use and AI-resilient assessments (p.7); and a district AI policy governing permitted/prohibited use.
- **Higher-education implication:** Maryland is trending toward a genuinely standardized, statute-backed AI-literacy baseline. In a few years Maryland graduates may arrive with more uniform AI literacy than voluntary-guidance states; for now assume variance and phase-in.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** Maryland statutorily assigns a **named 4-year university (Morgan State or another in-state institution) to support certification and evaluation of the AI tools K-12 uses** (SB0720-enrolled.pdf). This is a direct, ongoing higher-education role in K-12 AI governance — Maryland institutions are not just downstream recipients but statutory participants.
- **Higher-education implication:** Maryland students may arrive taught that AI detectors are "unreliable and inequitable" (MSDE-AI-Guidance-A.pdf, p.7). Institutions relying on detectors as primary evidence will diverge from that experience.
- **Higher-education implication:** Maryland's AI-resilient assessment standard (reasoning/process over recall, visible thinking, p.7) previews what incoming students will expect assessment to look like; it is also a ready model for university course redesign.
- **Higher-education implication:** Maryland created a named, funded, non-teaching AI coordinator in every district (SB0720-enrolled.pdf; sources.md). An institution can ask whether it has an equivalent dedicated AI role or whether the work is spread across people who already have other jobs.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Governance and partnership:** Maryland institutions (especially Morgan State) should operationalize the statutory tool-certification role (SB0720-enrolled.pdf) and coordinate entry expectations with the K-12 pipeline; other institutions can adopt the named-coordinator model.
- **Academic integrity:** Follow Maryland's stance — do not use AI detectors as primary evidence; support integrity through instructional design, transparency, and human judgment, addressing concerns "through instruction rather than punishment" (MSDE-AI-Guidance-A.pdf, p.7).
- **Assessment design:** Adopt AI-resilient assessment emphasizing reasoning, reflection, and visible thinking (p.7).
- **AI tool availability and privacy:** Apply Maryland's vendor standards and data-minimization/biometric-prohibition expectations to institutional procurement (pp.4-5).
- **Faculty development:** Mirror Maryland's role-specific PD and core AI competencies (p.8) for faculty and staff.
- **Advancing learning rather than replacing thinking:** Center the enhance-not-replace vision and over-reliance cautions (pp.2, 8).

## Unique Aspects

- **A named higher-education institution (Morgan State University or another 4-year institution) statutorily tasked with certifying/evaluating K-12 AI tools** (SB0720-enrolled.pdf) — among the most concrete higher-ed-binding provisions in the set.
- **A mandated central-office, non-instructional AI coordinator in every local school system** (SB0720-enrolled.pdf).
- **A statewide Maryland AI Education Collaborative** established by statute.
- **An explicit non-endorsement of AI-detection tools** and an AI-resilient assessment standard (MSDE-AI-Guidance-A.pdf, p.7).
- **A comprehensive June 2026 framework** spanning roles, privacy, equity, integrity, PD, literacy, operations, and procurement, aligned to the Blueprint for Maryland's Future.
