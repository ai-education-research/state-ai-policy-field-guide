**The governing fact for DC.** There is **no OSSE (state education agency) AI guidance** in the provided files, and DC's own State Board resolution confirms the gap: it states "there is currently no comprehensive, citywide strategy for integrating Artificial Intelligence into the educational curriculum and operations within the District" (SR24-12-draft.pdf, p.1). The substantive AI rules in hand are **district-level** (the DCPS acceptable-use policy). The State Board resolution is a set of recommendations to DC agencies, not binding guidance, and the copy provided is a draft.

## Executive Summary

The District of Columbia has no citywide or state-agency AI framework. What exists is two-layered. At the **state-board level**, SR24-12 (draft) recommends that DC build a citywide AI strategy and, among other priorities, "establish a common student use policy that addresses ethical concerns, privacy, anonymity, and safety," calling on OSSE, DCPS, and the Deputy Mayor for Education to collaborate (SR24-12-draft.pdf, p.2). It is aspirational, not operative. At the **district level**, the DCPS Acceptable Use Policy (effective January 20, 2026) contains real, enforceable AI provisions inside a broader technology-conduct policy: AI must be used "as a supplement, not a substitute, for learning," with specific prohibitions on AI-enabled impersonation and on submitting AI-generated work as original or using AI on tests unless a teacher directs it (DCPS-AcceptableUse-SY25-26.pdf, pp.7-8).

For a higher-education reader, two cautions matter. First, the DCPS policy binds DCPS students only; a large share of DC students attend independent public charter LEAs not covered by it, so there is no uniform DC-wide student experience. Second, the DCPS AI content is a conduct-and-compliance rule set, not a pedagogy or AI-literacy framework, so it says more about what DCPS students were forbidden to do than about what AI skills they were taught.

## K–12 AI Policy and Guidance

### Primary themes and policy objectives
- **DCPS (district):** one of the AUP's four stated aims is to "Set expectations around the responsible use of artificial intelligence" (DCPS-AcceptableUse-SY25-26.pdf, p.1, Purpose and Scope). *[Requirement — within DCPS]*
- **DCPS principle:** "AI tools should be used by students and staff as a supplement, not a substitute, for learning" (p.7, Section IV.B.9). *[Requirement — within DCPS]*
- **State Board (draft):** recommends a citywide AI strategy, educator capacity-building, curricular integration with accessibility and inclusivity, a common student-use policy, and expanded infrastructure (SR24-12-draft.pdf, p.2). *[Recommendation — aspirational, draft]*

### Intended audiences and scope
- The DCPS AUP "applies to all DCPS students, staff, visitors, and others who use the DCPS network or DCPS devices" (DCPS-AcceptableUse-SY25-26.pdf, p.1). It does **not** bind DC's independent charter LEAs. *[Scope]*
- SR24-12 addresses DC agencies (OSSE, DCPS, Deputy Mayor for Education), not classrooms directly (SR24-12-draft.pdf, p.2). *[Scope]*

### Permitted, restricted, and prohibited AI uses (DCPS)
- **Permitted with conditions:** responsible, ethical, supplement-not-substitute use (p.7). *[Requirement — within DCPS]*
- **Specifically prohibited:**
  - "Bullying and Harassment: Students are prohibited from using AI in a way that could harm themselves or others. Using AI tools to manipulate media to impersonate others is prohibited" (p.7). *[Requirement]*
  - "Plagiarism and Cheating: Students are prohibited from submitting AI-generated work as their original work or using AI to answer test, exam, or other assignment questions (unless directed to do so by their teacher)" (p.8). *[Requirement]*
- **Enforcement:** disciplinary responses follow the Safe and Positive Schools Policy, "beginning with the least severe appropriate response," ranging "from providing an opportunity to re-submit the work, to grade reduction" (p.8). *[Requirement]*
- **Staff AI use:** DCPS "maintains a list of approved AI-enhanced tools for student learning" with PD; off-list tools require an AI Tool Approval Form meeting EdTech Team criteria; staff "must verify the truthfulness of any AI-produced content" and "review and critically reflect on all AI-generated content before use" (p.8). *[Requirement — for DCPS staff]*

### Privacy, security, equity, accessibility, ethics, and academic integrity
- **Procurement security review:** "any applications or websites procured by schools must undergo a full security review and receive approval by DCPS' Office of Data and Technology," especially systems containing student information (p.5). *[Requirement]*
- **Privacy law:** the AUP is grounded in CIPA, COPPA, and DC's Protecting Students Digital Privacy Act (pp.1-2, Authority and Applicable Law); staff data-sharing without approval "violates the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA)" (p.6). *[Requirement]*
- **Monitoring:** DCPS uses "a safety management system to analyze and review content found in online file storage, inbound and outbound DCPS email... and links to websites," and reserves broad monitoring rights (p.4). *[Requirement]*
- **Accessibility:** students with disabilities receive IEP-based accommodations to complete required Digital Citizenship courses, and assistive technology may go home per IEP teams (pp.6, 12). *[Requirement]*
- **Academic integrity:** handled via the plagiarism/cheating prohibition and disclosure-by-teacher-direction model (p.8). A position on AI-detection tools is **Not found in the provided documents.** *[Limited]*

### Teacher training, curriculum integration, assessment, and implementation
- **AI literacy delivery:** "DCPS will provide students with lessons on the benefits and risks of AI through Digital Citizenship and AI-specific courses" (p.7). Students complete Digital Citizenship & Literacy courses, sometimes required before device issuance (p.3). *[Requirement — within DCPS]*
- **PD:** DCPS "will provide professional development to support the appropriate deployment of these [approved AI] tools" (p.8). *[Requirement — within DCPS]*
- A detailed curriculum-integration framework (SAMR, grade-band literacy progression, assessment redesign) is **Not found in the provided documents.** The AUP is a conduct policy, not a curriculum framework. *[Limited]*

## Learning and Cognitive-Offloading Strategies

- The only relevant provision is the DCPS principle that "AI tools should be used... as a supplement, not a substitute, for learning" (DCPS-AcceptableUse-SY25-26.pdf, p.7), reinforced by the prohibition on using AI to answer test/assignment questions unless teacher-directed (p.8). These guard against wholesale offloading but do not constitute a developed cognitive-science strategy. *[Requirement / limited]*
- A treatment of metacognition, productive struggle, or preserving independent reasoning is **Not found in the provided documents.** *[Evidence gap]*

## Expected Graduate AI Profile

- **DCPS graduates:** likely experienced AI framed as a compliance-and-conduct matter — responsible-use expectations, Digital Citizenship and "AI-specific courses" (p.7), and clear prohibitions on AI impersonation and on using AI for test answers without teacher permission (pp.7-8). Their AI use was governed, but the policy describes little about AI-creation skills or literacy depth. *Basis: DCPS AUP pp.7-8.*
- **Non-DCPS (charter) graduates:** governed by their own LEA policies, which are **Not found in the provided documents**; their experience cannot be characterized here. *[Evidence gap]*
- **Higher-education implication:** For DC-origin students, assume no uniform baseline. DCPS graduates may arrive with a conduct-oriented understanding of AI ("cite it, do not impersonate, do not use it on tests unless allowed") rather than a literacy-oriented or instrument-based one, and charter-origin students may vary entirely.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** DCPS students may arrive expecting a teacher-permission model for AI on assessments (AI on tests is barred "unless directed... by their teacher," p.8). A college syllabus that specifies AI permission per assignment will feel continuous; a silent syllabus will not.
- **Higher-education implication:** DCPS frames AI within an acceptable-use and disciplinary structure, so some DC students may associate AI misuse primarily with conduct consequences (grade reduction, discipline) rather than with academic-integrity adjudication. Institutions should make their own AI-integrity process explicit.
- **Higher-education implication:** DC's fragmentation (a district policy plus many independent charter LEAs, and no citywide strategy per SR24-12) means DC-origin cohorts will be especially heterogeneous. Do not treat "from DC" as a single policy background.
- **Higher-education implication:** SR24-12's call to "establish a common student use policy" (p.2) signals DC intends to move toward a citywide standard. Institutions enrolling many DC students should watch for that policy, since it would create the shared baseline that does not yet exist.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Onboarding:** Given DC's fragmentation and the absence of a citywide framework, run an explicit AI-literacy orientation and do not assume prior instrument exposure.
- **Assessment design:** Adopt clear per-assignment AI-permission language continuous with the DCPS teacher-permission model (p.8).
- **Academic integrity:** Define an explicit AI-integrity process; DCPS students may have experienced AI issues as conduct/discipline rather than integrity adjudication.
- **Procurement and governance:** DCPS's Office of Data and Technology security-review requirement (p.5) is a usable model for institutional AI-tool vetting, especially for tools touching student data.
- **AI tool availability and accessibility:** Provide sanctioned tools with IEP-style accessibility support, echoing the DCPS accommodations model (pp.6, 12).
- **Advancing learning rather than replacing thinking:** Build on DCPS's "supplement, not substitute" principle (p.7) with an explicit learning-preservation model the district policy itself does not supply.

## Unique Aspects

- **A state-board resolution that documents its own jurisdiction's absence of strategy** (SR24-12-draft.pdf, p.1) and calls for a common citywide student-use policy (p.2) — an unusually candid "we don't have this yet" state-level artifact.
- **A district AUP that names AI explicitly** — including a definition (p.2), a supplement-not-substitute principle, AI-impersonation and AI-on-tests prohibitions, and a staff AI-tool approval process (pp.7-8) — inside a broader conduct policy effective January 2026.
- **A strong procurement security-review gate** through the DCPS Office of Data and Technology (p.5).
