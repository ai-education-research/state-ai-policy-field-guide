## Executive Summary

Louisiana's guide is a conventional but instrument-bearing document. Its centerpiece for a higher-education reader is the **AI Integration Tiered Approach** (LDOE-AI-Guidance.pdf, p.7): a four-tier, assignment-level student-use scale running from **AI-Prohibited** (no AI, for tasks assessing core knowledge) through **AI-Assisted** (AI as a feedback/brainstorming tool, student creates all content) and **AI-Enhanced** (AI helps transform the task) to **AI-Empowered** (full engagement under supervision, redefining the task). Each tier is tied to a SAMR component. This is a genuine, granular student-use instrument.

Two other features stand out. First, Louisiana provides a **four-component cyclical governance framework** — Purpose & Research, Policy & Guidance, Engage Stakeholders, Evaluation & Monitoring (p.8). Second, and notably distinct from most states in this set, Louisiana **recommends using plagiarism/AI-detection tools** (Turnitin, Grammarly, GoGuardian lockdown browsers) as part of its academic-integrity strategy (p.5), rather than cautioning against them. The guide is voluntary, references the Louisiana Student Privacy Law (R.S. 17:3914, p.9), and has no explicit higher-education hook.

## K–12 AI Policy and Guidance

### Primary themes and policy objectives
- **AI as a supportive tool, not a replacement:** "AI is most effective as a supportive tool that empowers educators rather than serving as a replacement for their expertise" (LDOE-AI-Guidance.pdf, p.3). *[Recommendation]*
- **Five Guiding Principles** (p.2): Data Privacy and Security; Ethical Considerations; Student-Centered Learning ("promote agency and ownership over learning"); Transparency and Explainability; Professional Development. *[Recommendation]*
- **Shared responsibility** for safe use among educators, leaders, families, students, and external stakeholders (p.3). *[Recommendation]*

### Intended audiences and scope
- Addresses "school system leaders, school leaders, teachers and staff members, educational technology innovators, policymakers, and researchers," plus families (LDOE-AI-Guidance.pdf, pp.2-3). *[Scope]*

### Permitted, restricted, and prohibited AI uses — the AI Integration Tiered Approach (p.7)
A four-tier, assignment-level student-use scale, each tier mapped to SAMR:
- **AI-Prohibited (Substitution):** "AI tools are not permitted for assignments or tasks specifically designed to evaluate students' core knowledge and skills." Example: deliver a slideshow without AI. *[Recommendation / instrument level]*
- **AI-Assisted (Augmentation):** "Students may use AI as a feedback tool, but they should create all of the assignment's content themselves." Example: AI as a brainstorming thought partner. *[Instrument level]*
- **AI-Enhanced (Modification):** "Students may use AI as a tool to transform the learning process, but sources should be cited, and all generated content should be reviewed for accuracy." Example: AI to analyze data for a science report. *[Instrument level]*
- **AI-Empowered (Redefinition):** "Students are encouraged to fully engage with AI under human supervision, citing sources and carefully reviewing all generated content for accuracy." Example: AI-powered design software to create 3D models. *[Instrument level]*

### Privacy, security, equity, accessibility, ethics, and academic integrity
- **Academic integrity — recommends detection tools (distinctive):** the plagiarism/cheating strategies (p.5) include "Plagiarism Detection Software: Use plagiarism detection tools like Turnitin or Grammarly," "Lockdown Browsers: Implement software such as GoGuardian," version control, plus assessment redesign (embedded, personalized, open-ended, project-based assessments) and classroom management (screen-visible device orientation, regular check-ins). *[Recommendation — note: Louisiana endorses detection tools, unlike many states that caution against them]*
- **Privacy:** references the Louisiana Student Privacy Law (R.S. 17:3914) alongside safeguards at system and classroom level (pp.9-10). *[Recommendation]*
- **Ethics/bias:** Ethical Considerations principle covers fairness and avoiding algorithmic bias (p.2). *[Recommendation]*
- **Accessibility:** Section 504 accommodations referenced (grep line 473); a fuller accessibility framework is limited in the captured text. *[Limited]*

### Teacher training, curriculum integration, assessment, and implementation
- **SAMR Model** (p.6) as the conceptual basis for integration, adapted from Puentedura & Duckworth (2010). *[Instructional guidance]*
- **LDOE Framework for AI Integration** (p.8): a four-component cyclical process — Purpose & Research, Policy & Guidance, Engage Stakeholders, and Evaluation & Monitoring. *[Recommendation / governance framework]*
- **Safeguards** at School System Level and School Building/Classroom Level, including software approval and monitoring (pp.9-10). *[Recommendation]*
- **Professional Development** is a guiding principle (p.2); companion tools include the C.R.E.A.T.E. prompting framework and a Pre-Work Checklist (folder files, not analyzed in depth). *[Recommendation]*

## Learning and Cognitive-Offloading Strategies

- The tiered model's **AI-Prohibited** level explicitly preserves unaided demonstration: AI is "not permitted for assignments or tasks specifically designed to evaluate students' core knowledge and skills" (LDOE-AI-Guidance.pdf, p.7). *[Instrument level]*
- The **Student-Centered Learning** principle promotes "agency and ownership over learning" (p.2). *[Recommendation]*
- **Assessment-design strategies** to make offloading harder: open-ended questions requiring critical thinking, personalized assignments, and project-based assessments (p.5). *[Recommendation]*
- AI framed as supportive, not a replacement for educator expertise (p.3). *[Recommendation]*
- A developed cognitive-science treatment (metacognition, over-reliance research) is **Not found in the provided documents**; Louisiana's guard is the tiered scale plus assessment redesign. *[Limited]*

## Expected Graduate AI Profile

- **Uneven by district (voluntary).** Exposure depends on district adoption. *Basis: pp.1-2 disclaimer.*
- **Where adopted**, Louisiana students may bring: familiarity with a four-tier, per-assignment AI-permission scale mapped to SAMR (p.7); a citation-and-review habit for AI-generated content (p.7); and experience with detection tools and lockdown browsers during assessments (p.5).
- **Higher-education implication:** Louisiana-shaped students may arrive already understanding assignment-level AI tiers and expecting that some tasks are strictly no-AI. They may also be accustomed to detection software and proctoring-style monitoring, unlike students from states that reject those tools.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** Louisiana's tiered scale (p.7) is exactly the assignment-level granularity most university syllabi lack. A faculty member can adopt the four tiers (AI-Prohibited → AI-Assisted → AI-Enhanced → AI-Empowered) and expect partial prior familiarity from Louisiana students, with the SAMR mapping giving a shared conceptual anchor.
- **Higher-education implication:** Louisiana **recommends AI/plagiarism-detection tools** (p.5), the opposite of several states in this set. Louisiana students may arrive expecting detection and lockdown browsers as normal, whereas students from Arizona/Indiana/Illinois/California were taught those tools are unreliable. This is a real cross-state divergence institutions should anticipate.
- **Higher-education implication:** The SAMR framing (p.6) is already familiar in higher education, so Louisiana's model translates cleanly to college course design.
- **Higher-education implication:** No explicit higher-education hook exists in the document; Louisiana's relevance is via the transferable tiered instrument, not a binding of institutions.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Assessment design:** Adopt the four-tier, SAMR-aligned AI-permission scale (p.7) per assignment; preserve AI-Prohibited tasks where the goal is unaided demonstration of core skills.
- **Academic integrity:** Decide deliberately where the institution stands on detection tools — Louisiana students may expect them, but weigh the reliability and bias concerns other states raise. Favor Louisiana's assessment-redesign strategies (open-ended, project-based, personalized) as the more durable approach (p.5).
- **Governance:** Reuse the four-component cyclical framework (Purpose & Research, Policy & Guidance, Engage Stakeholders, Evaluation & Monitoring, p.8) as an institutional AI-governance cycle.
- **Onboarding:** Use the tiered vocabulary for continuity; run an entry check given voluntariness.
- **Advancing learning rather than replacing thinking:** Center the Student-Centered Learning principle (agency and ownership, p.2) and the AI-Prohibited tier for core-skill assessment.

## Unique Aspects

- **A single SAMR-aligned, assignment-level student-use scale** — AI-Empowered/Enhanced/Assisted/Prohibited mapped to Redefinition/Modification/Augmentation/Substitution (LDOE-AI-Guidance.pdf, p.7). Granular and directly syllabus-portable.
- **An explicit endorsement of detection tools** (Turnitin, Grammarly, GoGuardian, p.5) — distinctive against the anti-detector trend in several other states.
- **A four-component cyclical governance framework** (Purpose & Research, Policy & Guidance, Engage Stakeholders, Evaluation & Monitoring, p.8).
- **Companion instruments:** the C.R.E.A.T.E. prompting framework and a Pre-Work Checklist (folder files).
- **State-specific privacy grounding** in R.S. 17:3914 (p.9).
