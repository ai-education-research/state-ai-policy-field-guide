**Nature of the source.** Idaho is a **statute-first** jurisdiction. SB 1227 is enacted and binding, but it is an enabling law that **directs the creation of the substantive materials** (a statewide framework, literacy standards, assessments, professional development, and a parent-guidance document) rather than containing them. Those materials were **not yet published** as of the provided files (sources.md). So the requirements below are real (Requirement-level), but the actual framework content students would experience is **Not found in the provided documents**.

## Executive Summary

Idaho converted AI-in-education from suggestion into statutory duty. SB 1227 adds a new Chapter 70 to Title 33 of the Idaho Code and requires the State Department of Education (SDE), with State Board approval, to build a statewide "generative artificial intelligence in education framework," and requires **every local school district and public charter school to adopt a policy** governing student and employee GenAI use (S1227-enacted.pdf, §§33-7003, 33-7004). It further directs the SDE to develop **GenAI literacy standards for K-12 students, assessment guidelines to evaluate student understanding of GenAI, and a professional development plan** (§33-7005), plus a parent-guidance document (§33-7006) and procurement requirements (§33-7007).

For a higher-education reader, three points stand out. First, Idaho is one of the few states to statutorily require **assessment of student AI understanding** (§33-7005), which could mean future Idaho graduates arrive having been formally evaluated on AI literacy. Second, the statute repeatedly centers human authority — "human judgment remains the final authority" (§33-7002). Third, the operative content does not yet exist, so near-term Idaho graduates predate any guaranteed baseline; the substance is pending SDE development.

## K–12 AI Policy and Guidance

### Primary themes and policy objectives
- **Legislative intent (§33-7001):** "safeguard student privacy, protect the role of the educator, promote human-centered learning, increase transparency, and prepare Idaho students for success in an economy increasingly influenced by generative artificial intelligence," ensuring "responsible, ethical, safe, and effective use." *[Requirement — statutory intent]*
- **Human authority as final:** "generative artificial intelligence in education" is defined as responsible use "while ensuring that human judgment remains the final authority" (§33-7002(2)). *[Requirement — definitional]*

### Intended audiences and scope
- Applies to K-12 Idaho public schools and public charter schools; governs "students and employees in school buildings, on school grounds, during school activities, and on school-issued devices" (S1227-enacted.pdf, §33-7004). *[Scope, Requirement]*

### Permitted, restricted, and prohibited AI uses
- The statute does not itself list permitted/prohibited uses; it **requires each district/charter policy to "Define appropriate and prohibited uses of generative AI for instructional, administrative, and communication purposes"** (§33-7004(2)). *[Requirement — delegated to local policy]*
- The specific appropriate/prohibited-use definitions are therefore **Not found in the provided documents** (they will live in local policies aligned to the forthcoming framework). *[Unresolved]*

### Privacy, security, equity, accessibility, ethics, and academic integrity
- **Privacy/security:** district policies must include "safeguards for student privacy, data security, accessibility, and academic integrity" and comply with Idaho student data privacy law, FERPA, CIPA, and COPPA (§33-7004(3)-(4)). Procurement requires vendor disclosure of ML/predictive/generative features and "assurances regarding data protection, algorithmic transparency, and responsible use" (§33-7007). *[Requirement]*
- **Accessibility:** the framework must "Address accessibility, accommodations, and access to generative AI tools" (§33-7003(1)(c)). *[Requirement — framework content pending]*
- **Academic integrity and digital citizenship:** the framework must "Provide guidance on instructional integration, academic integrity, digital citizenship, and responsible student use" (§33-7003(1)(b)). *[Requirement — content pending]*
- A specific position on AI detection tools is **Not found in the provided documents.** *[Limited]*

### Teacher training, curriculum integration, assessment, and implementation
- **Literacy standards + assessments (notable):** SDE must develop "Generative AI literacy standards for K-12 students" covering "what generative AI is, how it works, its appropriate and age-appropriate uses, and how to use it ethically, securely, and transparently," plus "Assessment guidelines to evaluate student understanding of generative AI concepts, ethics, and responsible use" (§33-7005(1)-(2)). *[Requirement — content pending]*
- **Professional development plan** to build educator capacity (§33-7005(3)). *[Requirement — content pending]*
- **Parental engagement:** SDE must develop a GenAI guidance document for parents/guardians (§33-7006). *[Requirement — content pending]*
- **Framework as foundation:** the framework serves as "the foundation for local school district and public charter school policies, professional development, procurement practices, and statewide standards" (§33-7003(1)(d)). *[Requirement]*

## Learning and Cognitive-Offloading Strategies

- The statute's learning-preservation posture is expressed through **human-centered authority**: "human judgment remains the final authority" (S1227-enacted.pdf, §33-7002(2)), and legislative intent to "protect the role of the educator" and "promote human-centered learning" (§33-7001). *[Requirement — intent]*
- The framework must address "academic integrity... and responsible student use" (§33-7003(1)(b)), and literacy standards must cover "appropriate and age-appropriate uses" (§33-7005(1)) — but the detailed cognitive-offloading strategy is delegated to the not-yet-published framework and is **Not found in the provided documents.** *[Unresolved]*

## Expected Graduate AI Profile

- **Near-term graduates predate any baseline.** The framework, standards, and assessments are not yet published; district policies must align to a framework that does not yet exist. Per sources.md, classroom implementation "could begin as early as 2027-28." *Basis: §§33-7003 to 33-7005; sources.md.*
- **Future graduates (post-implementation)** should, by statute, have: been governed by a district policy defining appropriate and prohibited AI uses (§33-7004); been taught to GenAI literacy standards (§33-7005(1)); and potentially been **assessed on their understanding of GenAI concepts, ethics, and responsible use** (§33-7005(2)). *Basis: statute; content pending.*
- **Higher-education implication:** Idaho is a "watch this space" state. In a few years its graduates may arrive with statutorily standardized, and even assessed, AI literacy — a stronger and more uniform baseline than voluntary-guidance states. For now, assume no guaranteed baseline.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** Idaho drew a distinction higher education has largely avoided — a policy on file is not the same as a governance function that runs. The statute requires operational governance (district policies, standards, assessments, PD, procurement rules), not just aspirational guidance (§§33-7003 to 33-7007). Institutions can borrow that "governance that runs" framing.
- **Higher-education implication:** If Idaho implements assessment of student AI understanding (§33-7005(2)), future Idaho applicants may have documented AI-literacy outcomes. Admissions and placement could eventually treat AI literacy as a measured competency rather than an assumption.
- **Higher-education implication:** The statute contains **no explicit higher-education hook** (no naming of universities or teacher-prep programs) in the provided text. Idaho's relevance to higher education is via the future graduate profile, not a direct binding of institutions.
- **Higher-education implication:** Because the substantive framework is pending, institutions should not yet assume any specific Idaho student AI competency; the guaranteed baseline is a future-tense phenomenon.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Governance:** Adopt Idaho's "governance that runs" model — pair any institutional AI policy with defined roles, standards, PD, and procurement rules rather than a standalone statement (mirrors §§33-7003 to 33-7007).
- **Onboarding:** Treat current Idaho cohorts as pre-baseline; run an AI-literacy entry check. Revisit this once Idaho's standards and assessments are implemented.
- **Assessment design and academic integrity:** Provide explicit appropriate/prohibited-use definitions per assignment, mirroring what Idaho districts will be required to define (§33-7004(2)).
- **Procurement:** Reuse Idaho's vendor-disclosure requirements (ML/predictive/generative disclosure, data protection, algorithmic transparency, §33-7007) as institutional AI-procurement criteria.
- **Monitoring:** Track publication of Idaho's SDE framework, literacy standards, and assessment guidelines; when released, align entry expectations to them.

## Unique Aspects

- **A statutory duty, not guidance:** SB 1227 makes AI policy a legal requirement for every district and charter (§33-7004), one of the first states to do so.
- **Mandated assessment of student AI understanding** (§33-7005(2)) — an unusually strong provision requiring the state to develop guidelines to *evaluate* student GenAI literacy, not just teach it.
- **A statutory definition centering human authority** — "human judgment remains the final authority" (§33-7002(2)).
- **Emergency-clause enactment** effective July 1, 2026 (§Section 2), signaling urgency.
- **A required parent-guidance document** (§33-7006) as a distinct deliverable.
