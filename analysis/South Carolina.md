**Nature of the sources (read before everything below):** South Carolina has **no SCDE classroom AI-use guidance** in the record, and none was found. The one downloaded document, the "AI Standards Framework," is a **Career and Technical Education course-content standards document**, it specifies what students learn in AI/ML technical coursework (computing systems, data analytics, algorithms and programming), not how AI may be used across classrooms. A tracker could easily miscount this as South Carolina having an AI-use framework; it is not one. The two bills (H.5253, H.4582) are **introduced, not enacted.** **Nothing in the record is a current statewide classroom AI-use mandate or use-guidance framework.** Bill provisions below are tagged pending.

## Executive Summary

South Carolina has issued no classroom AI-use guidance. What exists is a CTE AI Standards Framework, a technical curriculum for teaching AI and machine learning as subject matter, and two pending bills. Per sources.md and news reporting, SCDE plans an internal AI framework with U.S. Digital Response through a 12-week process, but that is planned, not published.

The CTE framework is worth reading carefully precisely so it is not misread. It lists process standards ("foster an inclusive computing culture," "design solutions for privacy") and content themes (Computing Systems, Data and Analytics, Algorithms and Programming, Impacts of Computing, Security and Privacy), each broken into four proficiency levels (Level 1 through Level 4) that describe deepening technical mastery, for example progressing from identifying hardware/software in AI solutions to designing "domain-specific" AI and ML solutions (framework pp.1-2). These "Levels" are skill-proficiency tiers for learning to build AI, not AI-use permission levels for students across assignments.

For a higher-education reader, the operative facts are two. First, South Carolina set no state AI-use scale, prohibition list, or student-use vocabulary, so a graduate's classroom AI norms came from the district. Second, students who took the CTE AI course sequence may arrive with genuine technical AI/ML skills (Python, model building, data structures), a narrow but real capability distinct from general AI literacy.

## K–12 AI Policy and Guidance

### Primary themes and policy objectives
- **No classroom AI-use policy exists.** SCDE has published no framework governing how students and teachers may use AI across the curriculum. *[Not found in the provided documents]*
- **The one published framework is a technical curriculum**, whose objectives are computational, students "analyze the utilization of computing devices and investigate advancements in AI" and "evaluate the evolving legal and ethical tradeoffs that shape computing and AI practices" (framework p.2). *[CTE course standard]*
- **Pending legislation signals two directions**, guardrails on AI use (H.5253) and permission for AI electives (H.4582), both in committee. *[Pending, not enacted]*

### Intended audiences and scope
- The CTE framework's audience is students and teachers in AI/ML career-and-technical coursework, not the general classroom (framework pp.1-2). *[Scope statement]*
- No general K-12 AI-use audience or scope has been established by SCDE. *[Not found in the provided documents]*

### Permitted, restricted, and prohibited AI uses
- **There is no statewide permission scale and no statewide prohibition list.** The CTE framework's Level 1-4 tiers are technical proficiency levels, not AI-use permission levels (framework p.2). *[Not found in the provided documents]*
- **H.5253, if enacted, would create restrictions.** Per sources.md, it would add Section 59-28-195 requiring opt-in written parental consent for student AI use, barring AI from replacing "licensed teachers in core instruction or final grading," and requiring annual public disclosure of AI tools and data practices. This is pending. *[Pending requirement, if enacted]*
- **H.4582, if enacted, would permit AI electives** from 2026-27 and require SCDE implementation guidance (sources.md). This is pending. *[Pending, if enacted]*

### Privacy, security, equity, accessibility, ethics, and academic integrity
- Not found in the provided documents for general classroom use. The CTE framework touches privacy and security only as technical topics, students "research security and privacy protocols associated with AI and ML solutions" (framework p.2). Federal law (FERPA, COPPA) applies to South Carolina districts, but no SCDE AI-use guidance restates or extends it in the record. *[Unresolved]*
- H.5253's parental-consent and disclosure provisions would touch privacy if enacted, but they are pending (sources.md). *[Pending]*

### Teacher training, curriculum integration, assessment, and implementation
- **Curriculum integration exists only as CTE subject-matter standards** (framework pp.1-15), not as guidance for embedding AI use across the curriculum. *[CTE course standard]*
- **Implementation guidance is planned, not published.** SCDE reportedly plans an internal framework with U.S. Digital Response via a 12-week process (sources.md), and H.4582 would require SCDE implementation guidance if enacted. *[Planned / pending]*
- **Professional-development funding** would be authorized under H.4582 if enacted (sources.md). *[Pending]*

## Expected Graduate AI Profile

Because there is no state classroom AI-use guidance, no state-shaped general-use profile can be described.

- **A South Carolina graduate's classroom AI norms were set at the district level**, with no state scale or vocabulary. *Basis: confirmed absence of SCDE classroom guidance, sources.md.*
- **Students who completed the CTE AI course sequence may bring real technical skills**, coding in Python/R/MATLAB, building computational models on large data sets, and designing AI/ML solutions (framework pp.2, Computing Systems levels). This is narrow, elective-based technical capability, not general AI literacy. *Basis: framework pp.1-2, CTE standards.*
- **Higher-education implication:** Treat general AI-use exposure as unknown and district-specific. The CTE cohort is a distinct minority with genuine technical AI skills; do not generalize their capability to all South Carolina graduates, and do not assume the CTE "Levels" mean students learned an AI-use permission scale.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** South Carolina is, for general classroom AI use, a no-state-guidance jurisdiction. A faculty member cannot assume any statewide shared AI-use vocabulary, permission scale, or integrity norm, because SCDE authored none.
- **Higher-education implication:** A subset of incoming students, CTE AI-course completers, may have substantive technical AI/ML skills (framework pp.1-2). Faculty in computing, data science, and engineering may find these students notably prepared, while their peers had no comparable exposure. The variance is wide and course-driven.
- **Higher-education implication:** South Carolina's near-term direction is uncertain and pending. H.5253 (guardrails, parental opt-in) and H.4582 (AI electives) plus a planned SCDE framework could establish a baseline soon (sources.md). Entry assumptions should be treated as provisional.
- **Higher-education implication:** If H.5253 were enacted, incoming students would have grown up under an *opt-in parental-consent* regime and a bar on AI in "final grading" (sources.md), which could shape their expectations about consent and human grading. This is speculative and pending.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Onboarding:** Assume no state general-use baseline. Give incoming South Carolina students an explicit AI-use orientation and a short literacy diagnostic.
- **Differentiate the CTE cohort:** In technical programs, screen for CTE AI-course completers who may place beyond an introductory AI unit (framework pp.1-2), while providing a general AI-literacy floor for everyone else.
- **Assessment design:** State AI permission explicitly per assignment; South Carolina students share no statewide scale or vocabulary.
- **Academic-integrity processes:** Set integrity expectations from first principles; South Carolina established no statewide stance on AI detectors or disclosure.
- **Monitor pending legislation and the planned SCDE framework:** Track H.5253, H.4582, and the SCDE/U.S. Digital Response process (sources.md); any could establish a baseline that changes entry assumptions.

## Unique Aspects

- **A CTE course-standards framework that is easily mistaken for use guidance.** South Carolina's only published AI framework teaches AI as a technical subject, with Level 1-4 *proficiency* tiers, not AI-*use* permission levels (framework pp.1-2). Correctly classifying it is the key analytic point.
- **A planned-not-published posture.** SCDE reportedly intends an internal AI framework via a 12-week U.S. Digital Response process (sources.md), so South Carolina is mid-development rather than silent.
- **Two divergent pending bills.** H.5253 leans restrictive (parental opt-in, no AI in final grading), while H.4582 leans permissive (authorizing AI electives), both in the House Committee on Education and Public Works (sources.md).
