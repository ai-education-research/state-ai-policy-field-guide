**Nature of the document (read before everything below):** The WDE guidance is a voluntary policy-development aid for districts, not a rulebook. It helps districts write their own AI use policies and offers guiding questions throughout. **Nothing in it is a statewide mandate.** Every item below is a Recommendation, Instructional guidance, or Example unless explicitly noted. Its central instrument, the Student AI Usage Continuum (p.7), is an adaptable example, and it carries verbatim Perkins et al. AIAS attribution.

## Executive Summary

Wyoming's guidance is a compact (9-page), recommendation-level policy-development guide built on two borrowed structures. Its principles come from the TeachAI toolkit, seven named principles: Purpose, Compliance, Knowledge, Balance, Integrity, Agency, and Evaluation (guidance p.~149). Its student-use instrument, the "Student AI Usage Continuum for Empowered Learning" (p.7), is adapted from the Perkins et al. AI Assessment Scale, with the attribution line "As adapted from: Dr. Leon Furze, Dr. Mike Perkins, Dr. Jasper Roe FHEA, & Dr. Jason Mcvaugh" (guidance p.7). Definitions are adapted from Washington OSPI's Human-Centered AI Guidance v1.0.

The continuum has four named levels, AI FREE, AI ASSISTED, AI ENHANCED, and AI EMPOWERED, each with disclosure requirements that escalate with AI involvement (guidance p.7). Notably, at the AI ASSISTED level and above, students must submit "Disclosure statement & links to AI Chats... with final product," a granular transparency mechanism Wyoming shares with Oklahoma. This is one of several signs that Wyoming and Oklahoma drew from a common intermediate source: both misspell "MacVaugh" as "Mcvaugh" in their AIAS attribution.

For a higher-education reader, Wyoming is the third clearly confirmed AIAS-lineage state in the dataset (with Oklahoma and New Mexico, plus North Carolina), directly supporting the project's Perkins et al. framing. It also carries a concrete higher-education hook: the recommended district AI-policy committee includes a "Local College Representative" (guidance p.~141), and the Knowledge principle names "preparing students for college" (guidance p.~214).

## K–12 AI Policy and Guidance

### Primary themes and policy objectives
- **Seven TeachAI-derived guiding principles** anchor the guidance (guidance p.~149): Purpose (use AI ethically to help all students), Compliance (reaffirm existing policies and federal law), Knowledge (promote AI literacy), Balance (realize benefits, address risks), Integrity (advance academic integrity), Agency (maintain human decision-making), and Evaluation (regularly assess AI's impact). *[Recommendation]*
- **Human decision-making is preserved.** The Agency principle keeps "human decision-making when using AI" and asks how guidance "ensures that students retain appropriate agency" (guidance pp.~156, 261). *[Recommendation]*
- **College and workforce preparation** frames AI literacy under the Knowledge principle (guidance p.~214). *[Recommendation]*

### Intended audiences and scope
- The guidance is written for Wyoming school districts developing AI use policy, with a recommended advisory committee that includes a "Local College Representative" (guidance p.~141). *[Scope statement]*
- Scope is K-12 policy development, using guiding questions rather than mandates. *[Scope statement]*

### Permitted, restricted, and prohibited AI uses
- **The Student AI Usage Continuum (p.7) is the permission instrument**, an adaptable four-level example:
  - **AI FREE** — "Work must be completed entirely without any AI assistance. Students must rely entirely on their own knowledge, understanding, and skills. Any AI use is a violation of student academic integrity. An academic honesty pledge that AI was not used may be required." *[Example / instrument]*
  - **AI ASSISTED** — "AI is used for tasks as specified such as brainstorming, planning, or feedback. No AI content is allowed in the final submission. Usage beyond specified tasks is a violation of academic integrity. Disclosure statement & links to AI Chats should be submitted with final product." *[Example]*
  - **AI ENHANCED** — "AI is used interactively throughout... Student must provide human oversight and evaluation of all AI generated content... Student is responsible for the accuracy and [fairness] of all AI-generated content. Detailed disclosure statement and links to AI Chats." *[Example]*
  - **AI EMPOWERED** — full integration "enabling previously impossible creations," student responsible for accuracy, fairness, and originality, with detailed disclosure and chat links (guidance p.7). *[Example]*
- The continuum is offered as a model for districts to adopt or adapt, not a state rule. *[Recommendation]*

### Privacy, security, equity, accessibility, ethics, and academic integrity
- **Compliance is a full principle.** Key policy areas are "privacy," compliance with federal law, and existing procedures (guidance p.~176). *[Recommendation]*
- **Academic integrity is a principle and a continuum feature.** The Integrity principle addresses "plagiarism, and proper attribution issues," and every continuum level defines its integrity boundary (guidance pp.~228, 238, 7). *[Recommendation]*
- **Equity and ethics** appear under Knowledge, "safe, equitable, and ethical use of AI" (guidance p.~214). *[Recommendation]*
- **Position on AI detection tools:** the 9-page guidance does not center AI detectors; its integrity approach is continuum- and disclosure-based (chat links, honesty pledges) rather than detection-based (guidance p.7). *[Not emphasized]*

### Teacher training, curriculum integration, assessment, and implementation
- **A recommended district AI-policy committee** structures implementation and includes a Local College Representative (guidance p.~141). *[Recommendation]*
- **Guiding questions drive policy development** under each principle (e.g., "How does our guidance highlight the purposeful use of AI to achieve our shared education vision?", guidance p.~168). *[Recommendation]*
- **The continuum doubles as an assessment tool**, letting teachers set the permitted AI level per assignment with matching disclosure (guidance p.7). *[Instrument]*
- **Evaluation is a standing principle**, "regularly assess the impacts of AI" (guidance p.~157). *[Recommendation]*

## Learning and Cognitive-Offloading Strategies

Wyoming's offloading protection is built into the continuum's design and the Agency principle.

- **AI FREE preserves unaided cognition** by definition: students "must rely entirely on their own knowledge, understanding, and skills," and any AI use is an integrity violation (guidance p.7). *[Example / instrument]*
- **Escalating human oversight across levels.** From AI ENHANCED up, students "must provide human oversight and evaluation of all AI generated content" and are "responsible for the accuracy and [fairness]" of it (guidance p.7). *[Example]*
- **The Agency principle keeps humans deciding.** It maintains "human decision-making when using AI" and asks how students "retain appropriate agency" (guidance pp.~156, 261). *[Recommendation]*
- **Disclosure and chat-link submission** make AI use visible and accountable at every level above AI FREE (guidance p.7). *[Instrument]*
- Wyoming does not include a dedicated cognitive-offloading section or grade-band gating; its offloading control is the leveled continuum plus the Agency principle. *[Structural note]*

## Expected Graduate AI Profile

These describe habits a Wyoming graduate *may* bring. Because the guidance is voluntary and district-implemented, exposure varies.

- **Familiarity with a four-level AI-use continuum**, from AI FREE to AI EMPOWERED, where the district adopted it (guidance p.7). *Basis: guidance p.7, adaptable instrument.*
- **A strong disclosure-and-documentation habit**, specifically submitting links to AI chats with work above AI FREE (guidance p.7). Wyoming shares this with Oklahoma. *Basis: guidance p.7.*
- **A human-oversight-and-accountability orientation** at higher levels (guidance p.7). *Basis: guidance p.7.*
- **A college-and-career framing** of AI literacy under the Knowledge principle (guidance p.~214). *Basis: guidance p.~214.*
- **Higher-education implication:** Treat these as ceilings of possibility, not guarantees. Where the continuum was adopted, Wyoming students may arrive with transferable per-assignment AIAS-style vocabulary and a chat-link disclosure habit, but adoption is voluntary and district-dependent.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** Wyoming's continuum shares its lineage with the peer-reviewed AIAS (Furze, Perkins, Roe, MacVaugh; guidance p.7). A university adopting the AIAS can expect adopting-district Wyoming students to recognize the leveled structure, and can cite the same peer-reviewed source for continuity.
- **Higher-education implication:** Wyoming builds higher education into district AI governance by recommending a "Local College Representative" on the policy committee (guidance p.~141). A Wyoming college may already be a named partner in the districts feeding it.
- **Higher-education implication:** Wyoming students may arrive habituated to *submitting links to their AI chats* as disclosure (guidance p.7), the same distinctive practice Oklahoma teaches. Faculty who want process transparency can build on it.
- **Higher-education implication:** Students from adopting districts may expect AI permission to be *specified per assignment* along a four-level continuum with matching disclosure and honesty pledges (guidance p.7). A silent or blanket syllabus will read as less clear than what they experienced.
- **Higher-education implication:** The Knowledge principle's college-preparation framing (guidance p.~214) suggests Wyoming students may connect AI literacy to postsecondary readiness.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Assessment design:** Adopt an explicit per-assignment AI-permission scale; the AIAS gives direct continuity with Wyoming's continuum (guidance p.7). Preserve AI FREE assessments where core-skill demonstration is the goal.
- **Disclosure practice:** Consider adopting the chat-link disclosure convention Wyoming (and Oklahoma) teach (guidance p.7), since incoming students may already practice it.
- **Governance and partnership:** Wyoming colleges can leverage the "Local College Representative" role (guidance p.~141) to align entry expectations with feeder districts.
- **Onboarding:** Give a short AI-literacy diagnostic at entry; the continuum's four levels are a ready placement rubric.
- **Academic-integrity processes:** Favor disclosure- and continuum-based integrity, matching the state's approach (guidance p.7); set your own detector policy explicitly, since the guidance takes no position.

## Unique Aspects

- **A four-level Student AI Usage Continuum** (AI FREE, AI ASSISTED, AI ENHANCED, AI EMPOWERED) with primary-printed AIAS attribution (guidance p.7), the third clearly confirmed AIAS-lineage instrument in the dataset.
- **A shared-source signal with Oklahoma.** Both states misspell "MacVaugh" as "Mcvaugh" in their AIAS attribution and both require submitting links to AI chats, suggesting a common intermediate adaptation source, useful for the cross-state comparison. *[Cross-state note]*
- **A Local College Representative on the recommended district policy committee** (guidance p.~141), a concrete higher-education governance hook.
- **Seven TeachAI-derived principles** (Purpose, Compliance, Knowledge, Balance, Integrity, Agency, Evaluation) (guidance p.~149), shared DNA with other TeachAI-adapting states.
- **Definitions borrowed from Washington OSPI**, showing multi-source adaptation (guidance p.~55).
- **A compact, guiding-question policy-development format** (9 pages) aimed squarely at helping districts write their own policy.

## Evidence Gaps and Uncertainties

- **Wyoming upgrades from tracker to primary.** The 9-page WDE guidance was read page by page; the seven principles, the four-level continuum, and the AIAS attribution are all primary-confirmed. This is the first pass to retrieve any WDE primary document (sources.md). *[Resolved]*

- **The continuum graphic contains typos.** WDE misspells "Perkings"/"Mcvaugh" in the attribution and the graphic contains "mush," "fariness," and "knowlesge" (guidance p.7); these are transcribed as printed and corrected in brackets where quoted. *[Transcription note]*
- **The continuum is unnumbered.** Wyoming uses four named tiers (AI FREE through AI EMPOWERED), not a 0-4 or 1-5 numbering; do not report a numeric scale. *[Classification note]*
- **The higher-education hook is a committee recommendation, not a mandate.** The Local College Representative is a suggested committee member (guidance p.~141), weaker than Tennessee's statutory university mandate or Virginia's assigned roles. *[Scope of hook]*
- **No statute or bill is recorded** for Wyoming in this folder. No enacted or pending AI-in-education requirement can be asserted. *[Unresolved]*
