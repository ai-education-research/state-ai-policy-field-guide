**Nature of the documents (read before everything below):** Ohio is one of the few jurisdictions in this dataset with a genuine statewide **mandate**, but the mandate is narrow and specific. Under ORC 3301.24, enacted through HB 96 (the state budget, effective Sep 30, 2025), every traditional public school district, community school, and STEM school **must adopt a policy on the use of AI by July 1, 2026** (sources.md; model policy p.2). What is required is the *act of adopting a policy*, not any particular content. Districts "may adopt the department's model policy" or write their own. The DEW Model Policy itself is a **template with bracketed placeholders**, offered for adoption, so its provisions are Recommendation-level unless a district enacts them. The one Requirement-level fact is the adoption deadline.

## Executive Summary

Ohio's approach pairs a statutory adoption mandate with a ready-made model policy. HB 96 required DEW to publish a model policy by Dec 31, 2025 (met, published Dec 30, 2025) and requires all districts, community schools, and STEM schools to adopt some AI-use policy by July 1, 2026 (model policy p.2). The model policy is a fill-in-the-blank board policy, not a narrative framework or a student-use scale. Its ten sections run Purpose, Definitions, AI Literacy, Stakeholder Engagement, Data Privacy and Security, Procurement, Ethical Use, Acceptable Use, Academic Integrity, and Implementation and Review, plus notes on non-consensual intimate imagery (model policy pp.2-5).

For a higher-education reader, two facts stand out. First, the July 1, 2026 mandate means every Ohio public district and community/STEM school will have an AI-use policy of some kind. That is a firmer baseline than the purely voluntary states offer, even though the content of each policy is locally determined. Second, the Tier-A higher-education hook is confirmed in the primary text. The model policy's Stakeholder Engagement section recommends an AI workgroup that includes "external representatives such as local businesses and postsecondary institutions" (model policy p.3). That is a recommendation, not a requirement, but it names higher education directly.

Ohio deliberately makes the DEW model policy the lead document. The earlier InnovateOhio/aiEDU AI Toolkit (Feb 2024) is now orphaned, both its landing page and PDF return 404 (sources.md), so it should no longer anchor Ohio's entry.

## K–12 AI Policy and Guidance

### Primary themes and policy objectives
- **Human-centered AI is the stated value.** AI implementation "should be human-centered and should empower students, educators, and communities. It is a tool to support learning and teaching, not a substitute for student effort or the role of the educator" (model policy p.4, Ethical Use). *[Recommendation within the model]*
- **Prepare students for an AI-shaped future** while providing "consistent expectations, standards, and approval processes for safe and responsible implementation" (model policy p.2, Purpose). *[Recommendation]*
- **Statewide consistency through required adoption.** The animating objective of HB 96 is that every covered school have an AI-use policy in force by July 1, 2026 (model policy p.2). *[Requirement — adoption only]*

### Intended audiences and scope
- The model policy is written for adoption by a district or school Board of Education (bracketed "[insert name of district/school]," p.2). *[Scope statement]*
- The statutory mandate reaches traditional public districts, community schools (Ch. 3314), and STEM schools (Ch. 3326) (model policy p.2). *[Requirement scope]*
- Scope covers both educational and operational AI use, including generative AI (model policy p.2). *[Scope statement]*

### Permitted, restricted, and prohibited AI uses
- **There is no statewide leveled permission scale.** The Acceptable Use section delegates the permission line to educators per assignment: responsible uses "will vary depending on the context, including but not limited to grade level, subject, and/or the nature of the classroom activity," and expectations should be specified "in course syllabi and assignment instructions when relevant" (model policy p.4). *[Recommendation]*
- **The model does contain clear prohibitions where adopted.** "Use of tools that have not been approved is not permitted" (p.4), "Using AI tools for bullying, harassment, and any form of intimidation is strictly prohibited" (p.4), and "No assignment shall require the use of a tool that cannot be provided by the [district/school]" (p.4). *[Recommendation, becomes Requirement if a district adopts the model]*
- **Non-consensual intimate imagery (NCII) is flagged**, with a reference to Ohio's Braden's Law classifying sexual extortion as a felony (model policy p.5). *[Recommendation + statutory context]*

### Privacy, security, equity, accessibility, ethics, and academic integrity
- **Data privacy is a full section.** AI tools "must adhere to existing data privacy and security policies that include... PII, FERPA, and any other relevant state of Ohio and federal laws," should practice data minimization, and must be vetted for encryption and access control (model policy p.3). *[Recommendation restating Requirement-level law]*
- **Procurement is governed** by existing procurement policy plus AI-specific checks for data privacy, instructional and operational alignment, accessibility, and cost, with third-party vendors required to meet state and federal data-protection, ethical-use, and accessibility standards (model policy pp.3-4). *[Recommendation]*
- **Ethical use is human-centered**, requiring users to "critically analyze AI output, respect safeguards and rules, and be transparent about its use" (model policy p.4). *[Recommendation]*
- **Academic integrity is explicit and process-oriented.** "AI-enabled tools may be used to support student work (such as brainstorming or feedback), but AI-generated work must not replace student work," with proper citation "according to existing policies and expectations... (such as APA or MLA style formats)" (model policy p.4). *[Recommendation]*
- **Position on AI detection tools:** the model policy does not mention AI detectors at all. It neither endorses nor cautions against them, leaving integrity enforcement to existing academic-integrity procedures (model policy p.4). *[Not addressed]*

### Teacher training, curriculum integration, assessment, and implementation
- **AI literacy for all is a policy section.** The Board is to "responsibly integrate AI by building AI literacy for all students and educators, including integration of AI into relevant curriculum, professional learning opportunities, and safe and responsible usage" (model policy p.2). *[Recommendation]*
- **Educator use is bound to professional standards.** "Educator use should be in alignment with the Licensure Code of Conduct for Ohio's Educators" (model policy p.4). *[Recommendation]*
- **Assessment design is a named consideration.** Educators "should consider the impact on learning objectives and assessment of student learning when designing related instruction" (model policy p.4). *[Instructional guidance]*
- **Implementation is workgroup-driven and iterative.** An ongoing AI workgroup monitors developments and updates the policy, which "should be reviewed often" (model policy p.5). *[Recommendation]*

## Learning and Cognitive-Offloading Strategies

Ohio's model policy addresses offloading through its Ethical Use and Academic Integrity sections rather than a dedicated framework.

- **AI is defined as no substitute for student effort.** "It is a tool to support learning and teaching, not a substitute for student effort or the role of the educator" (model policy p.4). *[Recommendation]*
- **AI-generated work must not replace student work.** Students "are expected to complete assignments and assessments in a manner that reflects their own understanding and effort, critically analyze AI-generated content and not misrepresent it as original work" (model policy p.4). *[Recommendation]*
- **Critical analysis of AI output is a standing user obligation.** Users "should critically analyze AI output, respect safeguards and rules, and be transparent about its use" (model policy p.4). *[Recommendation]*
- **The document does not develop a grade-band cognitive-offloading progression** (unlike North Carolina or North Dakota). Its offloading controls are integrity- and effort-based statements, not developmental scaffolding. *[Absence noted]*

## Expected Graduate AI Profile

These describe habits an Ohio graduate *may* bring. The adoption mandate raises the floor, but policy content still varies by district.

- **Governed by a formally adopted AI-use policy.** From July 1, 2026, every Ohio public district and community/STEM school must have one, so an Ohio graduate's schooling was more likely than in voluntary states to carry an explicit, board-adopted AI policy. *Basis: model policy p.2; ORC 3301.24 (sources.md). [Requirement — adoption only.]*
- **Familiar with per-assignment, per-syllabus AI expectations**, if the district adopted the model, since it directs educators to specify AI use in syllabi and assignment instructions (model policy p.4). *Basis: p.4.*
- **A citation-and-effort integrity habit**, where the district adopted the model's academic-integrity language (APA/MLA citation of AI-assisted work; no misrepresentation) (model policy p.4). *Basis: p.4.*
- **An "approved tools only" expectation**, where the model was adopted (model policy p.4). *Basis: p.4.*
- **Higher-education implication:** Treat the *existence* of a policy as a reasonable assumption for recent Ohio graduates, but treat the *content* as unknown and district-specific. The mandate guarantees a policy, not a common vocabulary or scale.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** Ohio names higher education in the primary text. The model policy recommends an AI workgroup with "external representatives such as local businesses and postsecondary institutions" (model policy p.3). An Ohio college or university may be invited onto a district AI workgroup, a direct, if recommendation-level, K-16 hook.
- **Higher-education implication:** Because of the July 1, 2026 mandate, incoming Ohio students (from that point forward) came from schools that were *required to have an AI-use policy*. Faculty can reasonably assume a policy existed, which is a firmer starting point than the voluntary states, while still not assuming any particular content.
- **Higher-education implication:** Where districts adopted the model, students met an integrity norm that *AI may assist but not replace student work* and that AI-assisted work must be cited in APA or MLA (model policy p.4). This maps cleanly onto typical university integrity expectations.
- **Higher-education implication:** Students from model-adopting districts may expect AI permission to be *specified per syllabus and per assignment* and to use only *approved tools* (model policy p.4). A university syllabus that states AI expectations explicitly will feel continuous with that experience.
- **Higher-education implication:** Ohio's model is silent on AI detectors (model policy p.4), so Ohio students were neither trained to expect nor to distrust detection tools. Their expectations on that point are unknown and should not be assumed.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Governance and partnership:** Ohio institutions should expect, and can seek, seats on district AI workgroups, which the model policy explicitly invites (model policy p.3). This is a channel to align entry expectations with feeder districts.
- **Assessment design:** State AI permission explicitly per assignment and per syllabus, mirroring the model's Acceptable Use language (model policy p.4). Ohio students from adopting districts will recognize this pattern.
- **Academic-integrity processes:** Build on the model's assist-not-replace and cite-your-AI norms (model policy p.4). These translate directly to university integrity policy.
- **Onboarding:** Assume a policy existed but not a shared scale. Give a short AI-literacy diagnostic; content varied across the many districts that wrote their own policies rather than adopting the model.
- **Do not assume a detector stance.** Because the model is silent on detectors, set your own integrity-tooling expectations explicitly.

## Unique Aspects

- **A genuine statewide adoption mandate.** ORC 3301.24 (HB 96) requires every district, community school, and STEM school to adopt an AI-use policy by July 1, 2026, one of the dataset's few true statewide requirements (model policy p.2; sources.md).
- **A fill-in-the-blank model policy**, a board-policy template with bracketed placeholders rather than a narrative guidance document (model policy pp.2-5).
- **A primary-confirmed higher-education hook**, postsecondary institutions named as recommended AI-workgroup members (model policy p.3).
- **Explicit non-consensual intimate imagery language**, tied to Ohio's Braden's Law on sexual extortion (model policy p.5), a specific harm few state documents name.
- **Tight integration with existing board policies** (bullying, academic integrity, procurement, special education) rather than a standalone regime (model policy p.5).
- **Educator use bound to the Licensure Code of Conduct for Ohio's Educators** (model policy p.4), linking AI use to professional licensure standards.

## Evidence Gaps and Uncertainties

- **The mandate is adoption, not content.** ORC 3301.24 requires each school to adopt *a* policy; it does not require the DEW model or any specific provision. Districts "may adopt the department's model policy... or write their own," so the actual student experience varies by district. The only Requirement-level claim is the July 1, 2026 adoption deadline. *[Scope of mandate]*
- **The statute is not in the folder as a document.** ORC 3301.24's text is recorded in sources.md (verified page) and quoted within the model policy (p.2), but the standalone codified section was not separately captured. The quoted subsections are taken from the model policy's reproduction. *[Unresolved]*
- **No leveled student-use instrument exists.** Ohio's model delegates permission to educators per assignment and offers no 0-4 or 1-5 scale and no adaptation of an external scale. This is a genuine absence.
- **The AI Toolkit lead document is orphaned.** The InnovateOhio/aiEDU AI Toolkit (Feb 2024) that data.js currently leads Ohio with returns HTTP 404 at both its landing page and PDF (sources.md). The DEW model policy should become the lead document; the toolkit's "analyzes 21 guideline sets" claim could not be confirmed. *[Flag for data.js]*
- **An editable .docx of the model policy is linked but was not analyzed**; the PDF is authoritative here (sources.md).
