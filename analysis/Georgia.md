## Executive Summary

Georgia is one of the most instrument-rich jurisdictions in the set, and its instruments are exactly the kind a faculty member can lift. The GaDOE guidance supplies three transferable artifacts: a **high-stakes versus non-high-stakes use table** (Leveraging-AI-in-K12-Setting.pdf, p.4), a seven-criterion **Evaluation Process Rubric** for vetting AI tools (p.10), and a classroom **"redlight system"** for signaling per-assignment AI permission, adapted from Phil Hintz's THINK guide (p.12). The GaPSC document adds **seven ethical principles**, each with standards and guiding questions, grounding AI use in the Georgia Code of Ethics for Educators (GaPSC-Ethical-Considerations-AI-2025-06-16.pdf).

For a higher-education reader, three things matter. First, the redlight system is the project's flagship "portable syllabus artifact" — a three-signal way to state AI permission per assignment — and it is confirmed real, though adapted rather than original to GaDOE. Second, the high-stakes/non-high-stakes table is a ready model for deciding what a faculty member should never delegate to AI (subjective grading, evaluations, IEP-style individualized decisions). Third, GaPSC explicitly involves **educator preparation providers (EPPs)** — university teacher-prep programs — giving Georgia a soft higher-education hook. Both documents are voluntary, so student exposure varies by district.

## K–12 AI Policy and Guidance

### Primary themes and policy objectives
- **AI as tool, not replacement.** "AI is a tool to increase efficiencies and effectiveness, not a replacement for human interaction during the teaching and learning process" (Leveraging-AI-in-K12-Setting.pdf, p.1). GaPSC: "AI is not a primary source" and "should not take the place of the teacher" (GaPSC..., Important Understandings #1). *[Recommendation]*
- **Supportive, not high-stakes, use.** The four ethical musts: use AI "in a supportive, not high-stakes manner"; "enhance, not replace, human judgment"; "promote transparency"; "respect privacy" (Leveraging-AI-in-K12-Setting.pdf, p.3). *[Recommendation]*
- **Academic integrity via attribution.** Proper attribution of AI-generated content is "crucial" (Leveraging-AI-in-K12-Setting.pdf, p.14). *[Recommendation]*

### Intended audiences and scope
- GaDOE guidance addresses district and school leaders and educators across Georgia K-12 public schools (Leveraging-AI-in-K12-Setting.pdf, p.1). *[Scope]*
- GaPSC guidance addresses "pre-service and in-service administrators, educators, and staff," explicitly including **educator preparation providers (EPPs)** (GaPSC..., Audience; Purpose). *[Scope — includes teacher-prep]*

### Permitted, restricted, and prohibited AI uses
- The GaDOE **high-stakes vs non-high-stakes table** (Leveraging-AI-in-K12-Setting.pdf, p.4) is the closest thing to a prohibition list, framed as uses to "refrain from":
  - **High-stakes uses to avoid:** "Write IEP goals for individual students"; "Evaluate lesson plans"; "Evaluate educators"; "Evaluate the resumes and applications of job candidates to narrow a group of finalists"; "Grade student assignments or projects that are subjective"; "Analyze student data to predict future success, recommend class placements, or flag potential disciplinary issues." *[Recommendation — confirms the previously tracker-flagged IEP/evaluation/subjective-grading list at primary source]*
  - **Non-high-stakes uses permitted:** create learning targets; assist in lessons/lesson plans; develop job descriptions; develop a rubric; feedback on non-graded work; "Grade multiple choice exams"; modify reading level of grade-level text (p.4). *[Example]*
- The **redlight system** (p.12): "educators may use a 'redlight system' to communicate to students when AI use is allowable and to what extent," adapted from Phil Hintz's THINK guide. Per sources.md's image-based read, RED = not allowed / academic dishonesty; YELLOW = content-creation assistance; GREEN = encouraged with citation. *[Instrument / recommendation; color definitions confirmed via graphic, not plain text]*
- **PII prohibition:** "Never input PII, such as social security numbers, home addresses, health information, academic information... into AI systems" (p.5). *[Recommendation, strong]*

### Privacy, security, equity, accessibility, ethics, and academic integrity
- **Privacy:** dedicated PII-safeguarding section; use anonymized/aggregated data; annual PII professional learning; federal statutes listed (COPPA, FERPA, Privacy Act, PPRA) plus Section 504 and IDEA accessibility requirements (Leveraging-AI-in-K12-Setting.pdf, pp.5-6). *[Recommendation]*
- **Vendor governance:** formal vendor agreements, TrustEd Apps catalog reference, and a formal vetting process with a review committee (pp.9-10). *[Recommendation]*
- **Bias:** GaPSC Principle #6 — evaluate AI output for bias, choose vendors documenting bias-mitigation, ensure tools "do not exacerbate disparities" (GaPSC..., Principle #6). *[Recommendation]*
- **Academic integrity:** attribution/citation of AI content; integrate AI into existing academic-integrity, acceptable-use, grading, and code-of-conduct policies (Leveraging-AI-in-K12-Setting.pdf, pp.8, 14; GaPSC Principle #2). *[Recommendation]*
- **AI detection tools:** a position on detection software is **Not found in the provided documents.** *[Limited]*

### Teacher training, curriculum integration, assessment, and implementation
- **Tool-vetting instrument — the Evaluation Process Rubric** (Leveraging-AI-in-K12-Setting.pdf, p.10): seven criteria — Educational Value, Data Privacy, Usability, Cost, Scalability, Vendor Reputation, and Age restrictions. *[Instrument / recommendation]*
- **District policy-adoption process** (p.7): engage stakeholders, needs assessment, draft, pilot, finalize/implement, regular review. *[Recommendation]*
- **Professional learning** is emphasized as ongoing, not one-off; GaPSC repeatedly stresses that "effective professional development regarding AI cannot be accomplished in single, standalone sessions" (Leveraging-AI-in-K12-Setting.pdf, p.11; GaPSC..., Important Understandings #4). *[Recommendation]*
- **Assessment:** GaPSC Principle #1 — "Humans should always review and take responsibility for the output of AI tools when they are used to make decisions about student performance, placement, and/or discipline"; transparency when AI is used for assessment/feedback (GaPSC..., Principle #1). *[Recommendation]*

## Learning and Cognitive-Offloading Strategies

- The GaDOE classroom section ties clear AI boundaries to preserving thinking: "Clear guidelines help maintain academic integrity and ensure that students develop their own critical thinking and problem-solving skills," and educators should "Encourage critical thinking about AI limitations" (Leveraging-AI-in-K12-Setting.pdf, p.12). *[Recommendation]*
- The redlight system's Level RED ("must rely on their own knowledge... during exams or assessments," p.12) preserves unaided demonstration of understanding. *[Recommendation]*
- GaPSC Principle #2: "Aid students in understanding AI should be used as a tool and not a replacement for learning" (GaPSC..., Principle #2). *[Recommendation]*
- A developed cognitive-science treatment (metacognition, productive struggle, over-reliance research) comparable to Arizona's is **Not found in the provided documents.** Georgia's guard is the redlight boundaries plus the high-stakes/non-high-stakes framing. *[Limited]*

## Expected Graduate AI Profile

- **Uneven by district (voluntary guidance).** Both documents are voluntary, so exposure depends on district adoption. *Basis: p.1; GaPSC Purpose.*
- **Where adopted**, Georgia students may bring: familiarity with a per-assignment traffic-light signal for AI permission (redlight system, p.12); a disclosure-and-attribution habit for AI-assisted work (p.14); awareness that AI should not be used on assessments meant to show individual understanding (p.12); and general AI-literacy including AI's limitations and bias (GaDOE p.12; GaPSC Principle #6).
- **Higher-education implication:** The most Georgia-shaped students may arrive already understanding a three-signal AI-permission convention and an attribution expectation — a strong, transferable foundation. But assume variance given voluntariness.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** Georgia's redlight system (p.12) is the closest K-12 analogue to a syllabus AI-permission statement. Faculty can adopt the same three-signal convention (with attribution credit to Phil Hintz's THINK guide) and expect partial prior familiarity from Georgia students.
- **Higher-education implication:** The high-stakes/non-high-stakes table (p.4) transfers directly to higher-education practice — it is a ready decision rule for what faculty and administrators should never delegate to AI (subjective grading, evaluations, individualized high-stakes decisions like accommodations).
- **Higher-education implication:** GaPSC explicitly involves educator preparation providers (university teacher-prep programs) and urges districts to "work closely with their EPP partners" (GaPSC..., Important Understandings #7). Georgia colleges of education are already positioned in this pipeline.
- **Higher-education implication:** Georgia students may expect AI use to be signaled per assignment and attributed, not silently banned. A syllabus that adopts the redlight convention will feel continuous; a silent one will not.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Assessment design:** Adopt a per-assignment AI-permission signal modeled on the redlight system (p.12), and preserve unaided "own knowledge" assessments where the goal is individual understanding.
- **Academic integrity:** Require AI attribution/citation as the GaDOE and GaPSC documents do (p.14; Principle #2), and fold AI into existing integrity policy rather than a separate rule.
- **Governance and procurement:** Reuse the seven-criterion Evaluation Process Rubric (p.10) and the high-stakes/non-high-stakes distinction (p.4) as institutional tool-vetting and decision-boundary instruments.
- **Faculty development:** Treat AI PD as ongoing, not one-off, per GaPSC's explicit point (Important Understandings #4).
- **Teacher-prep alignment:** Georgia colleges of education should align with GaPSC's seven principles, since EPPs are named participants (GaPSC..., Purpose).
- **Advancing learning rather than replacing thinking:** Build on the "develop their own critical thinking and problem-solving skills" framing (p.12) and the "AI is not a primary source" stance (GaPSC..., Important Understandings #1).

## Unique Aspects

- **The redlight (traffic-light) system** for per-assignment AI permission (Leveraging-AI-in-K12-Setting.pdf, p.12) — the project's flagship portable artifact, confirmed in primary text and adapted from Phil Hintz's THINK guide.
- **A high-stakes vs non-high-stakes use table** (p.4) that names specific uses to avoid (IEP goals, educator evaluations, subjective grading, predictive data uses) and specific permitted uses.

- **Two complementary state documents** — GaDOE operational guidance plus GaPSC educator-ethics guidance grounded in the Georgia Code of Ethics, with seven principles, standards, and guiding questions.
