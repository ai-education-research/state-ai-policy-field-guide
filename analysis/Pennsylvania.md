**Nature of the document (read before everything below):** Pennsylvania has no PDE-authored K-12 classroom AI framework and no student-use scale in the record. Its one substantive primary document is a **teacher-certification endorsement framework**, an instrument aimed at university teacher-preparation programs, not at classrooms. The endorsement is itself voluntary: it is a 12-credit "Program Endorsement" added to an existing certificate that is "not required to perform service" (endorsement, p.3). So the document sets competencies for a program educators may choose to complete, not a mandate on schools or students. Everything below is Recommendation-level (competency expectations for a voluntary endorsement) unless noted.

## Executive Summary

Pennsylvania's distinctive contribution is not a classroom framework but a teacher-preparation instrument. In June 2025, PDE's Office of Postsecondary and Higher Education published a Framework for Artificial Intelligence Program Endorsement Guidelines, a set of competencies for a 12-credit certification endorsement that "baccalaureate, or graduate degree-granting institutions, alone or in cooperation with other institutions, community colleges or school entities, may offer" (endorsement, p.3). The endorsement is added to an existing Level I or Level II teaching certificate and documents "knowledge in new and emerging areas where formal certification does not exist" (endorsement, p.3).

The framework organizes teacher competencies into six domains: Foundations of AI and Its Applications, Ethical and Responsible AI Use, AI for Teaching and Assessment, AI Literacy for Students, AI Leadership and Innovation in Schools, and AI's Role in Human and Societal Thriving (endorsement, pp.5-7). It requires a capstone field experience in a PK-12 school (endorsement, p.5). Its purpose is to prepare educators to "integrate AI meaningfully into their teaching" in the absence of any professional-organization PK-12 AI standards, a gap the document names explicitly (endorsement, p.3).

For a higher-education reader, this is the dataset's clearest and most direct higher-education hook, and it runs the opposite direction from most states. Rather than describe what a K-12 graduate learned, Pennsylvania describes what university teacher-preparation programs (EPPs, Educator Preparation Programs) must teach the teachers. The framework was developed with university faculty (Millersville University, St. Vincent College, and Moravian University as advisory-committee lead; endorsement, p.8), and Domain 6 explicitly names cognitive offloading as a competency, requiring candidates to examine "how cognitive offloading to AI systems influences attention, memory, and problem-solving" (endorsement, p.7).

## K–12 AI Policy and Guidance

### Primary themes and policy objectives
- **The animating objective is educator preparation, not student regulation.** The framework exists because "there are currently no professional organizations that have established specific standards for its use in PK-12 settings," a gap it addresses with a proposed competency set (endorsement, p.3). *[Recommendation / framing]*
- **AI literacy for both teachers and students** is framed as an urgent, workforce- and equity-driven need (endorsement, Overview). *[Recommendation]*
- **Human-AI complementarity is a stated value.** Candidates must "articulate the principles of human-AI complementarity, demonstrating how AI can augment human capabilities and serve as an effective partner" (endorsement, p.7, Domain 6). *[Recommendation]*

### Intended audiences and scope
- The primary audience is **Educator Preparation Programs (EPPs)**, degree-granting institutions and community colleges that design the endorsement program (endorsement, p.3). *[Scope statement]*
- The ultimate audience is **PK-12 teachers** who complete it and, through them, their students (endorsement, Candidate Competencies). *[Scope statement]*
- Scope is teacher competency across PK-12, not a student-use rulebook. *[Scope statement]*

### Permitted, restricted, and prohibited AI uses
- **There is no student-use permission scale and no statewide prohibition list.** Pennsylvania regulates teacher preparation, not classroom permission. The framework instead teaches future teachers to "demonstrate responsible AI decision-making by recognizing when to leverage AI assistance and when to prioritize human judgment and creativity" (endorsement, p.6, Domain 2). *[Recommendation / not a classroom rule]*
- Candidates are expected to help students critically evaluate AI, but the framework sets no fixed allowed/prohibited student uses. *[Not found in the provided documents]*

### Privacy, security, equity, accessibility, ethics, and academic integrity
- **Ethics and privacy are Domain 2.** Candidates must "analyze terms of service and data usage policies of AI-powered educational platforms to assess their alignment with ethical guidelines and privacy regulations (e.g., FERPA, COPPA)" and "identify and differentiate types of bias in AI models" (endorsement, p.6). *[Recommendation]*
- **Equity is a throughline.** Candidates must "advocate for equitable AI integration... ensuring AI tools enhance learning opportunities rather than reinforce existing disparities" (endorsement, p.6), and Domain 3 requires supporting "learners with disabilities, multilingual learners, and neurodiverse students" (endorsement, p.6). *[Recommendation]*
- **Academic integrity is present but deliberately not the focus.** Domain 2 asks candidates to "confront AI's potential societal impact by examining both its benefits and risks beyond academic integrity concerns" (endorsement, p.6), signaling that PA frames AI as bigger than a cheating problem. *[Recommendation]*
- **Position on AI detection tools:** the framework does not address AI detectors. Its integrity content is competency-based (teaching future teachers to reason about AI), not enforcement-based. *[Not addressed]*

### Teacher training, curriculum integration, assessment, and implementation
- **This document is fundamentally about teacher training.** It is a full teacher-preparation program specification: program design and delivery, field experiences, and six competency domains (endorsement, pp.4-7). *[Recommendation]*
- **A capstone PK-12 field experience is required.** Candidates "secure a PK-12 school partner" and complete a synthesizing, real-world capstone (endorsement, p.5). *[Recommendation within the program]*
- **AI for Teaching and Assessment is Domain 3**, covering AI in lesson planning, personalized learning, timely feedback, and interpreting "AI-generated learning analytics to refine instructional strategies" (endorsement, p.6). *[Recommendation]*
- **AI Leadership (Domain 5)** prepares educators to assess district AI initiatives and "design professional learning sessions" for other educators (endorsement, p.7). *[Recommendation]*
- **Complementary state-affiliated resources** exist but are not PDE classroom frameworks: the PaTTAN AI Toolkit (a Bureau of Special Education project) and PDE's general "AI in K-12 Education" web guidance (sources.md). *[Example]*

## Learning and Cognitive-Offloading Strategies

Pennsylvania is unusual in making cognitive offloading an explicit teacher competency rather than an aside.

- **Domain 6 names cognitive offloading directly.** Candidates must "analyze the cognitive and psychological effects of AI usage and reliance," including "examining how cognitive offloading to AI systems influences attention, memory, and problem-solving" (endorsement, p.7). Few state documents in the dataset make this a formal, assessed competency. *[Recommendation / competency]*
- **Human agency is a required teaching stance.** Candidates must "model the balance of AI augmentation with human agency in academic and professional settings" (endorsement, p.6, Domain 4). *[Recommendation]*
- **Knowing when not to use AI is a competency.** Domain 2 requires recognizing "when to prioritize human judgment and creativity" (endorsement, p.6). *[Recommendation]*
- **Domain 6 also flags socialization and well-being effects**, "impact on socialization, social-emotional well-being, and sense of belonging" (endorsement, p.7). *[Recommendation]*
- Because this is a teacher-competency framework, the offloading concern is embedded in what future teachers must understand, not in a student-facing scale or grade-band scaffold. *[Structural note]*

## Expected Graduate AI Profile

Pennsylvania regulates teacher preparation, not student experience, so a graduate profile must be inferred cautiously and indirectly.

- **No state-set student experience.** Pennsylvania published no classroom framework or student-use scale, so a Pennsylvania graduate's AI exposure was set entirely at the district level. *Basis: sources.md; absence of a PDE classroom framework.*
- **Possible exposure to endorsement-trained teachers.** Students taught by a teacher who completed the AI endorsement may have encountered AI literacy aligned to the six domains, especially critical evaluation of AI content and human-agency framing (endorsement, pp.6-7). But the endorsement is voluntary and new (June 2025), so few teachers hold it yet. *Basis: endorsement, pp.5-7.*
- **Higher-education implication:** Treat student-level AI exposure as unknown and district-specific. The endorsement raises teacher capacity over time, but it does not create a uniform student experience, and its effect on any current graduate is minimal given how recently it launched.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** This is a direct obligation-shaping instrument for higher education itself. Pennsylvania universities and community colleges are the bodies that design and deliver the AI endorsement (endorsement, p.3). Teacher-preparation programs in Pennsylvania have a state competency template for AI, developed with university faculty (Millersville, St. Vincent, Moravian; endorsement, p.8).
- **Higher-education implication:** The endorsement's six domains are a ready-made scaffold a university could adopt for its own faculty development or for an AI-in-teaching credential, and Domain 6's explicit cognitive-offloading competency (endorsement, p.7) is a model for how to teach AI's effects on attention and memory.
- **Higher-education implication:** For incoming Pennsylvania students, there is no state K-12 baseline to assume. Entry planning should mirror the no-classroom-framework states, an explicit orientation and diagnostic, until PA's forthcoming statewide guidance (below) takes shape.
- **Higher-education implication:** A university-run process is actively shaping Pennsylvania's future K-12 AI guidance. Penn State's "Shaping AI in Education: A Pennsylvania Listening and Learning Tour" (March-June 2026), sponsored by and partnered with PDE, is expected to produce a comprehensive report and recommendations in fall 2026 (sources.md). Higher education is literally authoring the state's next step.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Teacher-preparation alignment:** Pennsylvania EPPs should map their programs to the six endorsement domains (endorsement, pp.5-7) and consider offering the 12-credit endorsement, which positions their graduates to teach AI literacy in PK-12.
- **Adopt the cognitive-offloading competency:** Domain 6's requirement to analyze offloading effects on attention, memory, and problem-solving (endorsement, p.7) is a strong template for any institution's own AI-literacy or faculty-development curriculum.
- **Onboarding for current cohorts:** Assume no state student baseline. Give incoming Pennsylvania students an explicit AI-use orientation and a short literacy diagnostic.
- **Engage the Penn State/PDE process:** Institutions can participate in or track the listening tour producing PA's statewide guidance (fall 2026) to align entry expectations early (sources.md).
- **Assessment design:** State AI permission explicitly per assignment; Pennsylvania students share no statewide scale or vocabulary.

## Unique Aspects

- **A teacher-certification endorsement as the state's flagship AI instrument.** Pennsylvania is the clearest case in the dataset of a state acting on AI through *teacher preparation and higher education* rather than a classroom framework (endorsement, p.3).
- **Issued by the Office of Postsecondary and Higher Education**, not a K-12 curriculum office, underscoring the higher-education locus (endorsement, title page).
- **Six named teacher-competency domains**, including a distinct Domain 6 on AI's societal role that formally includes cognitive-offloading analysis (endorsement, pp.5-7).
- **A required PK-12 capstone field experience** tying university coursework to classroom practice (endorsement, p.5).
- **University faculty co-authorship**, Millersville University, St. Vincent College, and Moravian University (advisory-committee lead) among the named developers (endorsement, p.8).
- **A university-run statewide guidance process** (Penn State listening tour, PDE-sponsored) that will produce PA's forthcoming K-12 recommendations (sources.md).

## Evidence Gaps and Uncertainties

- **The endorsement is voluntary and new.** It is "not required to perform service" (endorsement, p.3) and dates to June 2025, so few teachers hold it and its classroom effect on any current student is minimal. It shapes future teacher capacity, not present student experience. *[Timing/scope]*
- **No PDE classroom framework or student-use scale exists.** Pennsylvania's student-facing state posture is genuinely thin; the substantive document is teacher-preparation, not classroom guidance. This is a real characteristic, not a retrieval gap.
- **Universities reported to offer the endorsement are third-party-sourced.** Millersville, Point Park, and Lebanon Valley are named in secondary coverage (sources.md), not confirmed from a PDE list; only the framework document itself is primary. *[Unresolved]*
- **The PaTTAN AI Toolkit and PDE web guidance were not downloaded as files**, only recorded as verified pages (sources.md). Their content was not independently analyzed and is state-affiliated rather than a PDE classroom framework. *[Unresolved]*
- **PA's statewide guidance does not yet exist.** The Penn State/PDE listening tour's report is expected fall 2026 (sources.md); until then, Pennsylvania "currently lacks a consistent, statewide framework." *[Pending]*
- **No enacted K-12 AI instructional statute** is recorded for Pennsylvania. No classroom AI mandate can be asserted. *[Unresolved]*
