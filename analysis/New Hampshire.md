## Executive Summary

New Hampshire has no state education-agency K-12 AI guidance in the provided record. The state's posture is distinctive in one respect: rather than author its own framework, NHED endorsed a document produced by a coalition of non-governmental education associations. Per sources.md and the cited NHPR reporting, that coalition includes the NH Learning Institute (coordinator), the NH School Administrators Association, the NH Association of School Principals, the NH School Boards Association, the NH Association of Special Education Administrators, and NH Supporting Tech-using Educators. The Commissioner endorsed the June 2025 document but did not write it, and it does not carry the force of state guidance.

For a higher-education reader, the operative facts are two. First, New Hampshire set no state AI-permission scale, prohibition list, or student-use vocabulary of its own. Second, whatever common ground exists among New Hampshire graduates was shaped, if at all, by a coalition document whose content this analysis cannot verify because the PDF was not retrievable. The practical planning assumption is a no-state-baseline condition with a possible, unverified layer of coalition-influenced practice.
