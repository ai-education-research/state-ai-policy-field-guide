**Nature of the documents (read before everything below):** Tennessee is the most load-bearing state in this project, and its central document is enacted **law**, not guidance. Public Chapter 550 imposes genuine, binding requirements. What it mandates is the *adoption of a policy* (by universities, local school boards, and charter schools), not any particular policy content, so the substantive rules still vary by institution. Claims sourced to PC 550 are Requirement-level. The TSBA model policy is a third-party template; the copy in hand is Cannon County's adopted version, so it is Recommendation-level as a model and Requirement-level only for the district that enacted it. Tags below reflect that.

## Executive Summary

Tennessee is the state where the legislature put higher education on the same statutory clock as K-12. Public Chapter 550 (2024) requires three sets of bodies to adopt AI-use policies: the University of Tennessee board of trustees, the board of regents, and each state university local governing board (Section 1); local boards of education (Section 2); and public charter school governing bodies (Section 3). The enacted text is precise and primary-confirmed. For higher education, "The board of trustees of the University of Tennessee, the board of regents, and each local governing board of trustees of a state university shall adopt a policy regarding the use of artificial intelligence technology by students, faculty, and staff for instructional and assignment purposes... implemented no later than July 1, 2025" (PC 550, sec.1(b)).

This is the dataset's strongest and most direct higher-education hook, and it is not a recommendation. It is a statutory command with an enforcement backstop: boards must post the policy publicly and submit it to the legislature's education committee chairs by July 1, 2025, and a board that fails to comply "must appear before the joint government operations committee no later than sixty (60) days after July 1, 2025, to report on the governing board's noncompliance" (PC 550, sec.1(d)). On the K-12 side, local boards had to implement policies by the 2024-25 school year and report compliance to the Tennessee Department of Education (TDOE) each July 1 (PC 550, sec.2).

For a higher-education reader, this is the concrete rebuttal to the project's framing problem. Tennessee is the case where higher education did not get to debate on its own timeline. The state legislature required public universities to have AI policies in place by the same summer as the schools, and gave the requirement teeth. The TSBA model policy (4.214) shows what the K-12 side looks like in practice: approved-programs-only use, PII prohibitions, staff disclosure rules, academic-integrity instruction, parent notice, and the annual TDOE report.

## K–12 AI Policy and Guidance

### Primary themes and policy objectives
- **Mandatory policy adoption across three sectors.** PC 550 requires AI-use policies from public universities (sec.1), local school boards (sec.2), and charter schools (sec.3), all "for instructional and assignment purposes." *[Requirement — adoption]*
- **A uniform statutory AI definition.** All three sections use the "machine-based system that can... make predictions, recommendations, or decisions" formulation (PC 550, secs.1(a), 2, 3(a)). *[Requirement]*
- **The TSBA model policy's objective is safe, approved, disclosed use.** Only "approved AI programs may be utilized in student instruction or in completing student work" (TSBA 4.214, line 4). *[Model policy / Requirement for adopting districts]*

### Intended audiences and scope
- PC 550 reaches students, faculty, and staff at public universities (sec.1(b)) and students, teachers, and staff in K-12 districts and charter schools (secs.2, 3). *[Requirement scope]*
- The TSBA model policy addresses district staff and students, with separate Staff Use and Student Use sections (TSBA 4.214, lines 17, 5). *[Model policy scope]*

### Permitted, restricted, and prohibited AI uses
- **PC 550 mandates that policies exist; it does not set the permitted/prohibited line itself.** Each board writes its own policy content (PC 550, secs.1-3). *[Requirement — adoption, not content]*
- **The TSBA model policy sets an approved-programs-only rule.** "Only approved AI programs may be utilized in student instruction or in completing student work," with a submission process for new programs and technology-staff review for privacy-law compliance (TSBA 4.214, lines 4-9). *[Model policy / Requirement for adopting districts]*
- **PII is prohibited in AI systems.** Employees "shall not place personally identifiable information, financial information, intellectual property, or other confidential information into an AI system" (TSBA 4.214, lines 10-11). *[Model policy]*

### Privacy, security, equity, accessibility, ethics, and academic integrity
- **Data privacy is central to the model policy**, tying AI-program review to "state and federal student data privacy laws" and requiring secure storage (TSBA 4.214, lines 6-16). *[Model policy]*
- **Equity of access is stated.** "Any approved programs shall be accessible to all students" (TSBA 4.214, line 9). *[Model policy]*
- **Academic integrity is instruction-based.** Students "shall be instructed on responsible use standards," including "effective use of generative AI," when AI is appropriate in assignments, "how to determine whether AI responses are accurate," user responsibility, and "the difference between cheating and seeking support" (TSBA 4.214, lines 9-19). *[Model policy]*
- **Staff disclosure is triggered conditionally.** Employees must disclose generative-AI use if not doing so would violate the tool's terms, "mislead a supervisor or others as to the nature of the work," or be "inconsistent with the teacher code of ethics" (TSBA 4.214, lines 22-25). *[Model policy]*
- **Position on AI detection tools:** neither PC 550 nor the TSBA model policy addresses AI detectors. Integrity is handled through instruction, approved-tool limits, and disclosure. *[Not addressed]*

### Teacher training, curriculum integration, assessment, and implementation
- **Professional development is required by the model policy.** The Director of Schools "shall incorporate training programs on AI into professional development," focused on responsible use, PII, and data-privacy compliance (TSBA 4.214, lines 12-16). *[Model policy]*
- **Output verification is required of staff.** "Outputs from AI programs shall be verified by reliable sources and reviewed prior to use" (TSBA 4.214, p.2, lines 1-2). *[Model policy]*
- **Parent notice and an approved-programs list** are required (TSBA 4.214, lines 20-22). *[Model policy]*
- **A statutory reporting cycle governs implementation.** Under PC 550 sec.2, K-12 boards report compliance to TDOE by each July 1; the model policy operationalizes this with a June board report approved and submitted to TDOE by July 1 (TSBA 4.214, lines 23-26). *[Requirement + model policy]*

## Learning and Cognitive-Offloading Strategies

Tennessee's statute is structural rather than pedagogical, so its cognitive-offloading content lives in the model policy.

- **Approved-tools-only use limits unsupervised offloading** by design (TSBA 4.214, line 4). *[Model policy]*
- **Human verification of AI output is required** before use, reducing uncritical reliance (TSBA 4.214, p.2, lines 1-2). *[Model policy]*
- **Integrity instruction teaches the line between support and dependence.** Students learn "the difference between cheating and seeking support" and that "users assume responsibility for incorporating AI content responsibly" (TSBA 4.214, lines 17-19). *[Model policy]*
- PC 550 itself sets no cognitive-offloading doctrine; it requires that a policy exist and leaves the pedagogy to each board (PC 550, secs.1-3). *[Structural note]*

## Expected Graduate AI Profile

Because PC 550 mandates policy adoption but not content, a Tennessee graduate's floor is "some AI policy existed," with content varying by district.

- **Governed by a formally adopted AI policy since the 2024-25 school year.** Every Tennessee local board and charter school had to implement one and report compliance to TDOE (PC 550, sec.2-3). *Basis: PC 550. [Requirement — adoption only.]*
- **Likely an approved-tools-only experience**, where the district adopted TSBA-style language (TSBA 4.214, line 4). *Basis: TSBA 4.214.*
- **Integrity instruction that named the cheating/support distinction** and required responsible-use teaching before AI use (TSBA 4.214, lines 5-19). *Basis: TSBA 4.214.*
- **Parent-notified AI use** from an approved list (TSBA 4.214, lines 20-22). *Basis: TSBA 4.214.*
- **Higher-education implication:** Assume the *existence* of a district AI policy for recent Tennessee graduates, but treat the *content* as district-specific. The mandate guarantees a policy and a reporting habit, not a common scale or vocabulary.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** Tennessee law binds public universities directly. The UT board of trustees, the board of regents, and every state university governing board were required to adopt AI-use policies covering "students, faculty, and staff" by July 1, 2025, with public posting, legislative submission, and a noncompliance hearing before the joint government operations committee (PC 550, secs.1(b), 1(d)). A Tennessee public institution is not choosing whether to have an AI policy; it is complying with a statute.
- **Higher-education implication:** This is the dataset's clearest counter to "higher education debated while K-12 acted." Tennessee put universities on the *same* statutory timeline as schools, both effectively by summer 2025 / the 2024-25 year (PC 550, secs.1-2). Faculty at Tennessee public institutions can point to an existing, legally required institutional AI policy.
- **Higher-education implication:** Because the statute covers "faculty and staff," not just students (PC 550, sec.1(b)), Tennessee university faculty are themselves governed by an adopted AI policy, which shapes how they may use AI in instruction and assignments.
- **Higher-education implication:** PC 550 encourages, but does not require, institutions to "collaborate in the development and implementation of policies" (sec.1(c)), so there may be shared or similar language across Tennessee public institutions, worth checking for continuity with feeder-district policies.
- **Higher-education implication:** Incoming Tennessee students came from districts legally required to have AI policies and to teach responsible use, including the cheating-versus-support distinction (TSBA 4.214). A university integrity policy can build on that shared framing rather than introduce it.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Know your own obligation.** Tennessee public institutions should confirm their PC 550-required AI policy is adopted, posted, and submitted (PC 550, sec.1), and align it with incoming-student expectations.
- **Coordinate with feeder districts.** Because K-12 boards were required to adopt and annually report AI policies (PC 550, sec.2), and many used TSBA 4.214, institutions can align entry expectations with the approved-tools, disclosure, and integrity norms students were taught.
- **Build on the integrity framing.** Extend the "difference between cheating and seeking support" instruction (TSBA 4.214, lines 17-19) into course-level AI policy rather than starting from zero.
- **Assessment design:** State AI permission explicitly per assignment; the statute guarantees a policy existed but not a shared scale, so content varies.
- **Faculty governance:** Since PC 550 covers faculty and staff use (sec.1(b)), ensure faculty understand the institution's adopted policy governs their own AI use in instruction.

## Unique Aspects

- **A statute that binds public universities to adopt AI policies**, the only case in the dataset where enacted law reaches higher education directly, on the K-12 timeline (PC 550, sec.1).
- **An enforcement backstop.** Noncompliant governing boards must appear before the joint government operations committee within 60 days of the July 1, 2025 deadline (PC 550, sec.1(d)).
- **A three-sector sweep.** One act covers universities, local school boards, and charter schools with parallel requirements (PC 550, secs.1-3).
- **A UAPA carve-out.** The Uniform Administrative Procedures Act "does not apply" to the higher-ed policies (PC 550, sec.1(b)), a change from the introduced bill's rulemaking approach.
- **An annual K-12 compliance-reporting cycle** to TDOE each July 1 (PC 550, sec.2), operationalized in TSBA 4.214's June-report/July-submission steps.
- **A concrete K-12 model policy (TSBA 4.214)** showing approved-tools-only use, conditional staff disclosure, and integrity instruction naming the cheating/support line.

## Evidence Gaps and Uncertainties

- **The mandate is adoption, not content.** PC 550 requires policies to exist; it does not prescribe their substance (secs.1-3). Actual student and faculty experience varies by institution and district. The Requirement-level claim is the adoption-and-reporting obligation, not any specific rule. *[Scope of mandate]*
- **The TSBA copy is a district adoption, not TSBA's canonical file.** The document read is Cannon County's adopted version of 4.214 (adopted 07/11/24); TSBA's canonical file is members-only. Other districts' adopted versions may differ. Flag accordingly if quoted. *[Provenance]*
- **Date caution.** PC 550's own signature page reads "Approved this 11th day of March 2024," while the BillInfo page lists March 19; the chapter is preferred (sources.md). *[Minor]*
- **SB 1580 / Public Chapter 647 (2026) is not a school law.** It prohibits advertising or representing an AI system as a qualified mental-health professional, enforced under the Tennessee Consumer Protection Act ($5,000 per violation), not education code. The current data.js framing of it as restricting "AI tools used to assess or screen student mental health" with a "private right of action" needs correction. *[Flag for data.js]*
- **The TSBA AI Toolkit was not fetched** (sources.md); only the model policy was analyzed. *[Unresolved]*
- **No count of adopting universities or districts** is in the record. PC 550 required adoption, but this analysis cannot confirm how many boards actually complied by the deadline. *[Unresolved]*
