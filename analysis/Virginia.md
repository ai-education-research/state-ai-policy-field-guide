**Nature of the document (read before everything below):** Virginia's guidance is unusual in two ways. It was issued by the Governor's office under Executive Order 30, not by the education department, and it explicitly covers "Education in the Commonwealth" as a whole, K-12 and higher education together. It is guidance, not a rulebook; its Roles and Responsibilities are "an initial list... which will continue to be refined by the Advisory Group" (guidance p.~113). **The guidance itself is not a statewide mandate.** Its claims are Recommendation-level. Two enacted bills (HB 1186, SB 394, both effective July 1, 2026) create obligations, but their text is JS-rendered on Virginia LIS and was not machine-readable; their substance is corroborated only by secondary sources (sources.md), so they are treated as enacted-but-text-unverified.

## Executive Summary

Virginia's guidance is a recommendation-level, principles-based document that treats K-12 and higher education as one continuous system. It has three components: Guiding Principles, Strategies for Success, and Roles and Responsibilities (guidance p.~30). It does not use a leveled student-use scale. Its six guiding principles are Do no harm; Prioritize integrity; Augment, not replace humans; Harness AI to empower student success; Work in partnership; and Be constantly discerning (guidance pp.~38-65).

For a higher-education reader, Virginia is one of the strongest cases in the dataset, because higher education is written into the document from the first page and named as a governance partner throughout. The opening asserts that "the Commonwealth's colleges and universities lead the nation in technology research and development" (guidance p.~20). The "Work in partnership" principle states verbatim: "AI researchers and experts in Virginia colleges and universities and employers are key partners and guides around building the skills and knowledge required to be successful in the new economy" (guidance p.~60). And the Roles and Responsibilities section names SCHEV (the State Council of Higher Education for Virginia), the Virginia Community College System (VCCS), and "the boards of visitors/presidents/leadership teams at every public college and university" alongside the 131 K-12 school divisions (guidance pp.~117-120).

This makes Virginia's guidance genuinely K-16. It is not a K-12 document with a higher-education footnote; it assigns higher education formal roles and tasks VCCS with designing micro-badging and micro-credential training (guidance p.~152). Two 2026 statutes (HB 1186's AI Innovation in Education Pilot and SB 394's AI guidance requirement) build on it, but their enacted text could not be verified here.

## K–12 AI Policy and Guidance

### Primary themes and policy objectives
- **Education is a human endeavor AI cannot replace.** "AI should never fully replace teachers, who nurture students' critical thinking, values and character development" (guidance p.~15). *[Recommendation / stated philosophy]*
- **Six Guiding Principles** anchor the document (guidance pp.~38-65), led by "Do no harm" and "Prioritize integrity." *[Recommendation]*
- **Workforce and economic framing.** AI literacy is tied to preparing "a workforce that can leverage AI to its full potential" (guidance p.~12). *[Recommendation]*
- **A living, advisory-refined approach.** Roles will "continue to be refined by the Advisory Group," and principle six commits to constant revisiting ("not a one-and-done") (guidance pp.~65, 113). *[Recommendation]*

### Intended audiences and scope
- Scope is all of Virginia education, K-12 and higher education. Roles are assigned to VDOE and SCHEV/VCCS, K-12 division boards, and "every public college and university" (guidance pp.~117-120). *[Scope statement]*
- Audiences include state agencies, governing boards, higher-education programs and faculty, and school/classroom leaders (guidance pp.~117-121). *[Scope statement]*

### Permitted, restricted, and prohibited AI uses
- **There is no leveled permission scale and no statewide prohibition list.** Permission is governed by principle and by local condition-setting. The "Set The Right Conditions" strategy directs schools to "clearly outline the school or system's policies and protocols around data privacy, honor code, student code of conduct, acceptable use, and ethical considerations... including those related to plagiarism" (guidance p.~4, Strategies). *[Recommendation]*
- **The binding constraint is the "Do no harm" principle**, requiring compliance with EO30 and state/federal policy, privacy safeguards, and bias avoidance, and allowing AI "only when its use has the potential to contribute positive good/improvement to the status quo" (guidance p.~38). *[Recommendation]*

### Privacy, security, equity, accessibility, ethics, and academic integrity
- **"Do no harm" centers privacy, security, and anti-bias.** It requires "safeguarding of the privacy, security, and confidentiality of personally identifiable information" and ensuring algorithms are "not based on inherent biases that lead to discriminatory outcomes" (guidance p.~38). *[Recommendation]*
- **Equity of access** is built into "Harness AI to empower student success," which insists on "ensuring access for all learners" (guidance p.~54). *[Recommendation]*
- **Academic integrity is a standalone principle.** "Prioritize integrity" says education must "teach about morality, ethics, honor, cheating, and how artificial intelligence can lead to perverse and destructive outcomes" (guidance p.~44). *[Recommendation]*
- **Position on AI detection tools:** the guidance does not address AI detectors. Its integrity approach is principle- and condition-based (honor code, acceptable use, plagiarism protocols), not detection-based (guidance pp.~44, 4). *[Not addressed]*

### Teacher training, curriculum integration, assessment, and implementation
- **Strategies for Success are implementation-oriented**: Encourage Exploration (hands-on teacher experience), Provide Professional Development (workshops, micro-credentials, micro-badges), Spotlight Success, Host Stakeholder Conversations, and Set The Right Conditions (guidance pp.~72-4). *[Recommendation]*
- **VCCS is tasked with credential design.** State agencies "in partnership with the Virginia Community College System shall work in collaboration to design micro-badging and micro-credential training opportunities for the field" (guidance p.~152). *[Recommendation, a higher-ed role]*
- **It borrows another state's classroom resource.** The guidance cites Oregon's "Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) in K-12 Classrooms" resource "for K-12 and Higher Education" (guidance p.~185). *[Cross-state note]*
- **Roles and Responsibilities assign implementation across the system**, from state agencies to classroom leaders and higher-education faculty (guidance pp.~117-121). *[Recommendation]*

## Learning and Cognitive-Offloading Strategies

Virginia addresses over-reliance through its human-agency principles rather than a dedicated offloading section.

- **"Augment, not replace humans" is the core anti-offloading principle.** AI "should not overpower/over-ride the critical thinking, judgement, and morality of the learner" and "will never replace teachers who provide wisdom, context, feedback, empathy" (guidance p.~48). *[Recommendation]*
- **Integrity instruction targets misuse.** Teaching "morality, ethics, honor, cheating" is part of AI integration (guidance p.~44). *[Recommendation]*
- **Human judgment is reserved.** A faculty/staff role is to keep "human judgement" central (guidance p.~181). *[Recommendation]*
- **The document does not develop a dedicated cognitive-offloading doctrine or grade-band gating** (unlike Vermont, North Carolina, or North Dakota). Its offloading protection is the augment-not-replace principle plus condition-setting. *[Absence noted]*

## Expected Graduate AI Profile

These describe habits a Virginia graduate *may* bring. Because the guidance is voluntary and division-implemented, exposure varies.

- **Governed by locally set conditions, not a state scale.** A Virginia student's AI rules came from division-level honor codes, acceptable-use, and plagiarism protocols the guidance directed (guidance p.~4). *Basis: guidance p.~4.*
- **An augment-not-replace framing of AI**, if teachers followed the principles (guidance p.~48). *Basis: guidance p.~48.*
- **An integrity orientation tied to ethics and honor**, where the "Prioritize integrity" principle shaped instruction (guidance p.~44). *Basis: guidance p.~44.*
- **A workforce-and-economy framing** of why AI literacy matters (guidance p.~12). *Basis: guidance p.~12.*
- **Higher-education implication:** Treat these as ceilings of possibility, not guarantees. Virginia set no state scale, so faculty should expect division-level variance and no shared leveled vocabulary, but a shared "augment not replace" and integrity framing where the guidance took hold.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** Virginia is the clearest K-16 case in the dataset. Higher education is named as a governance partner from the first page (colleges and universities "lead the nation," guidance p.~20), the "Work in partnership" principle names "Virginia colleges and universities" as "key partners and guides" (guidance p.~60), and Roles and Responsibilities assign duties to SCHEV, VCCS, and "the boards of visitors/presidents/leadership teams at every public college and university" (guidance pp.~117-120). A Virginia institution is an assigned actor, not a bystander.
- **Higher-education implication:** VCCS is specifically tasked with designing micro-badging and micro-credential AI training (guidance p.~152). Virginia community colleges have a defined AI-workforce-credentialing role that four-year institutions can connect to.
- **Higher-education implication:** Because the guidance treats K-12 and higher education as one system, incoming Virginia students and their receiving institutions were addressed by the *same* document. Faculty can align entry expectations with a framework their own institution was named in.
- **Higher-education implication:** Virginia students may arrive with an *augment-not-replace* and *integrity-first* framing (guidance pp.~44, 48) but *no leveled AI-use vocabulary*, since the state set none. Faculty should teach a per-assignment permission structure explicitly.
- **Higher-education implication:** Two 2026 statutes (HB 1186 pilot, SB 394 guidance) build on this and take effect July 1, 2026 (sources.md), so Virginia's baseline is strengthening; entry assumptions should be revisited as those take effect.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Governance and partnership:** Virginia institutions should act on their named roles, engaging SCHEV/VCCS coordination and the "Work in partnership" mandate (guidance pp.~60, 117-120), and connect to VCCS micro-credential design (guidance p.~152).
- **Onboarding:** Give a short AI-literacy diagnostic at entry; division variance is high and no state scale anchors expectations.
- **Assessment design:** State AI permission explicitly per assignment. Build on the augment-not-replace and integrity framing Virginia students may share (guidance pp.~44, 48).
- **Academic-integrity processes:** Because the guidance is silent on AI detectors, set your own detector policy explicitly; favor honor-code and condition-based integrity, matching "Set The Right Conditions" (guidance p.~4).
- **Track the statutes:** Monitor HB 1186 and SB 394 (effective July 1, 2026, sources.md), which will require VDOE guidance and aligned school-board policies and may firm up the state baseline.

## Unique Aspects

- **An explicitly K-16, all-education framework** issued by the Governor's office under EO30, assigning formal AI roles to higher education (SCHEV, VCCS, every public college and university) alongside K-12 (guidance pp.~117-120).
- **A "Work in partnership" principle naming colleges and universities** as key partners and guides (guidance p.~60), a primary-confirmed higher-education hook.
- **A VCCS micro-credentialing mandate** for AI training (guidance p.~152).
- **A six-principle spine led by "Do no harm" and "Prioritize integrity"** (guidance pp.~38-65).
- **Cross-state borrowing.** Virginia points to Oregon's K-12 generative-AI resource "for K-12 and Higher Education" (guidance p.~185).
- **A national-security and R&D framing** grounded in Virginia's concentration of intelligence institutions and research universities (guidance pp.~20-21).
