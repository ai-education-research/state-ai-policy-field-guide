## Executive Summary

Hawaii has issued statewide K-12 AI guidance through the Hawaiʻi State Department of Education, in two parts (for employees and for students), but the documents are gated behind employee login and were not captured (sources.md). The landing page indicates the guidance covers "accountability, fairness/equity, human oversight, assessment, professional development, [and] data protection" (sources.md), which sketches a conventional responsible-use framework, but the specifics — principles, prohibitions, any student-use instrument — are not in the provided files.

For a higher-education reader, two facts are actionable. First, Hawaii is an active-guidance jurisdiction whose content is currently unverifiable from public documents. Second, the most notable higher-education angle is legislative and unverified: HB 1676 reportedly would situate an AI-in-education task force within the University of Hawaii (sources.md), which, if enacted, would be a direct higher-education hook. Neither the guidance content nor the bills could be confirmed in this pass.
