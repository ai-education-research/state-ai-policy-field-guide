It is explicitly recommendation-level. It repeatedly says "these are recommendations, not mandates" and "each PSU may determine its own guidelines and policies" (p.17). **Nothing in it is a statewide mandate on Public School Units (PSUs).** Every item below is a Recommendation, Instructional guidance, or Example unless explicitly noted.

## Executive Summary

North Carolina's guidance is a recommendation-level, unusually instrument-rich living document. It is organized around the five focus areas of the NC Digital Learning Plan and covers PK-13, meaning it deliberately runs from pre-kindergarten through grade 13 (the community-college and educator level). It carries a suite of named instruments a faculty member could lift directly: the "0 to Infinity" AI Use Continuum, the EVERY responsible-use checklist, the CRAFT 2.0 prompting framework, and a developmental grade-band AI-literacy progression.

The centerpiece for a higher-education reader is the "0 to Infinity: NCDPI AI Use Continuum v. 2.0," a five-level student AI-use scale (Level 0 AI Free through Level 4 AI Exploration). The document states plainly that it "is based on the work of Leon Furze, Dr. Mike Perkins, Dr. Jasper Roe and Dr. Jason McVaugh and has been adapted for the K12 environment" (p.31). This is a primary-source-confirmed lineage to the same peer-reviewed AI Assessment Scale that anchors the project's instruments library. North Carolina published both a teacher version (with assessment-design guidance) and a student version (with disclosure requirements).

Two more facts stand out. First, North Carolina takes a firm anti-detector stance, its "Use Great Caution with 'AI Detectors'" section says detectors "should never be used as the only factor" and flags false positives against non-native English speakers (p.32). Second, the framework's developmental gating is strong and specific: direct chatbot use "is not recommended" for grades PK-5, where children cannot yet separate make-believe from fact (p.17). The "AI Fluency" stage is explicitly labeled "Grades 9-13 and Educators," which is where the higher-education hook is written into the scope itself.

## K–12 AI Policy and Guidance

### Primary themes and policy objectives
- **AI literacy as a graduate necessity.** The document frames AI literacy as "a must-have for every graduate" and a durable skill (p.11), organized around the NC Digital Learning Plan's five focus areas (p.1). *[Recommendation]*
- **Human-centered co-creation.** The goal is "not to adapt education to AI, but to adapt education for the Intelligence Age," leveraging "what AI does best... to unlock what humans do best (innovation, empathy, judgment, collaboration)" (p.33). *[Recommendation / stated philosophy]*
- **Developmental appropriateness is a first-order design rule**, with distinct expectations by grade band (pp.17-19 and the AI-literacy stages). *[Recommendation]*
- **Local control governs adoption.** "Each PSU may determine its own guidelines and policies" (p.17). *[Scope statement]*

### Intended audiences and scope
- Scope is PK-13, explicitly including educators. The AI Fluency stage is titled "Working ALONGSIDE AI (Grades 9-13 and Educators)" (p.~53 of the doc's flow). *[Scope statement]*
- Authored by the NCDPI AI Guidelines Committee (lead contributor Vera Cubero) in collaboration with AI for Education, with a dedicated Office of Exceptional Children appendix (title page; Appendix D). *[Scope statement]*

### Permitted, restricted, and prohibited AI uses
- **The permission structure is the "0 to Infinity" AI Use Continuum v. 2.0** (Appendix B), offered as an adaptable acceptable-use scale, not a rule. Its five levels, per the educator version:
  - **Level 0 AI Free** — "students rely solely on their own thinking, knowledge, and skills... creates a clear baseline." *[Example / instrument]*
  - **Level 1 AI for Planning** — "AI is introduced as a thought partner in the earliest stages... a planning scaffold... without building the final product." *[Example]*
  - **Level 2 AI Collaboration** — "AI and students work together in a collaborative loop... students remain the decision-makers." *[Example]*
  - **Level 3 Full AI Use** — "students use AI as a strategic partner throughout the entire process... while maintaining critical oversight." *[Example]*
  - **Level 4 AI Exploration** — "the transformative zone, where students use AI as a launchpad for innovation." *[Example]*
- The document says the scale "should be referred to clarify what level, if any, is acceptable use of AI on a given task" and be "explicitly taught and posted" (p.31). *[Recommendation]*
- **A developmental restriction on chatbots.** For grades PK-5, "direct chatbot use is not recommended," and "students should not engage directly with the chatbots" (p.17). This is the document's clearest use restriction, framed as developmental protection, not prohibition-by-rule. *[Recommendation]*
- **AI-Resistant, AI-Assisted, and AI-Partnered assignment categories** are provided for assessment design (Update Log p.29). *[Instructional guidance]*

### Privacy, security, equity, accessibility, ethics, and academic integrity
- **Data privacy is a throughline.** Users must "never put PII (Personally Identifiable Information) into an AI tool" (AI Fluency stage), and a dedicated Office of Exceptional Children appendix governs AI use with student records and IEP drafting under human review (Appendix D). *[Recommendation]*
- **Accessibility has a named advisor** on the committee and dedicated AI-for-accessibility guidance for exceptional children (title page; Appendix D). *[Recommendation]*
- **Academic integrity is process- and disclosure-based.** The document teaches "mandatory AI-use disclosure statements," notes the US Copyright Office position that "purely AI-generated material is not copyrightable," and treats suspected over-reliance as "a teachable moment... rather than a 'gotcha'" (pp.29, 32). *[Recommendation]*
- **Position on AI detection tools:** strongly cautionary. "AI detectors have proven not to be dependable, therefore they should never be used as the only factor when determining if a student 'cheated'," citing false positives for non-native English speakers and creative writers and false negatives for skilled AI users (p.32). *[Recommendation — a caution against detectors]*
- **Environmental impact is named**, "every AI prompt carries an environmental" cost (p.~678 of the flow). *[Instructional guidance]*

### Teacher training, curriculum integration, assessment, and implementation
- **A developmental AI-literacy progression** structures curriculum integration (pp.~1860-1980 of the flow):
  - **AI Awareness (PK-5):** learn ABOUT AI, literacy-first, no direct chatbot use. *[Recommendation]*
  - **AI Literacy (6-8):** learn ABOUT and WITH AI, heavily scaffolded chatbot use under teacher direction. *[Recommendation]*
  - **AI Fluency (9-13 and Educators):** working ALONGSIDE AI as a "thinking partner," advanced prompting, iterative co-creation, metacognition portfolios. *[Recommendation]*
- **Named support tools:** the EVERY framework (below), the CRAFT 2.0 prompting framework with "Power-Ups" (Appendix C), and multiple custom GPTs and interactive teaching guides (pp.29, Appendix C). *[Instructional guidance]*
- **Assessment guidance emphasizes process evidence.** "Collect outlines, drafts, or chat links at Levels 2-4 to assess thinking, not just the final product," and "differentiate gradually," starting the year at Level 0 or 1 (Appendix B). *[Instructional guidance]*
- **Implementation support** runs through regional Digital Teaching and Learning Consultants and the DTL Hub (About this Resource). *[Recommendation]*

## Learning and Cognitive-Offloading Strategies

North Carolina engages offloading through both its scale design and its developmental gating.

- **Level 0 AI Free exists precisely to preserve unaided cognition.** It builds "essential cognitive habits, such as personal problem-solving, self-reliance, and academic integrity" and "creates a clear baseline" (Appendix B). *[Instructional guidance]*
- **The PK-5 no-chatbot recommendation is an anti-offloading control for early learners**, grounded in the argument that "young children are not developmentally ready to critically evaluate AI outputs" and that lifelike chatbots can "blur that boundary" between fantasy and fact (p.17). *[Recommendation]*
- **The EVERY framework builds verification into every AI interaction.** Created by AI for Education and Vera Cubero (NCDPI), it is "Evaluate, Verify, Engage, Revise, and remember that You are responsible for the final product" (p.29). *[Instructional guidance]*
- **Assessment shifts to process evidence** so that thinking, not output, is graded (Appendix B). *[Instructional guidance]*
- **It reframes over-reliance as teachable, not punitive.** Suspected over-dependence "should be viewed as a teachable moment to reinforce the appropriate partnership with AI tools" (p.32). *[Recommendation]*
- The document also acknowledges the flip side, noting that when routine generation "can now be offloaded to AI, teachers and students can spend more time on deeper learning" (p.~1106 of the flow), so offloading is managed, not uniformly resisted. *[Instructional guidance]*

## Expected Graduate AI Profile

These describe habits a North Carolina graduate *may* bring. Because the guidance is voluntary and PSU-implemented, exposure varies.

- **Familiarity with a five-level, AIAS-derived permission scale.** A student in an adopting PSU will recognize that assignments carry an explicit level from "AI Free" to "AI Exploration," posted and taught (Appendix B). *Basis: Appendix B, adaptable instrument.*
- **A grade-scaled AI-literacy trajectory.** Under the developmental progression, a graduate likely had no direct chatbot use before upper elementary, scaffolded use in middle school, and "AI Fluency" co-creation in high school (pp.17-19). *Basis: AI-literacy stages.*
- **A disclosure-and-verification habit** via the EVERY framework and mandatory disclosure modeling (p.29). *Basis: p.29.*
- **An expectation that AI detectors are unreliable and not decisive** (p.32). *Basis: p.32.*
- **Higher-education implication:** Treat these as ceilings of possibility, not guarantees. Because adoption is by PSU, faculty should expect a range, but North Carolina's instruments are specific enough that adopting-PSU students may arrive with genuinely transferable vocabulary.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** North Carolina's student-use scale shares its lineage with the peer-reviewed AI Assessment Scale (Furze, Perkins, Roe, McVaugh; p.31). A university can adopt the same source and expect adopting-PSU students to recognize the level structure, from "AI Free" to "AI Exploration," rather than starting from zero.
- **Higher-education implication:** The framework already extends to "Grades 9-13 and Educators" (AI Fluency stage), so its top band is explicitly postsecondary-facing. Community colleges and early-college programs in North Carolina may find the K-12 and entry-level college vocabulary already aligned.
- **Higher-education implication:** Students were taught that *AI detectors are unreliable and biased against non-native English writers* (p.32). Institutions relying on AI-detection software may face students explicitly trained to distrust it, a position the state document takes.
- **Higher-education implication:** Students from adopting PSUs may arrive expecting AI permission to be *specified per assignment* and *posted*, and expecting to submit process evidence (drafts, chat links) rather than only final products (Appendix B). A silent or blanket syllabus will read as less clear than what they experienced.
- **Higher-education implication:** The developmental gating means younger cohorts had *deliberately limited* early AI exposure. A faculty member should not assume long AI histories; formal fluency work is concentrated in high school and up.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Assessment design:** Adopt an explicit per-assignment AI-permission scale and consider using the same Furze/Perkins/Roe/McVaugh AIAS source (p.31) for continuity. Preserve Level 0 AI-Free assessments where core-skill demonstration is the goal (Appendix B).
- **Academic-integrity processes:** Favor process- and disclosure-based integrity, mirroring EVERY and the state's disclosure norm (p.29). Do not rely on AI detectors, which the students' own K-12 guidance calls undependable (p.32).
- **Onboarding:** Give a short AI-literacy diagnostic at entry. The state's grade-band progression (Awareness → Literacy → Fluency) is a ready rubric for placing students.
- **Prompt-literacy scaffolding:** Build on the CRAFT 2.0 framework and EVERY checklist students may already know (pp.29, Appendix C), extending toward source verification and citation.
- **Community-college alignment:** Because the framework already names grade 13, two-year and early-college programs can align entry AI expectations directly to the state's Fluency band.

## Unique Aspects

- **The "0 to Infinity" AI Use Continuum v. 2.0**, a five-level (0-4) student scale with both educator and student versions, primary-source-attributed to the Furze/Perkins/Roe/McVaugh AI Assessment Scale (p.31; Appendix B). A clear anchor for the project's Perkins-spine instruments library.
- **A PK-13 scope that names grade 13 and educators**, extending the framework into the community-college and educator space explicitly.
- **A developmental grade-band progression** (AI Awareness PK-5, AI Literacy 6-8, AI Fluency 9-13) with a strong PK-5 no-direct-chatbot recommendation grounded in child development (p.17).
- **The EVERY responsible-use framework** (Evaluate, Verify, Engage, Revise, You) and the **CRAFT 2.0 prompting framework** with Power-Ups (pp.29, Appendix C), both with interactive custom-GPT tools.
- **A firm anti-detector section** with a rationale and a "teachable moment" alternative (p.32).
- **A dedicated Office of Exceptional Children AI acceptable-use appendix** (Appendix D, launched Nov 2025) governing AI in IEP drafting under human review.
- **A genuine living-document practice** with a detailed public Update Log tracking every revision (pp.2-3).
