**Nature of the document.** The brief is **voluntary** — it "intends to provide an example of foundational guiding principles" and "can serve as an example to districts creating their own AI usage/integration policies" (KDE-AI-Guidance-Brief.pdf, pp.4, 6). It flows from the KETS Master Plan 2024-2030 growth goal. Nothing in it is a mandate; sources.md records no enacted statute. Citations are by page.

## Executive Summary

Kentucky's brief is distinctive for two moves. First, it deliberately **normalizes AI as one emerging technology among many** — framed through "Consistency, Balance, Normalization" — arguing that "treating it differently than other emerging technologies can hinder its effective integration and create unnecessary anxieties" (KDE-AI-Guidance-Brief.pdf, p.2). Second, it introduces **three application-and-research paradigms** grounded in academic literature (Ouyang & Jiao, 2021): **AI-directed** (learner as recipient), **AI-supported** (learner as collaborator), and **AI-empowered** (learner as leader) (p.12). These paradigms are its signature instrument and map cleanly onto course design.

The brief is organized around the KETS Master Plan verbs — Encourage, Engage, Empower — plus "Safe and Responsible" use and "Humans in the Loop" (pp.7-8), and it lists seven guiding principles (Education-first; Equity and Reduction of Bias; Transparency, Accountability and Disclosure; Digital Citizenship and Computer Science; Privacy and Data Protection; Ethical AI Use; Data Quality and Integrity, pp.9-10). For a higher-education reader, the paradigm model is the useful export, and Kentucky's "Engage" principle explicitly names partnership "with universities, research institutions, and AI vendors" (p.7), with the University of Kentucky's CELT referenced as a resource (p.11). The brief is voluntary, so student exposure varies by district.

## K–12 AI Policy and Guidance

### Primary themes and policy objectives
- **Normalize AI as an emerging technology** to defuse fear: Consistency, Balance, Normalization (KDE-AI-Guidance-Brief.pdf, p.2). *[Recommendation]*
- **KETS Master Plan goal:** "Encourage, engage and empower the safe and responsible uses of Artificial Intelligence... ensuring humans remain in the loop with strong AI implementations" (p.4, p.7). *[Recommendation]*
- **AI complements, not replaces, educators:** "AI is not a means to replace educators but could complement their strengths, automate routine tasks, and enhance their ability to differentiate" (p.5). *[Recommendation]*

### Intended audiences and scope
- Addresses KDE, districts, and school communities, with distinct "Policy-in-Action Spotlights" for CIOs, Digital Learning Coaches, STLP, Library Media Specialists, and Online/Virtual programs (KDE-AI-Guidance-Brief.pdf, pp.4-13). *[Scope]*
- Explicitly "not a one-size-fits-all approach"; districts retain autonomy (pp.5, 10). *[Scope]*

### Permitted, restricted, and prohibited AI uses
- **No statewide permission scale**; the brief models principles for local policy. *[Not a mandate]*
- **Seven guiding questions for AI integration** function as a use screen — e.g., PII exposure, whether the tool is in the KETS/KDE ecosystem or an unfamiliar third party, over-broad permissions, "freemium" data-as-payment models, ethics of the output, need for review against hallucinations/bias, and consulting a supervisor about unseen risks (p.3). *[Recommendation / screen]*
- **Disclosure rule:** "It is best practice to disclose the use of generative AI tools in all formal publications, presentations, outputs... However, the informal use of generative AI does not require citation" (p.9). *[Recommendation]*

### Privacy, security, equity, accessibility, ethics, and academic integrity
- **Privacy:** adhere to FERPA, COPPA, and Kentucky HB 5 (2015); treat student/educator data with confidentiality (KDE-AI-Guidance-Brief.pdf, p.10). *[Recommendation]*
- **Equity/bias:** "AI systems should not perpetuate or exacerbate disparities"; check GenAI content for bias and inaccuracy (p.9). *[Recommendation]*
- **Ethics:** references UNESCO's AI Ethics framework and the Partnership on AI for evaluating tools (p.10). *[Recommendation]*
- **Transparency/accountability:** people "should be able to understand how AI systems make decisions" and there should be "ways to hold them accountable" (p.9). *[Recommendation]*
- **Academic integrity:** students and teachers "should give credit to sources and tools and honestly present work that is genuinely their own" (Ethical AI Use, p.10). A specific AI-detection-tool position is **Not found in the provided documents.** *[Recommendation / limited]*

### Teacher training, curriculum integration, assessment, and implementation
- **TeachAI three-stage framework:** develop policies; help educators learn about AI; use AI to improve outcomes — pursued "simultaneously" (KDE-AI-Guidance-Brief.pdf, p.4). *[Recommendation]*
- **Digital Citizenship + Computer Science:** AI literacy connected to STLP (Student Technology Leadership Program), digital citizenship, and CS access in all KY schools (p.10). *[Recommendation]*
- **Post-Adoption Monitoring and Evaluation:** uses the "Emerging Technology Adoption Framework: For PK-12 Education" centered on "investigation, fidelity, and effectiveness" (p.13). *[Recommendation]*
- **Professional learning** via partnerships with "universities, research institutions, and AI vendors" and communities of practice (p.7). *[Recommendation]*

## Learning and Cognitive-Offloading Strategies

- The **three paradigms** (AI-directed/supported/empowered, p.12) are Kentucky's central lens on how much cognitive agency stays with the learner — recipient, collaborator, or leader — which directly frames offloading as a design choice. *[Instructional guidance]*
- **Human-in-the-loop (HITL)** is a named tenet: AI "should not operate autonomously without human oversight and control, especially in applications where AI decisions could have significant consequences" (p.8). *[Recommendation]*
- A caution against uncritical AI instruction: "GenAI-directed instruction could turn out to be 'just plain bad instruction,' or instruction centered around information hallucinations if left on its own and not guided by expert teachers" (p.6). *[Instructional guidance]*
- **Empower** framing: technology should "amplify human capacity for collaboration, creativity, and communication," not "support outdated education strategies" (p.8). *[Recommendation]*
- The STLP spotlight notes challenges "that require student original work still encourage AI use for inspiration" (p.6). *[Example]*
- A developed cognitive-science treatment (metacognition, over-reliance research) is **Not found in the provided documents**; Kentucky's guard is the paradigm model plus HITL. *[Limited]*

## Expected Graduate AI Profile

- **Uneven by district (voluntary).** Exposure depends on district adoption. *Basis: pp.4, 10.*
- **Where adopted**, Kentucky students may bring: a "treat AI like other tech" normalized stance (p.2); a disclosure habit for formal AI use (p.9); AI-literacy grounding via STLP/digital citizenship (p.10); and familiarity with AI framed along the directed/supported/empowered spectrum (p.12).
- **Higher-education implication:** Kentucky-shaped students may arrive with a calm, normalized view of AI (neither hype nor panic) and a sense that their role can range from recipient to leader depending on the task. Assume variance given voluntariness.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** Kentucky's three paradigms (p.12) map directly onto course design — "who is directing the learning, and does AI change that answer?" Faculty can adopt AI-directed/supported/empowered as a shared vocabulary for setting per-activity expectations.
- **Higher-education implication:** Kentucky explicitly names universities as partners in AI professional learning (p.7) and references the University of Kentucky's CELT (p.11). Kentucky institutions are already positioned in the state's AI-education network.
- **Higher-education implication:** The disclosure rule distinguishing formal (cite) from informal (no citation) AI use (p.9) is a nuanced model a college could adopt to avoid over-broad citation mandates.
- **Higher-education implication:** The "normalize AI as one tool among many" stance (p.2) suggests Kentucky students may resist both AI-panic and AI-hype framings; institutions can meet them with a measured, integration-focused posture.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Assessment design:** Use the directed/supported/empowered paradigm (p.12) to specify, per assignment, how much cognitive leadership stays with the student.
- **Academic integrity:** Adopt Kentucky's formal-vs-informal disclosure distinction (p.9) and the "genuinely their own work" standard (p.10); pair with the seven guiding questions (p.3) as a tool-vetting screen.
- **Governance and procurement:** Reuse the seven guiding questions (p.3) and the Emerging Technology Adoption Framework (investigation/fidelity/effectiveness, p.13) for institutional AI-tool evaluation.
- **Faculty development:** Partner with in-state universities' teaching centers (e.g., UK CELT, p.11), extending Kentucky's "Engage" model (p.7) into higher education.
- **Advancing learning rather than replacing thinking:** Center HITL (p.8) and the caution that unguided AI instruction can be "just plain bad instruction" (p.6).

## Unique Aspects

- **Three application-and-research paradigms** — AI-directed, AI-supported, AI-empowered (learner as recipient/collaborator/leader), grounded in Ouyang & Jiao (2021) and framed alongside SAMR/TCoP (KDE-AI-Guidance-Brief.pdf, p.12).
- **A deliberate "normalize AI as an emerging technology" stance** (Consistency, Balance, Normalization, p.2) intended to defuse fear.
- **Seven guiding questions for AI integration** as a practical vetting screen (p.3).
- **KETS-Master-Plan framing** (Encourage/Engage/Empower/Safe and Responsible/School Efficiency/Humans in the Loop, pp.7-8).
- **A formal-vs-informal disclosure distinction** (p.9) and a research-based post-adoption evaluation framework (p.13).

## Evidence Gaps and Uncertainties

- **Voluntariness.** District adoption and student exposure are **Not found in the provided documents.**
- **No enacted statute** (sources.md: "No enacted statute identified").
- **No AI-detection-tool position, student-use permission scale, or grade-band progression** appears; Kentucky's model is paradigm- and principle-based rather than a leveled scale. *[Limited]*
- **The three paradigms describe learner roles, not permission levels** — they are an instructional lens, not a per-assignment AI-use scale like Oklahoma's or New Mexico's. Do not conflate. *[Clarification]*
- **Higher-education references** (universities as partners, UK CELT) are soft hooks, not binding provisions (pp.7, 11). *[Note]*
