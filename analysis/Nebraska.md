## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** Nebraska is a no-state-guidance jurisdiction. A faculty member cannot assume any shared AI vocabulary, permission scale, or integrity norm among incoming Nebraska students, because the state established none.
- **Higher-education implication:** The absence cuts both ways. Nebraska students were not taught to distrust AI detectors (unlike students from states with explicit anti-detector guidance), and they were not taught a leveled per-assignment model either. Faculty should treat prior AI instruction as unknown and district-specific.
- **Higher-education implication:** Because even NDE's own AI activity was staff-facing PD rather than student guidance (sources.md), Nebraska teachers may have been more recently oriented to AI than their students. The likeliest common ground is low and uneven, arguing for an explicit, from-scratch AI orientation at entry.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Onboarding:** Assume no common baseline. Give every incoming Nebraska student an explicit AI-use orientation and a short literacy diagnostic rather than relying on prior instruction.
- **Assessment design:** State AI permission explicitly per assignment. Nebraska students are unlikely to have met a standardized scale and will not carry a shared vocabulary into class.
- **Academic-integrity processes:** Set integrity expectations from first principles. Do not assume Nebraska students were taught either to use or to distrust AI detectors.
- **Equity:** Because district-level variance is unbounded, watch for gaps between students from well-resourced districts and those from districts that did little with AI, and provide a common floor of access and instruction on campus.
