**Nature of the evidence.** Kansas is an **absence state at the agency level**: the Kansas State Department of Education (KSDE) "has not issued any guidance to districts regarding AI technology" (statement attributed to KSDE, reported by CRPE, October 2023, per sources.md). No KSDE guidance document exists in the provided files. Two later developments are recorded but **not captured as documents**: a University of Kansas-authored "Framework for Responsible AI Integration in Pre-K-20 Education" presented to the State Board in February 2026 (a KU product, not KSDE-issued), and the Lawrence USD 497 Gaggle surveillance lawsuit (no public docket retrieved). Claims below are cited to sources.md; the underlying documents were not obtained.

## Executive Summary

Kansas has no state education-agency AI guidance. The KSDE statement of no guidance is now nearly three years old (October 2023), and while Kansas is "no longer inactive," the substantive activity is not agency guidance. In February 2026 the State Board heard a **University-of-Kansas-authored "Framework for Responsible AI Integration in Pre-K-20 Education"** (Basham, Vasquez, Fulchini Scruggs) — a KU product spanning K-12 through higher education, not a KSDE-issued framework (sources.md). Separately, Kansas is home to the marquee K-12 AI-surveillance case: the Lawrence USD 497 **Gaggle lawsuit**, filed August 2025, raising First and Fourth Amendment and Kansas Student Publications Act claims over AI monitoring of student communications (sources.md).

For a higher-education reader, three things matter. First, there is **no shared Kansas AI baseline**, so incoming Kansas students' AI experience is entirely district-dependent. Second, the KU framework is explicitly **"Pre-K-20"** — a higher-education-authored attempt at a K-through-college through-line, which is directly relevant to institutions but is not yet state policy and was not captured. Third, the Gaggle litigation is a live constitutional test of AI surveillance in schools, with clear implications for campus monitoring, proctoring, and wellness-flagging tools.

## Expected Graduate AI Profile

- **No common baseline; extreme variance.** With no KSDE guidance, incoming Kansas students' AI exposure depends entirely on their district. *Basis: sources.md (no KSDE guidance).*
- **Higher-education implication:** Do not assume any shared Kansas AI baseline. District-to-district variation will be large, and some Kansas students attended districts (e.g., Lawrence) where AI surveillance tools like Gaggle were in use and litigated — an experience of AI as monitoring rather than as a learning tool.

## Implications for Higher Education

- **Higher-education implication:** The absence of a state baseline is the core message for Kansas: institutions should expect wide within-cohort variation in AI literacy and governance experience, and should not infer any common preparation.
- **Higher-education implication:** The **KU "Pre-K-20" framework** (sources.md) is a higher-education-authored attempt at a K-through-college AI through-line. Kansas institutions — especially KU — are effectively originating the state's most substantive AI-education thinking. This is a direct higher-education hook worth engaging, though it is not yet state policy and was not captured.
- **Higher-education implication:** The Lawrence Gaggle case is the clearest constitutional test of AI surveillance in K-12, and campus AI monitoring, proctoring, and wellness-flagging tools sit on the same legal ground. Institutions using such tools should track the case regardless of their state.
- **Higher-education implication:** Kansas students from districts that used AI surveillance may associate school AI primarily with monitoring and discipline; onboarding should distinguish institutional AI-for-learning tools from surveillance.

## Recommended Higher-Education Response

- **Onboarding:** Given no state baseline, run an explicit AI-literacy orientation and assume high variance among Kansas students.
- **Academic integrity and assessment design:** Provide clear per-assignment AI-permission structures; Kansas students are unlikely to arrive with a shared AI-use framework.
- **Governance and student rights:** Treat the Gaggle litigation as a caution for institutional AI-surveillance, proctoring, and monitoring tools — review First/Fourth Amendment and student-privacy exposure, and be transparent about any monitoring.
- **Engage the KU framework:** Kansas institutions should engage the KU "Pre-K-20" framework as a potential K-20 through-line, since it explicitly reaches into higher education (sources.md).
- **Advancing learning rather than replacing thinking:** With no state model to inherit, the institution must supply its own learning-preservation expectations.
