# US State-Level K-12 Artificial Intelligence Policy: A Primary-Source Dataset for Higher Education Faculty

*Compiled July 28, 2026. Each jurisdiction uses six consistent fields: **FRAMEWORK · INSTRUMENTS · PROHIBITIONS · CLASSROOM GUIDANCE · LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE STATUS · HIGHER ED HOOK.** Where a field genuinely has no content, that is stated. Unverified claims are flagged explicitly so they can be marked as such in publication rather than asserted.*

---

## TL;DR
- **As of mid-2026, 34 states plus Puerto Rico have issued official department-of-education K-12 AI guidance** (AI for Education, October 2025), with FutureEd/Working Educators citing "35+" as of June 2026 — and the field has shifted decisively from voluntary guidance to **enacted mandates** (Ohio, Tennessee, Idaho, Maryland, Oklahoma, Virginia, Utah, California).
- **The information that actually tells you what students were taught lives in the instruments, not the principle lists:** scaffolded student-use scales (Washington's 5-step, New Mexico's 5-level, Oklahoma's 5-level 0–4, Wyoming's 4-stage continuum, Louisiana's 4-tier, Georgia's red/yellow/green, Puerto Rico's 5-step), grade-band gates (Vermont, Rhode Island, North Dakota), tool-evaluation rubrics (Georgia, Maryland, Colorado), and maturity rubrics (Michigan's Investigating→Implementing→Innovating across eight domains).
- **The policy-practice gap remains the dominant reality:** 82% of teachers received no formal AI guidance across work tasks and 34% received none at all (Gallup/Walton "Teaching for Tomorrow," 2,069 teachers, Feb–Mar 2026). Several commonly repeated claims are also overstated — New Jersey's higher-ed bill develops *model curricula*, it does not mandate universities offer degrees; Arizona's university hook (HB 4040) died in committee; Georgia's "seven principles" are full sentences, not the short labels in circulation.

---

## Key Findings

**The authoritative count.** AI for Education counts **34 states plus Puerto Rico** with official department-of-education guidance (updated October 28, 2025; as cited by K-12 Dive: "As of October 2025, 34 states and Puerto Rico had released AI guidance for schools"). FutureEd/Working Educators cite "35+" as of June 2026; Ballotpedia lists 31 as of January 2026; NASBE says 34 by end of 2025. The honest headline is **"34 states plus Puerto Rico as of late 2025, with several trackers citing 35+ by mid-2026,"** the exact figure depending on whether one counts state-endorsed third-party documents (Michigan, Arizona) and mandate states whose guidance is not yet published (Idaho, Maryland).

**From guidance to law.** Ohio (HB 96), Tennessee (Public Chapter 550), Idaho (SB 1227), Maryland (SB 720/Ch. 634), Oklahoma (SB 1734), Virginia (HB 1186), Utah (HB 218), and California (AB 2148) have enacted binding requirements. **Ohio is the first state to convert AI policy from a suggestion into a statutory duty** for every district.

**Federal frame.** Executive Order 14277, "Advancing Artificial Intelligence Education for American Youth" (signed April 23, 2025), established the White House Task Force on AI Education chaired by the Director of the Office of Science and Technology Policy and including the Secretaries of Education and Labor. The **U.S. Department of Education finalized its AI grant-priority rule April 13, 2026** (effective May 13, 2026), prioritizing discretionary grants for AI-literacy projects and defining "computer science" to include machine learning and AI.

**2029 PISA.** AI literacy will be assessed internationally for the first time in the OECD's 2029 PISA cycle, in an innovative domain the OECD named **"Media and Artificial Intelligence Literacy (MAIL)," led by the Australian Council for Educational Research (ACER)**; a first-draft framework was issued January 2026, with results expected December 2031. It will measure how AI systems function, critical evaluation of outputs, recognition of bias/privacy risks, and ethical use.

**Policy-practice gap.** Gallup/Walton Family Foundation, "Teaching for Tomorrow: Closing the Expectations Gap" (2,069 U.S. public K-12 teachers, Feb 9–Mar 2, 2026; released May 27, 2026; ±2.5 pts): **82% received no formal guidance** across work tasks ("fewer than one in 10 teachers receiving formal guidance on any specific activity"); **34% received no guidance at all; 48% only informal guidance;** 58% received no guidance on AI grading/feedback; 69% none on one-on-one instruction/tutoring. Higher-needs schools were less likely to receive guidance. (An earlier Gallup/Walton finding: weekly AI-using teachers save ~5.9 hours/week, ~6 weeks/year.)

## Details — State by State

### Alabama
- **FRAMEWORK:** "AI Policy Template for Local Education Agencies," Alabama State Department of Education (ALSDE), June 2024, from the November 2023 ALSDE AI Summit. **Eight foundational pillars: Strategy, Governance, Data Privacy and Security, Procurement, Implementation, Competency Development, Risk Management, and Utility and Effectiveness.** Risk framework based on NIST standards.
- **INSTRUMENTS:** Model policy template; Appendix A includes specific AI-vendor contract language; requires AI Governance Committees, regular compliance audits, corrective-action plans, and a process for contesting AI-generated grades.
- **PROHIBITIONS:** Prohibits unauthorized use of school data to train AI models; restricts AI-powered surveillance and behavioral tracking; requires human verification of AI-generated content and grading.
- **CLASSROOM GUIDANCE:** AI supplements, not replaces, instruction; rules for citing AI use; human-in-the-loop required.
- **LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE:** HB 329 (enacted 2026) requires all students to complete an approved computer science course including AI instruction to graduate, beginning with the class of 2032; adds AI to the state CS definition.
- **HIGHER ED HOOK:** None specific identified.

### Alaska
- **FRAMEWORK:** "Alaska K12 AI Framework," Alaska Department of Education & Early Development (AKDEED) with the Alaska AI K12 Advisory Group, released October 2025. **Seven guiding principles: Human-Centered, Fair Access, Transparency, Oversight, Security, Ethical Use, and Cultural Responsiveness.**
- **INSTRUMENTS:** Recommendations (not mandates); vendor-vetting/procurement guidance; companion resource website.
- **PROHIBITIONS:** Warns against blanket bans; requires AI tools affirm rather than diminish Alaska Native cultures; warns against misappropriation of local cultural context.
- **CLASSROOM GUIDANCE:** Academic-integrity/citation framework; references prompting frameworks; aligns with Alaska Standards for Culturally Responsive Schools; includes an AI Use Disclaimer modeling transparency.
- **LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE:** No enacted statute identified.
- **HIGHER ED HOOK:** None specific identified.

### Arizona
- **FRAMEWORK:** Guidance authored by the **Arizona Institute for Education and the Economy (Northern Arizona University)**, not the state agency. Covers understanding GenAI, teaching/learning, AI literacy, responsible/ethical implementation. **Five key ethical considerations: bias, misinformation, intellectual property, data privacy, and equitable access.** Three-stage implementation (Create a Strong Foundation, Build Momentum, Continuous Improvement); incorporates AI for Education's "Top 6 Questions for GenAI EdTech Providers."
- **INSTRUMENTS:** "Human agency and oversight imperative" throughout; vendor-question checklist.
- **PROHIBITIONS:** None enumerated as explicit prohibitions.
- **CLASSROOM GUIDANCE:** References state academic standards and local district examples.
- **LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE:** **HB 4005** ("Artificial intelligence; course; public schools") would have required AI instruction for grades 3, 6, 8, 10, 12 — **vetoed by Gov. Katie Hobbs June 19, 2026** (objections that charter schools were not held to the same standard and that curriculum should be developed by educators). Gov. Hobbs vetoed all three AI bills passed that session.
- **HIGHER ED HOOK:** **HB 4040** ("Public schools; universities; AI policies," Rep. Nickolas Kupper) would require districts, charter schools, AND universities overseen by the Arizona Board of Regents to establish policies addressing unauthorized student AI use by July 1, 2027, with annual reports to the Department of Education (K-12) and Board of Regents (universities). Introduced June 13, 2026; **died in House Rules Committee.** Scope confirmed via official summary and code citation (§15-1626.02, university chapter); verbatim statutory text not extracted.

### Arkansas
- **FRAMEWORK:** No Arkansas Department of Education K-12-specific AI classroom guidance document identified. Activity is (a) the **Virtual Arkansas** planning guide (a district/consortium resource, not an ADE statewide framework) and (b) the state's government-wide **AI and Analytics Center of Excellence (AI CoE)**.
- **INSTRUMENTS / PROHIBITIONS / CLASSROOM GUIDANCE:** None K-12-specific from ADE.
- **LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE:** The AI CoE, launched June 2024 by Gov. Sarah Huckabee Sanders, delivered an initial report February 7, 2025 and a comprehensive effectiveness/safety report; recommendations are **government-wide, not education-specific** (Chief AI Officer, NIST-based evaluation, data classification/risk management, workforce training). Education Commission of the States lists an "Arkansas (2025)" task-force report among those prioritizing AI-literacy curricular frameworks, educator PD, equitable access, and data protection.
- **HIGHER ED HOOK:** Workforce/higher-ed touched only at the state-government-strategy level.
- **VERIFICATION FLAG:** Confirmed ADE has **not** issued a standalone K-12 classroom AI guidance document; the substantive state activity is the government-wide AI CoE work plus the Virtual Arkansas planning resource.

### California
- **FRAMEWORK:** (1) "Learning with AI, Learning about AI," California Department of Education (CDE), September 2023 — non-binding guidance incorporating the **"5 Big Ideas of AI"** framework and integrating AI with California CS standards. (2) **"Model Policy: Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Education," posted to the CDE website June 25, 2026,** developed by the California AI in Education Working Group convened under **SB 1288 (Becker, 2024).**
- **INSTRUMENTS:** The model policy is a ready-to-adapt template covering legal, ethical, and logistical considerations for LEAs/charter schools of any size.
- **PROHIBITIONS:** See AB 2148.
- **CLASSROOM GUIDANCE:** Emphasis on human relationships; guidance on developing AI (not just using tools). CDE guidance explicitly disclaims mandatory compliance (Educ. Code §33308.5).
- **LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE:** **AB 2148 (Muratsuchi), signed by Gov. Newsom June 30, 2026, Chapter 45, Statutes of 2026, effective January 1, 2027** — adds Education Code §98 defining a public school employee and a contractor providing services in a public school each to mean a **"natural person,"** closing off treating an AI system as the employee/contractor where the law requires one. It is **not** a ban on AI for lesson planning, grading support, translation, or student practice. It was substantially narrowed from an initial sweeping version. AB 3216 (2024) separately required smartphone-limitation policies by July 1, 2026.
- **HIGHER ED HOOK:** AB 2148's earlier drafts reached higher education and employment-decision restrictions but were removed; the enacted version is K-12 Education Code only.

### Colorado
- **FRAMEWORK:** "Colorado Roadmap for AI in K-12 Education," August 2024 (with Colorado Education Initiative). Emphasizes local control, student voice, equitable access.
- **INSTRUMENTS:** Two named working-group tools — the **"If and How Checklist"** and the **"AI Resource Evaluation Tool."**
- **PROHIBITIONS:** None enumerated.
- **CLASSROOM GUIDANCE:** AI-literacy definition; continuous adaptation; recommendations for districts and statewide organizations.
- **LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE:** No enacted K-12 education AI statute (Colorado's SB 205 consumer AI act is separate, not education-specific).
- **HIGHER ED HOOK:** None specific identified.

### Connecticut
- **FRAMEWORK:** "Guidance on Artificial Intelligence (AI)," Connecticut Commission for Educational Technology. Covers types/applications, instructional and decision-making best practices, risks, policy, resources.
- **INSTRUMENTS:** Policy-consideration frameworks; references state laws.
- **PROHIBITIONS:** None enumerated; emphasis on human oversight.
- **CLASSROOM GUIDANCE:** Goal that all students have a baseline AI understanding within digital literacy as part of a "portrait of the graduate"; discusses social/emotional risks.
- **LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE:** References recent and pending Connecticut AI legislation.
- **HIGHER ED HOOK:** "Portrait of the graduate"/college-and-career framing.

- **INSTRUMENTS:** Version-history table; short- and long-term action framing.
- **PROHIBITIONS:** None enumerated; addresses bias.
- **CLASSROOM GUIDANCE:** SAMR-based; professional learning.
- **LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE:** No enacted statute identified.

### District of Columbia

- **INSTRUMENTS / PROHIBITIONS / CLASSROOM GUIDANCE / LEGAL / HIGHER ED:** Not confirmed.

### Florida
- **FRAMEWORK:** No confirmed FLDOE statewide classroom AI guidance framework identified.
- **INSTRUMENTS / PROHIBITIONS / CLASSROOM GUIDANCE:** State-level not confirmed; activity is legislative and district-level.
- **LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE:** **CS/CS/HB 1503 (and companion SB 1694)** would require AI instruction within CS coursework and responsible AI-application opportunities in general-education courses (pending, 2026).
- **HIGHER ED HOOK:** None specific identified.
- **VERIFICATION FLAG:** FLDOE statewide guidance status unconfirmed; recommend direct FLDOE confirmation.

### Georgia
- **FRAMEWORK:** (1) "Leveraging AI in the K-12 Setting," Georgia DOE, January 2025. (2) **Georgia Professional Standards Commission (GaPSC), "Ethical Considerations in the Appropriate Use of AI for Educators," June 16, 2025** — voluntary guidance built on **seven full-sentence ethical principles** (NOT short labels): (1) responsible/ethical classroom use; (2) modeling/teaching ethical use and mitigating academic-integrity misuse; (3) protecting personally identifiable information; (4) professional practice in evaluating/selecting tools; (5) transparent communication of AI use; (6) understanding and mitigating bias; (7) staying informed given AI's rapid pace.
- **INSTRUMENTS:** **"Traffic Light" system — Red: AI not allowed (academic dishonesty); Yellow: AI allowed for content-creation assistance; Green: AI encouraged with proper citation.** Tool-evaluation Process Rubric with criteria: educational value, data-privacy compliance, usability/accessibility, cost, scalability, vendor reputation, age restrictions. Integration with TrustEd Apps; District Self-check sections.
- **PROHIBITIONS:** High-stakes/non-high-stakes distinction: **prohibits AI for IEP goals, educator evaluations, and subjective grading;** allows AI for lesson-planning assistance, rubric development, and multiple-choice grading.
- **CLASSROOM GUIDANCE:** AI attribution protocols; PD requirements; human oversight.
- **LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE:** Guidance is voluntary; no district-mandate statute. A 2024 task-force report fed the guidance.
- **HIGHER ED HOOK:** None specific identified.

### Hawaii
- **FRAMEWORK:** "AI Guidance for Employees," Hawaii State Department of Education. Covers accountability, fairness/equity, human oversight, assessment/effectiveness, PD, data protection.
- **INSTRUMENTS:** Links to internal support offices.
- **PROHIBITIONS:** **Explicitly cautions against using AI detection tools for plagiarism due to their limitations/inaccuracy** — one of the confirmed states advising against detection tools (with Massachusetts and Washington).
- **CLASSROOM GUIDANCE:** Recommends citing AI-generated content; using AI to transform assessments and personalize instruction.
- **LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE:** **SB 2212** would require all juniors/seniors to complete a six-week AI-literacy course beginning 2027–28 plus a $5M AI Education Grant Pilot; **HB 2466** would direct a statewide K–12 social-media/AI-literacy curriculum; **HB 1676** would create an AI in Education Task Force within the University of Hawaii (all pending).
- **HIGHER ED HOOK:** HB 1676 situates the task force within the University of Hawaii.

### Idaho
- **FRAMEWORK:** Statewide framework **mandated but not yet finalized.** SB 1227 requires the State Department of Education (with State Board approval) to develop a "human-centered," transparent, safe generative-AI framework covering privacy, procurement safeguards, transparency, academic integrity, AI-literacy standards, and PD.
- **INSTRUMENTS / PROHIBITIONS / CLASSROOM GUIDANCE:** To be defined in the forthcoming framework; not yet published.
- **LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE:** **SB 1227 signed by Gov. Brad Little March 26, 2026, effective July 1, 2026** (new Chapter 70, Title 33, Idaho Code). Districts and public charters must adopt local policies. A committee's draft recommendations are under public review/State Board oversight; if approved, classroom implementation could begin as early as 2027–28. A public-private training partnership with Microsoft and Micron was announced at signing.
- **HIGHER ED HOOK:** None specific in the K-12 statute.
- **VERIFICATION FLAG:** The mandated framework's actual contents **have not been published**; only the enabling statute and draft-committee stage are confirmed.

### Illinois
- **FRAMEWORK:** **Illinois State Board of Education (ISBE) AI guidance, released July 9, 2026, reported at 409 pages** (some outlets: ~400). Non-mandatory; districts decide how AI is used, consistent with the 2025 enabling law. The document discloses that ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini assisted in drafting later versions (initial language written without AI; AI used for sourcing links, conciseness, and clarity feedback, human-reviewed).
- **INSTRUMENTS:** Promotes "informed use" — asking not whether AI *can* do a task but "whether its use supports the purposes of schooling." Grade-level-specific examples of teacher AI use, including prompt-writing for lesson plans.
- **PROHIBITIONS:** Walks teachers through ethical problem areas: student data privacy, transparency about AI in family email, cultural bias, and misinformation from AI "hallucinations."
- **CLASSROOM GUIDANCE:** Grade-band examples; evaluation of bias, privacy, transparency, risk.
- **LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE:** **Senate Bill 1920 (2025)** directed ISBE to publish guidance by July 1, 2026 (recommendations, not rules; passed the House 74–34). ISBE plans further guidance and training over the coming year. Also pending: HB 4411 (full year of CS+AI from ninth-graders entering 2028–29) and HB 5113 (temporary commission on AI best practices).
- **HIGHER ED HOOK:** None specific identified in the guidance.
- **VERIFICATION FLAG:** The reported distinction permitting AI to *support but not score* a teacher evaluation is plausible given the document's ethics sections but **was not confirmed against the 409-page body**; verify before asserting.

### Indiana

- **INSTRUMENTS:** None named as reusable scales/rubrics.
- **PROHIBITIONS:** Directs that inputs to public-facing AI tools be free of PII; FERPA/COPPA compliance.
- **CLASSROOM GUIDANCE:** Integrates AI literacy with CS concepts; personalization, time-saving, improved feedback.
- **LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE:** No enacted statute identified.
- **HIGHER ED HOOK:** None specific identified.

### Iowa
- **FRAMEWORK:** No Iowa Department of Education standalone statewide AI classroom guidance confirmed; CRPE reported Iowa defers to districts/local accountability policies.
- **INSTRUMENTS / PROHIBITIONS / CLASSROOM GUIDANCE:** Not confirmed at state level.
- **LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE:** Multiple pending bills — **HSB 610, HF 2540, SF 2094** — would require the graduating class of 2030–31 and beyond to complete a semester of CS/AI coursework and strengthen teacher-prep requirements. The Iowa Department of Education announced a $3 million investment to provide all Iowa elementary schools an AI reading-tutor tool using voice recognition.
- **HIGHER ED HOOK:** None specific identified.

### Kansas
- **FRAMEWORK:** **None.** The Kansas State Department of Education has, per CRPE, "not issued any guidance to districts regarding AI technology and, as of right now, has not discussed any plans to do so."
- **INSTRUMENTS / PROHIBITIONS / CLASSROOM GUIDANCE:** None at state level; district activity exists (Emporia, Goodland USD, Salina USD 305 are AI for Education partners).
- **LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE / HIGHER ED:** None identified.
- **LITIGATION (marquee national case):** **Lawrence Public Schools (USD 497) Gaggle case** — nine current/former Lawrence and Free State High School students filed a federal civil-rights suit August 1, 2025, alleging the AI surveillance tool Gaggle violated First and Fourth Amendment rights (unreasonable searches; censorship of student journalism, art, and mental-health communications). The district bought Gaggle in August 2023 for $162,000 over three years, later switching to ManagedMethods without a board vote. Judge Kathryn H. Vratil denied an ex parte TRO (Aug. 19, 2025) but noted Kansas law protects journalism advisers; found the district violated the Kansas Open Records Act (April 2026); and **ordered the district to pay $113,480 ($112,425 in attorney fees for nearly 250 hours plus $1,055 in costs), per The Lawrence Times (July 27, 2026).** First and Fourth Amendment claims continue against the district; board and administrators dismissed; graduated students' claims dismissed as moot.

### Kentucky
- **FRAMEWORK:** Kentucky Department of Education AI guidance brief, organized around purpose, definition, KETS Master Plan alignment, guiding principles, community engagement, application/research paradigms, post-adoption monitoring.
- **INSTRUMENTS:** "Policy-in-Action Spotlights"; three application paradigms — **AI-directed, AI-supported, and AI-empowered learning.**
- **PROHIBITIONS:** None enumerated; treats AI as one emerging technology among many.
- **CLASSROOM GUIDANCE:** Normalize AI; connect to digital citizenship and CS.
- **LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE:** No enacted statute identified.
- **HIGHER ED HOOK:** None specific identified.

### Louisiana
- **FRAMEWORK:** Louisiana Department of Education (LDOE) AI guidance, Fall 2024. **LDOE Framework for AI Integration** — four-component cyclical approach: Purpose & Research, Policy & Guidance, Engage Stakeholders, Evaluation & Monitoring.
- **INSTRUMENTS:** **Four-tier AI Integration scale aligned to SAMR — AI-Empowered, AI-Enhanced, AI-Assisted, and AI-Prohibited.** Curated tool list with age-band recommendations; glossary.
- **PROHIBITIONS:** "AI-Prohibited" tier; system- and classroom-level safeguards on data storage, software approval, monitoring, human oversight.
- **CLASSROOM GUIDANCE:** Strategies to mitigate plagiarism/cheating; digital-citizenship requirements.
- **LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE:** References Louisiana Student Privacy Law R.S. 17:3914. No district-mandate statute identified.
- **HIGHER ED HOOK:** None specific identified.

### Maine
- **FRAMEWORK:** Interactive "AI Guidance Toolkit," Maine Department of Education, released February 20, 2025; core principle **"KEEP THE HUMAN IN AI."**
- **INSTRUMENTS:** **Five interconnected ethical-integration principles: Teach Critical Thinking, Promote Ethical Practices, Empower Human Agency, Commit to Lifelong Learning, Model Ethical Behavior.** Leadership **"ROOTS" framework: Responsibility, Oversight, Opportunity, Transparency, Sustainability.** Future-Ready Student Competencies framework.
- **PROHIBITIONS:** Explicitly **rejects technology prohibitions** ("technology prohibitions prevent students from learning critical skills…").
- **CLASSROOM GUIDANCE:** Grounds AI in CS, computational thinking, digital citizenship; "AI-resistant" teaching strategies; embeds AI for Education's "Drafting a GenAI Academic Policy."
- **LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE:** Followed a 2024 executive-order task force. No district-mandate statute identified.
- **HIGHER ED HOOK:** None specific identified.

### Maryland
- **FRAMEWORK:** Maryland State Department of Education (MSDE) AI framework organized around **eight critical elements**, with companion resources: a Local Action Planning Guide, a Classroom Companion, separate state- and local-agency guidance, and an **AI Tool Evaluation Rubric.** MSDE previously published a "Responsible Use of Artificial Intelligence Tools" administrative policy (procurement review, data privacy, baseline acceptable use). MSDE reportedly held the framework's public release until it could be aligned with SB 720; exact publication date not stated in available coverage.
- **INSTRUMENTS:** The AI Tool Evaluation Rubric plus Local Action Planning Guide and Classroom Companion are the concrete reusable tools.
- **PROHIBITIONS:** Emphasis (sponsor and MSDE) that AI does not replace the teacher as subject-matter expert.
- **CLASSROOM GUIDANCE:** AI literacy embedded in CS education; procurement guardrails against bias and data-privacy risk.
- **LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE:** **SB 720, "Artificial Intelligence Ready Schools Act," Chapter 634, signed by Gov. Wes Moore May 26, 2026, effective June 1, 2026.** Requires MSDE to develop/annually review AI guidance, publish a rubric and evaluative tools, provide train-the-trainer PD (intent to train educators by July 1, 2027), and staff a statewide **Maryland AI Education Collaborative** (2 years). Every local school system must designate a **non-instructional, central-office AI coordinator** and adopt an aligned policy; AI literacy in state standards by June 1, 2027. Reported district compliance deadline is fall 2026 (a 120-day clock whose exact start is not public). Companion HB 1057.
- **HIGHER ED HOOK:** SB 720 provides $150,000 in FY2027 for MSDE to **partner with an institution of higher education (or third party)** to develop the initial PD training/module; the "university-supported certification of AI tools" appears in the evaluation/certification provisions.
- **VERIFICATION FLAG:** Exact framework publication date and the precise fall-2026 district deadline rely on secondary coverage (Maryland Matters) and should be confirmed with MSDE.

### Massachusetts
- **FRAMEWORK:** "Massachusetts Guidance for Artificial Intelligence in K–12 Education," Department of Elementary and Secondary Education (DESE), released August 2025; developed with DESE's AI Task Force (14 districts) and ISTE+ASCD. **Five core ethical principles: data privacy & security; transparency & accountability; bias awareness & mitigation; human oversight & educator judgment; academic integrity.**
- **INSTRUMENTS:** **Action-oriented planning tables** (three columns: potential action steps; steps taken/self-assessment; plans forward) in each section; District AI Integration Potential Actions Checklists (appendix). Framework addressing three digital divides (access, use, design) from the 2024 National Educational Technology Plan.
- **PROHIBITIONS:** **Explicit guidance against AI detection tools** due to unreliability and punitive effects; recommends trust-based, transparency-focused, process-oriented approaches instead.
- **CLASSROOM GUIDANCE:** Distinguishes teaching ABOUT AI vs. teaching WITH AI; aligns with the Massachusetts DLCS Framework; cross-departmental operational guidance (budgeting, HR, data, procurement).
- **LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE:** Voluntary guidance; no district-mandate statute identified.
- **HIGHER ED HOOK:** None specific identified.

- **HIGHER ED HOOK:** None specific identified.

### Missouri
- **FRAMEWORK:** "AI Guidance for Local Education Agencies," Missouri Department of Elementary and Secondary Education (DESE), Version 1.0 for 2025-26, developed with Missouri's Computer Science Advisory Council. Four core best-practice principles: **Responsible Implementation, Transparency, Rigor, and Curiosity.**
- **INSTRUMENTS:** **Cyclical seven-step policy-development process: Purpose, Compliance, Knowledge, Balance, Integrity, Agency, Evaluation.** Prompt-engineering guidance via AI for Education's **"Five S" model** plus a "Who, What, Why, Where" framework. - **PROHIBITIONS:** Human decision-making must remain central; compliance with FERPA, COPPA, IDEA, CIPA, Section 504, ADA.
- **CLASSROOM GUIDANCE:** Academic-integrity/honor-code recommendations; AI literacy across grade levels; deepfake classroom guide.
- **LEGAL/LEGISLATIVE:** References Missouri Revised Statutes §171.011. **HB 3139** would add AI to the CS definition and require a half-unit beginning 2027–28.
- **HIGHER ED HOOK:** None specific identified.

### Montana
- **FRAMEWORK:** "Montana Artificial Intelligence in K12 Education Guidelines," Office of Public Instruction (OPI) with Montana Digital Academy, October 2025; biannual review cycle. **Five core principles: Data Privacy & Security; Transparency & Accountability; Bias Awareness & Information Verification; Human Oversight & Educator Judgment; Academic Integrity.**

## Cross-Cutting Context

**Federal.** Executive Order 14277, "Advancing Artificial Intelligence Education for American Youth" (signed April 23, 2025), established the White House Task Force on AI Education, chaired by the Director of the Office of Science and Technology Policy and including the Secretaries of Education and Labor; it directs grant prioritization for teacher AI training, encourages AI courses/certifications for high schoolers, and establishes a Presidential AI Challenge. The **U.S. Department of Education final rule** ("Final Priority and Definitions — Advancing Artificial Intelligence in Education," published April 13, 2026, effective May 13, 2026) prioritizes discretionary grants for projects expanding understanding of AI or its appropriate/ethical use, weighting AI-literacy integration; it defines "AI literacy" broadly (technical knowledge, durable skills, civic awareness, ethical reasoning, critical social inquiry, interdisciplinary problem-solving, creativity) and "computer science" to include machine learning and AI.

**2029 PISA / MAIL.** AI literacy will be assessed internationally for the first time in the OECD's 2029 PISA cycle, in an innovative domain the OECD named **"Media and Artificial Intelligence Literacy (MAIL)," led by the Australian Council for Educational Research (ACER);** a first-draft framework was issued January 2026, with results expected December 2031. It will measure how AI systems function, critical evaluation of outputs, recognition of bias/privacy risks, and ethical use.

**Policy-practice gap.** Gallup/Walton Family Foundation, "Teaching for Tomorrow: Closing the Expectations Gap" (2,069 U.S. public K-12 teachers, Feb 9–Mar 2, 2026; released May 27, 2026; ±2.5 pts): **82% received no formal guidance** across tasks ("fewer than one in 10 teachers receiving formal guidance on any specific activity"); **34% received no guidance at all; 48% only informal;** 58% none on AI grading/feedback; 69% none on one-on-one instruction/tutoring; higher-needs schools less likely to receive guidance. (Earlier finding: weekly AI-using teachers save ~5.9 hours/week.)

**Litigation.** The **Lawrence, Kansas Gaggle Fourth Amendment case** (detailed under Kansas) is the marquee K-12 AI-surveillance suit: nine students; district purchased Gaggle for $162,000 (2023); KORA violation found (April 2026); **court-ordered payment of $113,480 in attorney fees/costs (July 27, 2026)**; First/Fourth Amendment claims proceed against the district. Proposed private-right-of-action provisions in South Carolina (H.B. 5253) and West Virginia (HB 5205) signal a coming wave of student-plaintiff enforcement.

**Restriction, not just enablement.** New York's A.9190 (sub-ninth-grade AI ban), NYC's ed-tech purchasing pause and delayed guidance, South Carolina's H.B. 5253 opt-in regime, and California's AB 2148 "natural person" law represent a distinct restrictive/protective countercurrent to the enablement-focused majority.

6. **For the higher-ed audience, foreground the four strongest hooks:** Tennessee Public Chapter 550 (binds universities), Ohio DEW model policy (names postsecondary institutions on the district workgroup), Maryland SB 720 (higher-ed PD partnership + tool certification), and Arizona HB 4040 (would have bound Board of Regents universities but died in committee). Note Virginia's "work in partnership" principle and New Mexico's national-lab partnerships as softer hooks.

**Benchmarks that would change these recommendations:** if Idaho/Maryland/Illinois publish detailed student-use scales, upgrade those entries from "framework mandated" to full instrument detail; if the Lawrence Gaggle case reaches a merits ruling on the Fourth Amendment claim (not just KORA/fees), elevate it from "pending" to precedent; if the ED grant-priority rule survives anticipated litigation over the agency's restructuring, treat federal AI-literacy prioritization as durable rather than contingent.

## Caveats
- **Guidance ≠ law ≠ pending bill.** This dataset distinguishes enacted statutes (with chapter/bill numbers and dates), released guidance (voluntary unless a statute makes it binding), and pending 2026 legislation. Most state "guidance" is explicitly non-binding (e.g., California's disclaims mandatory compliance under Educ. Code §33308.5).
- **Authorship attribution matters.** Several "state" documents are authored by third parties and endorsed/hosted by the agency: Michigan Virtual (endorsed by MDE), Arizona's NAU-based institute, and Tennessee's TSBA model policy. Faculty should weight these accordingly.
- **Recency and versioning.** Many documents are living/versioned (Washington v3.0, West Virginia v1.2, Utah March 2026, Missouri v1.0, Oklahoma v2.0). Dates given are the latest confirmed.
- **Unverified items are labeled in-line** (Wisconsin pillars; DC/PA/FL/TX statewide status; Illinois teacher-evaluation distinction; Maryland exact deadline; New Jersey higher-ed scope; Texas district workflows; Arizona HB 4040 verbatim text). An honest "could not confirm" is used throughout rather than a plausible-sounding assertion.
- **Source hierarchy.** Where only secondary sources were available (some Illinois provisions via Chalkbeat/Capitol News Illinois; the Maryland deadline via Maryland Matters; the Gaggle fee figure via The Lawrence Times), that reliance is flagged in the relevant entry. Primary-source documents (state-agency PDFs, state-legislature/LegiScan bill texts, the Federal Register, Gallup, and the OECD) underlie all other claims.
