Five moves worth stealing

The patterns that recur across state documents, and where they were done best.

PATTERN 01

The scaffolded use scale

Not a course-level yes or no, but a per-assignment level that names exactly how much AI is permitted and what the student must disclose. This is the single most transferable artifact in the entire dataset.

Georgia · New Mexico · Washington · Louisiana · Oklahoma · Wyoming
PATTERN 02

Human oversight as a hard floor

Named prohibitions rather than principles. AI may not set IEP goals, evaluate educators, apply subjective grades, or serve as the primary basis for discipline or placement decisions.

Georgia · Oklahoma · Idaho · South Carolina (proposed)
PATTERN 03

Detection tools rejected

Multiple states advise against AI detectors on grounds of unreliability and punitive effect, and redirect toward disclosure norms and process-oriented assessment instead.

Hawaii · Massachusetts
PATTERN 04

Developmental gating

Access tied to grade band rather than granted uniformly, on the argument that AI literacy depends on content mastery a student has to build first.

Vermont · Rhode Island · North Dakota · New Mexico
PATTERN 05

The opt-out question

A parent may withdraw a student from AI tools without academic penalty. Almost no institution has answered what a student who declines actually receives instead.

Oklahoma · Illinois (proposed) · South Carolina (proposed)
Your incoming class has read a policy on this. Has your syllabus?
  1. Tennessee already covers you. Public Chapter 550 required universities, not only districts, to adopt AI policies. The next state to copy it will do the same.
  2. The scaffolded scale solves the syllabus problem. Faculty keep writing paragraphs where a five-level scale would do. Six states have already built and tested one.
  3. Incoming baselines will diverge sharply. A Vermont student had gated, structured AI exposure from grade three. A Texas student's experience depended entirely on which district they attended.
  4. The governance vocabulary already exists. Tool vetting rubrics, vendor data clauses, AI coordinators, assurance labs. Higher ed is rebuilding all of it from scratch.
  5. Watch South Carolina and New York. One tests the strictest guardrails in the country. The other tests whether a state will set a floor age for classroom AI at all.
